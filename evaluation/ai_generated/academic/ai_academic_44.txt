Title: Investigating the Impact of Climate Change on Arctic Char Population Dynamics in Subarctic Regions: A Longitudinal Study

Abstract

Climate change has profoundly influenced ecosystems worldwide, with significant ramifications for aquatic biota. This study aims to elucidate the effects of climate change on the population dynamics of Arctic char (Salvelinus alpinus) in subarctic regions, focusing particularly on the Kvarken-Tornio waterway, a unique transboundary ecosystem shared by Finland and Sweden.

The research employs a multi-disciplinary approach encompassing ecological, geographical, and climatological perspectives. Longitudinal data collection spanning over two decades (1998-2019) was conducted to monitor changes in water temperature, salinity, and other environmental factors potentially impacting Arctic char populations. Additionally, a comprehensive analysis of the fish's life history traits, such as growth rates, reproductive success, and mortality patterns, was undertaken using tagging and recapture techniques.

Key findings reveal a substantial increase in water temperature during the study period, which positively correlated with changes in Arctic char population dynamics. Specifically, warmer temperatures have led to shifts in distribution ranges, alterations in life history traits (e.g., delayed maturation and altered growth rates), and increased mortality rates, particularly among juvenile populations. Moreover, the study highlights the importance of understanding the interplay between climate change, environmental factors, and life history traits in predicting future population trends of Arctic char in subarctic regions.

The implications of these findings are profound, as they underscore the vulnerability of aquatic species to climate change and emphasize the need for comprehensive management strategies that account for changing environmental conditions. This research contributes significantly to our understanding of the impacts of climate change on freshwater fish populations in subarctic regions and provides valuable insights for conservation efforts aimed at preserving this vital ecosystem. Future research should focus on developing adaptive management strategies tailored to the specific needs of Arctic char populations as they continue to face the challenges posed by a changing climate.
