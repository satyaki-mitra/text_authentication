Title: Exploring the Impact of Cognitive Behavioral Therapy on Anxiety Disorders in Adolescents: A Randomized Controlled Trial

Abstract

Background Context: Anxiety disorders, prevalent among adolescents, pose significant challenges to their psychosocial development and academic performance. Given the burgeoning incidence of these disorders, there is an urgent need for effective interventions that cater specifically to this age group. Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT), a well-established evidence-based treatment, has shown promising results in managing anxiety disorders in adults and children. However, the efficacy of CBT for adolescents remains under-researched.

Research Methodology: In this randomized controlled trial, 150 adolescents (aged 13-18 years) diagnosed with various anxiety disorders were recruited from secondary schools and community mental health clinics. Participants were randomly assigned to either the CBT group or a waitlist control group. The CBT group received 16 weekly sessions of individual therapy, while the control group continued with their usual care. Anxiety levels were assessed using standardized self-report measures (i.e., the State-Trait Anxiety Inventory and the Multidimensional Anxiety Scale for Children) at baseline, post-intervention, and follow-up (3 months post-intervention).

Key Findings: Results revealed significant reductions in anxiety levels for the CBT group compared to the control group both immediately post-intervention and at follow-up. Specifically, participants in the CBT group demonstrated a 48% reduction in anxiety scores compared to a 15% increase in the control group (p<0.001). Furthermore, significant improvements were observed in participants' self-reported symptoms of depression, sleep disturbances, and school absenteeism. The findings suggest that CBT is an efficacious intervention for reducing anxiety symptoms in adolescents with anxiety disorders.

Implications: This study provides robust evidence supporting the use of CBT as a viable treatment option for adolescents suffering from anxiety disorders. Given the high prevalence and debilitating consequences of these disorders, the findings underscore the need for widespread implementation of CBT programs in schools and community mental health settings. Additionally, the results highlight the importance of early intervention to mitigate the long-term impacts of anxiety disorders on adolescents' psychosocial development and academic achievement. Future research should focus on examining the effectiveness of CBT as a preventative measure for anxiety disorders in at-risk populations.
