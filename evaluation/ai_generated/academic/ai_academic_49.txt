Title: Investigating the Impact of Cognitive Behavioral Therapy on Anxiety Disorders in Adolescents: A Randomized Controlled Trial

Abstract

Background Context: Anxiety disorders are prevalent among adolescents, significantly impairing their academic, social, and emotional well-being. Despite the availability of various treatment modalities, there is a need for effective, evidence-based interventions tailored to this age group. This study aimed to evaluate the efficacy of Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT) in reducing symptoms of anxiety disorders among adolescents.

Research Methodology: A randomized controlled trial was conducted with 150 adolescent participants aged 12-18 years, diagnosed with an anxiety disorder according to the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders (DSM-5). Participants were randomly assigned to either a CBT group or a waitlist control group. The CBT group received 16 weekly sessions of individual therapy delivered by licensed therapists trained in CBT. Outcome measures included the Anxiety Disorders Interview Schedule for DSM-5 (ADIS-5) and the State-Trait Anxiety Inventory for Children (STAIC). Data were analyzed using intention-to-treat principles, with repeated measures analysis of variance (ANOVA) to assess within- and between-group changes.

Key Findings: At post-treatment, the CBT group showed significant reductions in anxiety symptomatology compared to the waitlist control group (F(1, 148) = 23.76, p < .001). The effect size was large (d = 1.23), indicating a substantial improvement in anxiety symptoms for the CBT group. Follow-up assessments at three and six months post-treatment demonstrated sustained improvements in the CBT group, with no significant changes observed in the waitlist control group.

Implications: The findings of this study provide robust evidence supporting the efficacy of CBT as an effective intervention for reducing symptoms of anxiety disorders among adolescents. Given the large effect size and sustained improvements, CBT may represent a valuable addition to existing treatment options for this population. Further research is warranted to explore the potential benefits of CBT in various clinical settings and to investigate potential mechanisms underlying its therapeutic effects. Additionally, efforts should be made to disseminate CBT to practitioners working with adolescents to ensure that this evidence-based intervention is more widely available to those who need it most.
