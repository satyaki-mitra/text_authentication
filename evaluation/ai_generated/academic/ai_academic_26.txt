Title: Investigating the Impact of Exogenous Amino Acids on Muscle Protein Synthesis in Elderly Individuals: A Randomized Controlled Trial

Abstract

Background Context: Sarcopenia, a progressive and generalized loss of skeletal muscle mass, is a prevalent issue among the aging population, significantly impacting physical function, mobility, and overall quality of life. Enhancing muscle protein synthesis (MPS) has been proposed as a potential strategy to combat sarcopenia. This study aimed to explore the effect of exogenous amino acid supplementation on MPS in elderly individuals.

Research Methodology: A double-blind, randomized controlled trial was conducted with 80 healthy older adults (65-75 years old) who were randomly assigned to either a treatment group receiving an amino acid supplement (containing leucine, isoleucine, valine, and essential amino acids) or a placebo group. The study spanned over a period of 12 weeks, with participants consuming the allocated supplement daily. Muscle biopsies were taken at baseline, week 4, and week 12 to assess MPS using stable isotope-labeled leucine tracer methodology. Physical function was evaluated using timed up-and-go test (TUG) and handgrip dynamometry.

Key Findings: The results demonstrated a significant increase in muscle protein synthesis rates in the treatment group compared to the placebo group at weeks 4 and 12 (p<0.05). Furthermore, the treatment group showed improvements in physical function as indicated by reduced TUG times (p<0.05) and increased handgrip strength (p<0.05) compared to the placebo group. No adverse effects were reported during the study period.

Implications: The findings suggest that exogenous amino acid supplementation could be a promising strategy for promoting muscle protein synthesis in older adults, potentially delaying or mitigating sarcopenia-related declines in physical function. Further research is warranted to investigate long-term effects and optimize dosage regimens for various age groups and populations with different health statuses. These results contribute to the growing body of evidence supporting the potential role of nutritional interventions in combating age-related muscle loss and maintaining functional independence among older adults.
