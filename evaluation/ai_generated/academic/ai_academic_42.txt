Title: Investigating the Impact of Climate Change on Arctic Sea Ice Thickness: A Multi-Temporal Analysis Using Envisat ASAR Data

Abstract

Background Context: The rapid melting of Arctic sea ice, a consequence of climate change, poses significant ecological, economic, and geopolitical implications. This study aims to examine the temporal evolution of Arctic sea ice thickness (SIT) using advanced Synthetic Aperture Radar (ASAR) data from the European Space Agency's Envisat satellite.

Research Methodology: The research employed a multi-temporal analysis approach, utilizing Envisat ASAR data spanning from 2003 to 2012, across various regions of the Arctic Ocean. The study applied the ESA's Polarized Ascending/Descending Interferometric SAR (PAInSAR) technique for interferometric processing and SIT estimation. Statistical analysis was performed using ANOVA tests to compare SIT variations among different years, seasons, and regions.

Key Findings: The results revealed a significant decline in Arctic sea ice thickness over the study period, with an average reduction of 34% across all analyzed regions. Seasonal analyses indicated that summer ice was more susceptible to thinning compared to winter ice, with summer SIT reducing by approximately 50%. Moreover, the study identified regional variations in SIT changes, with the Barents Sea and Kara Sea exhibiting the most pronounced thinning rates.

Implications: This research contributes to the growing body of evidence highlighting the accelerating loss of Arctic sea ice due to climate change. The findings underscore the urgency for policymakers to address this critical issue, as reduced SIT could exacerbate global warming through increased albedo feedback and enhanced heat transfer between the ocean and atmosphere. Additionally, the study's methodology offers a valuable tool for monitoring Arctic sea ice dynamics, providing insights into potential future scenarios and informing climate change mitigation strategies.
