Title: Investigating the Impact of Dietary Diversity on Cognitive Function in Older Adults: A Cross-Sectional Study

Abstract

Background Context: The global aging population is growing at an unprecedented rate, with a concomitant increase in age-related cognitive decline and associated disorders such as dementia. As the search for preventive measures intensifies, diet has emerged as a potential modifiable factor that could influence cognitive health. This study aimed to examine the relationship between dietary diversity and cognitive function among older adults.

Research Methodology: A cross-sectional design was employed in this study. Data were collected from 1000 community-dwelling older adults aged 60 years and above, residing in urban and rural areas of a mid-sized city in the United States. Dietary intake was assessed using a validated food frequency questionnaire, while cognitive function was measured using the Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MoCA). Linear regression analyses were conducted to examine the association between dietary diversity and cognitive scores, adjusting for potential confounders such as age, sex, education level, physical activity, and comorbidities.

Key Findings: The results indicated a significant positive association between dietary diversity and cognitive function (β = 0.25, p < 0.001). Specifically, higher dietary diversity scores were associated with better performance in executive functions, memory, attention, and language domains of the MoCA test. Moreover, the findings showed that older adults consuming a more diverse diet had lower odds of having cognitive impairment (OR = 0.68, p < 0.01).

Implications: These findings provide evidence supporting the role of dietary diversity in maintaining cognitive health among older adults. The results underscore the importance of promoting a diverse dietary pattern as a potential strategy for preventing age-related cognitive decline and dementia. Future research should focus on longitudinal studies to further establish the causal relationship between diet, dietary diversity, and cognitive function. Additionally, interventions aimed at improving dietary diversity among older adults are warranted, with the goal of enhancing cognitive health and quality of life in this vulnerable population.
