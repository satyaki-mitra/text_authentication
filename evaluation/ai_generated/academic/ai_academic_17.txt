Title: Examining the Impact of Cognitive Behavioral Therapy on Depression Symptoms in Adolescents: A Randomized Controlled Trial

Abstract

Background Context: Depression is a significant public health concern, particularly among adolescents, with its prevalence escalating globally. The impact of depression during adolescence can be devastating, affecting academic performance, social relationships, and long-term mental health outcomes. Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT), an evidence-based psychotherapeutic intervention, has shown promise in addressing depression symptoms among adults; however, its efficacy in treating adolescent depression remains under-researched.

Research Methodology: This study employed a randomized controlled trial design to investigate the effectiveness of CBT in reducing depressive symptoms among adolescents aged 13-18 years. A total of 200 participants were randomly assigned to either a CBT intervention group or a waitlist control group. The CBT intervention consisted of 16 weekly sessions, each lasting 50 minutes, delivered by licensed psychotherapists trained in CBT. Assessments were conducted at baseline, post-intervention, and three-month follow-up using standardized measures such as the Children's Depression Inventory (CDI) and the Beck Depression Inventory-II (BDI-II).

Key Findings: Results revealed significant reductions in depressive symptoms for the CBT intervention group compared to the control group at post-intervention and three-month follow-up. Specifically, the CBT group showed a mean decrease of 14.2 points on the CDI and 13.5 points on the BDI-II, while the control group demonstrated minimal change. Effect size calculations indicated large effects for both measures (Cohen's d > 1.0). Additionally, findings suggested that improvement in depressive symptoms was associated with greater adherence to the CBT intervention.

Implications: The results of this study provide robust evidence supporting the effectiveness of CBT in reducing depressive symptoms among adolescents. These findings underscore the importance of integrating CBT into mental health service delivery models for adolescents experiencing depression. Future research should focus on implementing CBT in real-world settings, examining its long-term efficacy, and investigating potential moderators and mediators of treatment response to optimize CBT outcomes for this population.
