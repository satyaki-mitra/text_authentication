Title: Investigating the Effects of Cognitive Behavioral Therapy on Anxiety Disorders in Adolescents: A Randomized Controlled Trial

Abstract

Background Context:
Anxiety disorders, a significant public health concern, are prevalent among adolescents worldwide. These disorders impose substantial emotional distress and functional impairment, often persisting into adulthood if left untreated. Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT), an evidence-based psychotherapeutic approach, has demonstrated efficacy in managing anxiety disorders in adults. However, its application in adolescent populations remains understudied. This study aimed to investigate the effectiveness of CBT for anxiety disorders in adolescents compared to a waitlist control group.

Research Methodology:
Participants included 120 adolescents (M age = 15.3 years, SD = 1.8; 60% female) diagnosed with an anxiety disorder (Generalized Anxiety Disorder, Social Anxiety Disorder, Panic Disorder, or Separation Anxiety Disorder). Participants were randomly assigned to either a CBT group or a waitlist control group. The CBT group received 16 weekly sessions of individual therapy delivered by licensed therapists trained in CBT. Outcome measures included the State-Trait Anxiety Inventory (STAI), Children's Global Assessment Scale (CGAS), and Clinical Global Impressions-Severity scale (CGI-S). Pre-treatment, post-treatment, and 3-month follow-up assessments were conducted.

Key Findings:
Significant improvements were observed in the CBT group compared to the waitlist control group on all outcome measures at post-treatment (p < .001) and 3-month follow-up (p < .01). Specifically, the CBT group showed a mean reduction of 9.5 points on the STAI total score, a 2.5-point increase on the CGAS, and a mean decrease of 1.5 points on the CGI-S at post-treatment. These improvements were maintained at the 3-month follow-up.

Implications:
The findings support the effectiveness of CBT in treating anxiety disorders in adolescents, replicating previous research with adult populations. The study provides empirical evidence for the use of CBT as a first-line treatment for adolescent anxiety disorders. Future research should focus on optimizing CBT delivery methods and exploring its efficacy in comorbid conditions to further enhance its utility in clinical practice. Additionally, long-term follow-up studies are warranted to examine the durability of these improvements and potential relapse prevention
