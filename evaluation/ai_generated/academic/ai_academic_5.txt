Title: An Examination of the Impact of Digital Transformation on Knowledge Management Systems within Organizations: A Comparative Study

Abstract

In the contemporary business landscape, the digital transformation (DT) revolution has brought about a paradigm shift, reshaping various organizational functions, including knowledge management systems (KMS). This study endeavors to delve into the intricate relationship between DT and KMS within organizations. A comparative approach is adopted, analyzing two distinct case studies: a traditional manufacturing company undergoing digital transformation and a tech-native startup that has already embraced digital transformation fully.

The research employs a mixed-methods design, incorporating both qualitative and quantitative approaches to gather comprehensive insights. The qualitative phase comprises semi-structured interviews with key stakeholders from both organizations, enabling in-depth exploration of the subjective experiences and perceptions regarding DT's influence on KMS. Quantitative data is gathered through surveys distributed among employees in both case study organizations, aimed at assessing the objective changes in KMS post-digital transformation.

The findings indicate that digital transformation significantly impacts knowledge management systems, primarily through increased accessibility, enhanced collaboration, and improved knowledge sharing mechanisms. In the traditional manufacturing company, DT has led to a more efficient and streamlined KMS, fostering a culture of continuous learning and innovation. Conversely, in the tech-native startup, DT has been instrumental in shaping the very foundation of their KMS, enabling agility, adaptability, and rapid response to market changes.

However, the study also reveals challenges associated with digital transformation, such as data privacy concerns, resistance to change, and the need for robust IT infrastructure. To mitigate these challenges, strategic recommendations are proposed, including the implementation of comprehensive digital transformation strategies, investment in employee training and development programs, and the adoption of best practices for knowledge management in a digital age.

This research adds valuable insights to the existing body of knowledge on digital transformation and knowledge management systems, offering practical implications for both academia and industry. The comparative analysis between traditional and tech-native organizations provides a nuanced understanding of how DT influences KMS and offers potential avenues for future research in this burgeoning field.
