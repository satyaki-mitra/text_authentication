Title: Investigating the Effects of Cognitive Behavioral Therapy on Depression Symptoms in Adolescents: A Randomized Controlled Trial

Abstract

Background Context: Depression is a prevalent mental health disorder among adolescents, significantly impacting their academic, social, and emotional well-being. Traditional pharmacological interventions may have limited efficacy and potential side effects in this age group, necessitating the exploration of alternative therapeutic approaches. Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT), a psychosocial intervention that focuses on modifying dysfunctional emotions, behaviors, and thoughts, has shown promise in managing depression symptoms in various populations, including adolescents. However, empirical evidence supporting its efficacy in this specific age group is still limited and warrants further investigation.

Research Methodology: This study employed a randomized controlled design to examine the effects of CBT on depression symptoms in a sample of 150 adolescents aged 13-18 years, diagnosed with major depressive disorder (MDD). Participants were randomly assigned either to a CBT group or a waitlist control group. The CBT intervention consisted of 16 weekly sessions, each lasting 50 minutes, delivered by trained therapists following the manualized protocol developed by Beck and colleagues (Beck, Rush, Shaw, & Emery, 1979). Assessments were conducted at baseline, post-intervention, and three-month follow-up using standardized measures of depression symptoms, including the Children's Depression Rating Scale-Revised (CDRS-R; Poznanski & Mokros, 1980) and the Beck Depression Inventory-II (BDI-II; Beck, Steer, & Brown, 1996).

Key Findings: The results indicated significant reductions in depression symptoms in the CBT group compared to the control group at post-intervention and follow-up. Specifically, the CBT group demonstrated a mean reduction of 20.5 points on the CDRS-R at post-intervention (t(74) = -13.69, p< .001), which was maintained at three-month follow-up (t(74) = -8.96, p< .001).
