Title: An Examination of the Impact of Digital Transformation on Small and Medium Enterprises (SMEs) in Developing Economies: A Case Study of Kenya

Abstract

This study delves into an exploration of the transformative effects of digitalization on Small and Medium Enterprises (SMEs) within the Kenyan economy, a developing nation. The research is predicated on the growing significance of digital transformation as a catalyst for economic development and competitiveness in the contemporary global business landscape. In this context, the study seeks to address two primary research questions: (1) What are the key drivers propelling the digitization of SMEs in Kenya? (2) How does the digitization of SMEs impact their operational efficiency, productivity, and overall economic contribution?

The study employs a mixed-methods approach that combines quantitative and qualitative data collection techniques. A stratified random sampling method was utilized to select 300 SMEs across various industries in Kenya for the survey component of the research. Additionally, semi-structured interviews with key informants from industry associations, government regulatory bodies, and selected SMEs were conducted to provide rich insights into the subject matter.

Preliminary findings suggest that factors such as government incentives, increased internet penetration, and the need for competitiveness drive the digitization of SMEs in Kenya. In terms of impact, the study finds that the digital transformation of SMEs enhances operational efficiency through automation, improves productivity by facilitating real-time decision making, and contributes to economic growth by expanding market reach and fostering innovation. However, challenges such as inadequate digital skills, lack of access to affordable digital tools, and cybersecurity threats persist, necessitating targeted interventions from policy makers, industry stakeholders, and development agencies.

This study provides valuable insights for policymakers, entrepreneurs, academics, and development agencies seeking to understand the dynamics of digital transformation in SMEs within developing economies. The findings underscore the need for concerted efforts to address the challenges hindering the full potential of digitization while leveraging its benefits to spur economic growth and competitiveness in Kenya and similar contexts. Future research should explore long-term impacts, best practices, and policy recommendations for effective digital transformation strategies in SMEs in developing economies.
