Title: Investigating the Effects of Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT) on Anxiety Disorders in Adolescents: A Randomized Controlled Trial

Abstract

Background Context: Anxiety disorders represent a significant public health concern, particularly among adolescents, due to their prevalence, chronicity, and potential for impairment across multiple domains. Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT) has emerged as an effective evidence-based treatment for anxiety disorders in adults; however, its efficacy in adolescent populations remains under-investigated.

Research Methodology: In this study, a randomized controlled trial design was employed to evaluate the impact of CBT on anxiety symptoms in adolescents (N=150) aged 13-18 years, diagnosed with one or more anxiety disorders according to Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders (DSM-5). Participants were randomly assigned to either a CBT intervention group (n=75) or a waitlist control group (n=75). The CBT intervention consisted of 16 weekly sessions, each lasting 60 minutes, delivered by trained therapists. Outcome measures included the Spence Children's Anxiety Scale (SCAS), the State-Trait Anxiety Inventory for Children (STAIC), and the Youth Outcomes Questionnaire (Y-OQ). Assessments were conducted at baseline, post-treatment, and 3-month follow-up.

Key Findings: Results revealed significant reductions in anxiety symptoms for the CBT intervention group compared to the control group on all outcome measures at post-treatment and follow-up (p<0.001). Effect sizes ranged from moderate to large, with Cohen's d values exceeding 0.80. Furthermore, mediational analyses suggested that changes in cognitive appraisals and maladaptive behaviors mediated the effects of CBT on anxiety symptoms.

Implications: The findings provide strong evidence for the efficacy of CBT in reducing anxiety symptoms in adolescents with anxiety disorders. These results underscore the importance of integrating CBT into routine clinical practice for this population. Additionally, the identification of cognitive and behavioral mechanisms underlying treatment effects may inform the development of more targeted and efficient interventions. Future research should focus on examining the long-term maintenance of treatment gains and exploring potential moderators and mediators of treatment response in diverse samples.
