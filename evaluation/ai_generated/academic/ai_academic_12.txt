Title: An Examination of the Impact of Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT) on Depression Symptoms in Adolescents: A Randomized Controlled Trial

Abstract

Background Context: Adolescence is a critical developmental stage characterized by significant emotional and cognitive changes. The prevalence of depressive disorders among adolescents has been escalating, posing a substantial public health concern worldwide. Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT), an empirically supported psychotherapeutic intervention, has demonstrated efficacy in reducing depression symptoms in adults. However, the application and effectiveness of CBT for treating adolescent depression remain under-explored.

Research Methodology: This study conducted a randomized controlled trial with 150 adolescents aged 13-18 years diagnosed with major depressive disorder (MDD) according to DSM-5 criteria. Participants were randomly assigned to either the CBT intervention group or the waiting list control group. The CBT intervention consisted of 16 weekly sessions, each lasting 50 minutes, delivered by licensed psychotherapists trained in CBT. The primary outcome measure was the Hamilton Depression Rating Scale (HAM-D), administered at baseline, post-intervention, and three months follow-up.

Key Findings: Results revealed significant reductions in depression symptoms post-intervention for the CBT group compared to the control group (t = -10.75, p < 0.001). The effect size was large (Cohen's d = 1.23), indicating substantial improvement in depressive symptomatology following CBT intervention. At three months follow-up, the CBT group maintained statistically significant and clinically meaningful improvements compared to the control group (t = -7.56, p < 0.001).

Implications: The findings provide robust evidence supporting the efficacy of CBT in reducing depression symptoms among adolescents with MDD. The large effect size indicates that CBT may be a promising intervention for addressing the rising public health concern of adolescent depression. Future research should explore the implementation of CBT as a standard treatment for adolescent depression within clinical settings, as well as examine potential moderators and mediators of treatment efficacy to optimize outcomes. Additionally, investigating the long-term effects of CBT on adolescent depression is essential to establish its durability in promoting mental health and wellbeing among this vulnerable population.
