Title: Investigating the Impact of Cognitive Behavioral Therapy on Depression Symptoms in Adolescents: A Randomized Controlled Trial

Abstract

Background Context: Depression is a prevalent mental health disorder among adolescents, with significant implications for their emotional wellbeing, academic performance, and future life prospects. Traditional pharmacological interventions for depression in this age group have been met with limited success and potential side effects, necessitating the exploration of alternative treatment modalities. This study aims to evaluate the efficacy of Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT) as a viable non-pharmacological approach for managing depression symptoms in adolescents.

Research Methodology: A randomized controlled trial was conducted involving 250 adolescents aged 13-18 years, diagnosed with major depressive disorder according to the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders (DSM-5). Participants were randomly assigned either to a CBT intervention group or a waitlist control group. The CBT group received 16 weekly sessions, each lasting an hour, delivered by licensed psychologists experienced in adolescent mental health. Depression symptoms were assessed using the Hamilton Rating Scale for Depression (HRSD) at baseline, post-intervention, and follow-up assessments at 3 and 6 months.

Key Findings: At post-intervention, participants in the CBT group demonstrated a statistically significant reduction in depression symptoms compared to the control group (t(248)=10.79, p<0.001). This effect was maintained at both follow-up assessments, with the CBT group continuing to show lower HRSD scores than the control group (F(2, 246)=57.32, p<0.001). The findings suggest that CBT is an effective intervention for reducing depression symptoms in adolescents and may serve as a valuable alternative or adjunct to pharmacological treatments.

Implications: These results underscore the potential of CBT as a promising non-pharmacological approach for treating depression in adolescents. By addressing maladaptive thought patterns and behaviors, CBT may offer a more targeted and personalized approach compared to traditional pharmacological interventions. Furthermore, the maintenance of symptom reduction at follow-up assessments indicates that CBT may have lasting effects on adolescent mental health. Future research should focus on optimizing CBT delivery methods for maximum efficacy and exploring its potential use in combination with pharmacological treatments.
