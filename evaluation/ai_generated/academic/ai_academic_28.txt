Title: An Examination of the Effects of Cognitive Behavioral Therapy on Depression Symptoms in Adolescents: A Randomized Controlled Trial

Abstract

Background Context: Adolescence is a critical developmental period, characterized by significant physiological, psychological, and social changes. The prevalence of depression among adolescents has been steadily increasing worldwide, with profound implications for their future well-being and productivity. Given the deleterious impact of untreated depression on this age group, there is an urgent need to identify effective interventions that can mitigate its burdensome effects. This study aimed to evaluate the efficacy of Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT) in reducing symptoms of depression among adolescents.

Research Methodology: A randomized controlled trial design was employed, involving 150 adolescents aged 13-18 years who met diagnostic criteria for Major Depressive Disorder (MDD). Participants were randomly assigned to either a CBT intervention group or a waiting-list control group. The CBT intervention consisted of 16 weekly sessions, each lasting 60 minutes, delivered by trained therapists following established protocols. The primary outcome measure was the Beck Depression Inventory-II (BDI-II), and secondary outcomes included the Child Depression Inventory (CDI) and the Youth Outcome Questionnaire (Y-OQ). Assessments were conducted at baseline, post-intervention, and three months follow-up.

Key Findings: Significant reductions in depression symptoms were observed in the CBT intervention group compared to the control group at post-intervention and follow-up assessments (p<0.001). The effect size for the primary outcome measure at post-intervention was d=1.2, indicating large effects. Furthermore, improvements in depression symptoms were associated with reductions in negative automatic thoughts, as well as enhanced problem-solving skills and coping strategies. No significant adverse events were reported during or after the intervention.

Implications: The findings provide strong evidence for the effectiveness of CBT in reducing depression symptoms among adolescents. Given its demonstrated efficacy, scalability, and cost-effectiveness, CBT represents a promising intervention that can be readily implemented in clinical settings to address the rising burden of depression in this age group. Future research should focus on optimizing the delivery of CBT through technology-based platforms, as well as exploring its potential benefits for other mental health disorders prevalent among adolescents.
