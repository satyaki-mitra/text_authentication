Title: Assessing the Impact of Environmental Enrichment on Cognitive Development in Juvenile Rhesus Macaques (Macaca mulatta)

Abstract

Background Context: The cognitive development of non-human primates, particularly juvenile rhesus macaques (Macaca mulatta), has been a subject of extensive research due to their phylogenetic proximity to humans and shared cognitive capacities. However, the impact of environmental enrichment on their cognitive development remains under-explored. This study aimed to investigate the effects of environmental enrichment on the cognitive performance of juvenile rhesus macaques.

Research Methodology: A total of 48 juvenile rhesus macaques were randomly assigned to either an Enriched Environment Group (EEG, n=24) or a Standard Environment Group (SEG, n=24). The EEG received enrichment items such as mirrors, puzzle feeders, and social groups, while the SEG was housed in standard barren cages. Cognitive performance was assessed using object permanence and problem-solving tasks. Data were analyzed using a mixed-effects model with environment group as a fixed effect and individual macaque as a random effect.

Key Findings: Results demonstrated significant improvements in cognitive performance among juvenile rhesus macaques housed in enriched environments compared to those in standard environments (F(1, 46) = 23.79, p<0.001). Specifically, the EEG showed a higher success rate in object permanence tasks and problem-solving tasks, indicating enhanced memory retention and problem-solving skills.

Implications: These findings provide compelling evidence for the positive influence of environmental enrichment on cognitive development in juvenile rhesus macaques. The study underscores the importance of providing cognitively stimulating environments to non-human primates in captivity, as it may potentially improve their welfare and contribute to our understanding of human cognitive development. Future research should focus on long-term effects, varying degrees of environmental enrichment, and comparisons with other species to further elucidate the role of environmental factors in cognitive development.
