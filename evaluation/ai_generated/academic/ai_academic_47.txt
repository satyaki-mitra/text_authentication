Title: Investigating the Impact of Climate Change on Arctic Sea Ice Thickness: A Multi-Year Analysis Using Satellite Data

Abstract

Climate change, a pressing global concern, has been observed to significantly alter various ecological and geophysical systems. One such system profoundly affected is the Arctic sea ice, which plays a pivotal role in regulating Earth's climate by reflecting solar radiation and influencing oceanic currents. This study aims to examine the long-term trend of Arctic sea ice thickness (SIT) changes in response to climate change, utilizing a comprehensive dataset from satellite remote sensing.

The research methodology employed a time series analysis of SIT data collected by the Advanced Microwave Scanning Radiometer – Earth Observing System (AMSR-E) and its successor, AMSR2, from 2003 to 2019. The data was processed using a novel algorithm that combined passive microwave brightness temperatures with sea surface salinity to generate accurate SIT estimates. To account for potential regional variations, the Arctic Ocean was divided into sectors based on geographical and ice dynamics characteristics.

Key findings revealed a significant decline in Arctic sea ice thickness over the study period, with an average annual loss of 1.37 cm per year. The rate of SIT reduction was found to be more pronounced during summer months, indicating a shift towards thinner, seasonal ice cover. Furthermore, the study demonstrated that the western sector of the Arctic Ocean experienced the most substantial decline in SIT, followed by the central and eastern sectors.

The implications of these findings are far-reaching. Thinning Arctic sea ice will likely accelerate the melting process during summer months, exacerbating climate change feedback loops such as increased albedo loss and enhanced oceanic heat exchange with the atmosphere. Moreover, the decline in SIT may have profound consequences for local ecosystems, indigenous communities, and global shipping routes.

In conclusion, this study provides robust evidence of a long-term decline in Arctic sea ice thickness due to climate change. The findings underscore the urgent need for continued monitoring, improved understanding of complex feedback mechanisms, and the development of effective mitigation strategies to combat the deleterious effects of climate change on Arctic sea ice. Future research should focus on refining SIT estimation techniques, exploring potential regional drivers of change, and assessing the socio-economic impacts of a warming Arctic.
