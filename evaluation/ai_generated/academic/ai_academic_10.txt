Title: Investigating the Impact of Cognitive Behavioral Therapy on Depressive Symptoms in Adolescents: A Randomized Controlled Trial

Abstract

Background Context: Adolescence is a critical developmental stage, characterized by significant emotional and psychological changes. The prevalence of depressive symptoms among adolescents has been escalating worldwide, posing a substantial public health concern. Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT), an evidence-based psychotherapeutic approach, has demonstrated efficacy in addressing depression across various age groups. However, its utility in treating depressive symptoms among adolescents remains under-researched and warrants further investigation.

Research Methodology: This study employed a randomized controlled trial design to evaluate the effect of CBT on depressive symptoms in adolescents aged 13-18 years. Participants were randomly assigned either to a CBT intervention group or a waitlist control group. The CBT group received 12 weekly sessions, while the control group was placed on a waiting list and subsequently provided with CBT after the completion of the study. The primary outcome measure was the Beck Depression Inventory-II (BDI-II), administered pre-intervention, post-intervention, and at a three-month follow-up.

Key Findings: Results revealed significant reductions in depressive symptoms among adolescents who underwent CBT compared to those in the waitlist control group. At post-intervention, the mean BDI-II score for the CBT group was 12.5 (SD=3.7), as opposed to 18.4 (SD=5.9) in the control group (t(60)=14.2, p<0.001). These improvements were sustained at the three-month follow-up, with mean BDI-II scores of 13.2 (SD=4.2) and 18.7 (SD=5.9), respectively (t(60)=12.6, p<0.001).

Implications: The findings underscore the effectiveness of CBT in reducing depressive symptoms among adolescents. Given the escalating prevalence of depression in this age group and the high demand for evidence-based interventions, these results have significant implications for clinical practice. Furthermore, the study provides empirical support for the integration of CBT into mental health services for adolescents. Future research should focus on optimizing CBT delivery methods to enhance accessibility and efficacy in diverse populations.
