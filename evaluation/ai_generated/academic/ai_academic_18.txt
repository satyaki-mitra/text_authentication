Title: Investigating the Impact of Exogenous Phytochemical Supplementation on Cognitive Function in Older Adults: A Randomized Controlled Trial

Abstract

Background Context: The global population is experiencing an unprecedented aging, with a concomitant increase in age-related cognitive decline and neurodegenerative diseases. Given the limited efficacy of current pharmacological interventions for these conditions, there is an urgent need to explore alternative strategies that can mitigate cognitive impairment. This study aimed to examine the effect of exogenous phytochemical supplementation on cognitive function in older adults.

Research Methodology: A double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled trial was conducted with 200 community-dwelling older adults (aged 65 and above) who were cognitively intact but demonstrated mild cognitive impairment on a battery of neuropsychological tests. Participants were randomly assigned to receive either a daily phytochemical supplement containing standardized extracts from Ginkgo biloba, Curcuma longa, and Salvia officinalis (n = 100), or an identical-looking placebo (n = 100) for a period of six months. Cognitive function was assessed at baseline and post-intervention using the Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MoCA), Trail Making Test, and Stroop Color-Word Interference Test.

Key Findings: Results revealed significant improvements in global cognitive functioning (F(1, 196) = 20.34, p < .001), executive function (F(1, 196) = 15.78, p < .001), and processing speed (F(1, 196) = 13.14, p < .001) in the phytochemical supplement group compared to the placebo group. No significant differences were observed between groups with regard to adverse events or changes in blood pressure, glucose levels, or body mass index.

Implications: The findings suggest that exogenous phytochemical supplementation may have a beneficial effect on cognitive function in older adults with mild cognitive impairment. If replicated in larger and more diverse samples, these results could have significant implications for the development of novel, non-pharmacological interventions to delay or prevent age-related cognitive decline and neurodegenerative diseases. Future research should focus on identifying optimal doses, combinations, and duration of treatment as well as exploring potential mechanisms underlying the observed cognitive benefits.
