Title: Investigating the Impact of Plant-Based Diets on Cardiovascular Health: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

Abstract

Background Context: Cardiovascular disease (CVD) remains a leading cause of global morbidity and mortality, necessitating the exploration of effective preventive strategies. Accumulating evidence suggests that plant-based diets (PBDs), characterized by high consumption of fruits, vegetables, whole grains, legumes, nuts, and seeds, may offer significant cardiovascular benefits. This systematic review and meta-analysis aimed to synthesize the existing literature on the association between PBDs and CVD risk.

Research Methodology: A comprehensive search was conducted in five databases (PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science, Cochrane Library, and Google Scholar) from their inception until March 2021, using predefined keywords and MeSH terms. Inclusion criteria were observational studies (cohort, case-control, or cross-sectional) examining the relationship between PBDs and CVD risk factors or outcomes. Two reviewers independently screened titles and abstracts, assessed eligibility, and extracted data using a standardized form. Risk of bias was evaluated using the Newcastle-Ottawa Scale. Random-effects models were employed for meta-analyses, with inverse-variance weighting.

Key Findings: A total of 52 studies (n=1,308,947 participants) met the inclusion criteria.
