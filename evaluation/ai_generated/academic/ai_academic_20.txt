Title: An Examination of the Effects of Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT) on Depression Symptoms in Adolescents: A Randomized Controlled Trial

Abstract

Background Context: Adolescence is a critical developmental stage characterized by significant emotional and psychological changes. Depression, a prevalent mental health disorder among adolescents, can have detrimental effects on academic performance, interpersonal relationships, and overall quality of life. Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT), an evidence-based psychotherapeutic approach, has demonstrated efficacy in treating depression across various age groups. However, its effectiveness in addressing adolescent depression remains under-researched.

Research Methodology: This study conducted a randomized controlled trial involving 200 adolescents aged 13-18 years diagnosed with major depressive disorder (MDD) according to Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, Fifth Edition (DSM-5). Participants were randomly assigned to either a CBT group or a waitlist control group. The CBT group received 16 weekly sessions of individual therapy, while the control group did not receive any intervention during the study period. Depression symptoms were assessed using the Hamilton Depression Rating Scale (HAM-D) at baseline, post-intervention, and three-month follow-up.

Key Findings: Significant differences in depression symptom reduction were observed between the CBT group and the control group post-intervention (p<0.001). The CBT group showed a mean decrease of 9.5 points on the HAM-D scale, while the control group demonstrated a mean decrease of 2.3 points. These findings suggest that CBT is an effective intervention for reducing depression symptoms in adolescents with MDD. Moreover, these improvements were maintained at the three-month follow-up, indicating the durability of the treatment effects.

Implications: The results of this study contribute to the growing body of evidence supporting the use of CBT as a viable and effective treatment option for adolescent depression. Given the high prevalence of depression among adolescents and its associated negative outcomes, the implementation of CBT in clinical practice can potentially improve mental health outcomes for this population. Future research should focus on exploring the long-term effects of CBT, optimizing therapy protocols, and identifying factors that may influence treatment response. Additionally, efforts should be made to increase accessibility and affordability of CBT services to ensure equitable care for all adolescents in need.
