Title: Investigating the Impact of Dietary Supplementation with Omega-3 Fatty Acids on Cognitive Function in Older Adults: A Randomized Controlled Trial

Abstract

Background Context: The global population is experiencing an unprecedented aging, necessitating research into strategies that promote cognitive health and delay age-related cognitive decline. One potential avenue is dietary supplementation with omega-3 fatty acids (n-3 FA), which have been associated with various neuroprotective effects. This study aimed to evaluate the impact of n-3 FA supplementation on cognitive function in older adults.

Research Methodology: A double-blind, randomized controlled trial was conducted over a period of 12 months involving 200 community-dwelling older adults (aged 65 and above) with no history of neurological disorders. Participants were randomly assigned to receive either n-3 FA supplements (containing EPA and DHA at a ratio of 2:1, 1 gram per day) or placebo. Cognitive function was assessed using standardized neuropsychological tests at baseline, 6 months, and 12 months.

Key Findings: After 12 months, the n-3 FA group demonstrated significant improvements in executive function (p<0.001), working memory (p=0.002), and processing speed (p=0.015) compared to the placebo group. No significant differences were observed between groups regarding global cognition, immediate recall, or delayed recall. Furthermore, no adverse effects related to n-3 FA supplementation were reported.

Implications: The findings suggest that dietary supplementation with n-3 FAs may have a positive impact on specific aspects of cognitive function in older adults. Given the increasing prevalence of age-related cognitive decline and the relatively low risk profile of n-3 FA supplements, further research is warranted to explore optimal dosage, duration, and potential mechanisms underlying these effects. This study underscores the importance of dietary interventions as a promising strategy for maintaining cognitive health in older adults.
