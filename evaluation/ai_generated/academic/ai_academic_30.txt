Title: Optimizing Solar Panel Efficiency: A Comparative Study of Monocrystalline and Polycrystalline Silicon Photovoltaic Modules

Abstract

Background Context: The global energy landscape is undergoing a paradigm shift, with renewable energy sources gaining prominence as the world grapples with climate change and depleting fossil fuel reserves. Solar photovoltaic (PV) technology has emerged as a promising solution for sustainable electricity generation, with silicon-based PV modules dominating the market due to their cost-effectiveness and high efficiency. This study aims to compare the performance of monocrystalline (mono-Si) and polycrystalline silicon (poly-Si) PV modules under standard test conditions, with an emphasis on identifying potential avenues for enhancing their efficiency.

Research Methodology: A comparative analysis was conducted using commercially available mono-Si and poly-Si modules from leading manufacturers. The modules were subjected to standardized laboratory testing according to International Electrotechnical Commission (IEC) guidelines, including irradiance, temperature coefficient, and voltage-current characteristics measurements. Additionally, the impact of module design parameters such as cell size, anti-reflection coatings, and encapsulation materials was examined.

Key Findings: The results revealed that mono-Si modules exhibited a higher efficiency (18.2%) compared to poly-Si modules (16.7%), owing to their smaller grain size, superior crystalline quality, and optimized cell design. However, poly-Si modules demonstrated lower manufacturing costs due to simpler production processes and less stringent raw material requirements. Furthermore, the study identified several strategies for improving the efficiency of both types of modules, such as implementing advanced cell structures (e.g., heterojunction with intrinsic thin layer, HIT), employing high-transmission encapsulation materials, and incorporating anti-reflective coatings.

Implications: This research provides valuable insights into the performance characteristics and potential for efficiency enhancement of mono-Si and poly-Si PV modules. The findings underscore the importance of balancing cost and efficiency considerations when selecting solar PV technology for various applications. As the global transition towards renewable energy accelerates, this study serves as a foundation for future research aimed at optimizing the performance of silicon-based PV modules, ultimately contributing to a more sustainable and resilient energy infrastructure.
