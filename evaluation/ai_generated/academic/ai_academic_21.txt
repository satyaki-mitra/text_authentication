Title: An Examination of the Impact of Cognitive Behavioral Therapy on Depression Symptomatology in Adolescents: A Randomized Controlled Trial

Abstract

Background Context: Depression is a pervasive and debilitating mental health disorder, particularly prevalent among adolescents. The World Health Organization has identified depression as the leading cause of disability worldwide for individuals between the ages of 15 and 44 years (World Health Organization, 2017). Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT) has emerged as a promising intervention strategy, yet its efficacy in addressing adolescent depression remains under-explored.

Research Methodology: This study employed a randomized controlled trial design to investigate the effects of CBT on depression symptomatology in a sample of 150 adolescents (M age = 16.3 years, SD = 1.2; 70 males, 80 females) from secondary schools in a suburban area. Participants were randomly assigned to either the experimental group (n=75), receiving 12 weekly sessions of CBT, or the control group (n=75), who received no intervention but standard school counseling services. The primary outcome measure was the Beck Depression Inventory-II (BDI-II; Beck et al., 1996). Pre-intervention, post-intervention, and follow-up assessments were conducted at baseline, immediately following the intervention, and three months post-intervention, respectively.

Key Findings: Significant differences between groups were observed on the BDI-II scores at all time points (F(2, 298) = 164.03, p < .001). Post hoc analyses revealed that adolescents in the CBT group demonstrated substantial reductions in depression symptoms compared to the control group across all assessments (p's < .001). Furthermore, significant within-group improvements were found for both the experimental and control groups from pre-intervention to follow-up (F(2, 298) = 56.34, p < .001), indicating that standard school counseling services may also have a positive impact on adolescent depression symptomatology.

Implications: The findings of this study provide strong evidence supporting the efficacy of CBT in reducing depression symptoms among adolescents. The significant improvements observed in both intervention groups suggest that integrating CBT into standard school counseling services may be an effective strategy for addressing adolescent mental health concerns. Future research should explore the long-term effects of CBT and its potential role in preventing the development and progression of depression in this population.
