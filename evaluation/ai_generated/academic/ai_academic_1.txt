Title: Investigating the Effects of Exogenous Melatonin on Sleep Quality in Shift Work Disorder: A Randomized Controlled Trial

Abstract

Background Context: Shift work disorder (SWD) is a circadian rhythm sleep-wake disorder primarily affecting individuals engaged in non-traditional work schedules. The disruption of the normal sleep-wake cycle, characterized by insomnia, excessive daytime sleepiness, and impaired alertness, negatively impacts the quality of life and productivity among affected workers. Melatonin, a hormone secreted by the pineal gland that regulates circadian rhythms, has been proposed as a potential therapeutic agent for SWD due to its role in sleep regulation.

Research Methodology: This randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial enrolled 150 participants diagnosed with SWD based on the International Classification of Sleep Disorders criteria. Participants were randomly assigned to receive either melatonin (3 mg) or a placebo for an eight-week intervention period. Sleep quality was assessed using the Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index (PSQI), while alertness and daytime functioning were evaluated using the Epworth Sleepiness Scale (ESS) and the Stanford Sleepiness Scale (SSS), respectively.

Key Findings: Compared to the placebo group, participants in the melatonin group demonstrated significant improvements in sleep quality as indicated by lower PSQI scores (p<0.001). Furthermore, participants in the melatonin group reported reduced daytime sleepiness (lower ESS and SSS scores; p<0.001) and improved alertness (higher mean SSS scores; p=0.02). The safety profile of melatonin was favorable, with no significant adverse events reported during the study period.

Implications: These findings suggest that exogenous melatonin supplementation may be an effective and safe strategy for improving sleep quality and alleviating daytime sleepiness in individuals with SWD. Further research is warranted to investigate the long-term effects of melatonin therapy, optimal dosage regimens, and potential mechanisms underlying its therapeutic benefits. If confirmed, these findings could have significant implications for the management of SWD and may contribute to improved sleep health and quality of life among shift workers.
