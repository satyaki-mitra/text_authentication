Title: Investigating the Impact of Climate Change on Arctic Sea Ice Thickness: A Multi-temporal Analysis Using Envisat ASAR Data

Abstract

Background Context: The rapid melting of Arctic sea ice, a critical component of Earth's climate system, has gained significant attention due to its profound implications for global weather patterns and biodiversity. This study aims to contribute to the understanding of the temporal dynamics of Arctic sea ice thickness (SIT) in response to climate change, using synthetic aperture radar (SAR) data from the European Remote Sensing (ERS-2) satellite's Advanced Synthetic Aperture Radar (Envisat ASAR).

Research Methodology: This study employs an interdisciplinary approach, integrating remote sensing techniques with climate science methodologies. Envisat ASAR data from 1998 to 2012 were utilized to generate SIT maps for the Arctic Ocean's five major sea ice regions (Beaufort, Chukchi, East Siberian, Laptev, and Greenland seas). Time-series analysis was performed to examine the trends in SIT, while a linear regression model was developed to evaluate the relationship between SIT and climate variables such as air temperature, atmospheric pressure, and wind speed.

Key Findings: The results indicate a significant decrease in Arctic sea ice thickness over the study period, with an average annual decline of 2.6 cm/year (p<0.05). The Greenland Sea exhibited the most pronounced reduction, with an annual rate of 4.3 cm/year. The regression analysis revealed a strong positive correlation between SIT and air temperature (R²=0.71, p<0.001), suggesting that warmer air temperatures contribute significantly to the decline in sea ice thickness.

Implications: This study provides robust evidence of the ongoing thinning of Arctic sea ice, which has important implications for climate change research and policy-making. The findings underscore the urgency for continued monitoring and modeling efforts to improve our understanding of the complex interactions between sea ice, ocean, and atmosphere in a changing climate. Additionally, the methodology employed in this study can serve as a foundation for future investigations using advanced SAR data from upcoming satellite missions like Sentinel-1. Ultimately, these studies will contribute to more accurate climate projections and informed decision-making regarding Arctic policy and resource management.
