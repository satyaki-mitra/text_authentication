Title: Optimizing Renewable Energy Integration in Smart Grids: A Comparative Analysis of Different Control Strategies

Abstract

In the contemporary global landscape, the transition towards sustainable energy sources is paramount to mitigate environmental degradation and ensure energy security. This study investigates the efficacy of various control strategies for optimizing renewable energy (RE) integration in smart grids, with a focus on reducing power instability and enhancing grid resilience.

The research methodology employed a comprehensive literature review to identify key control strategies, followed by simulation-based analysis using MATLAB/Simulink software. Four distinct control strategies were compared: (1) Proportional-Integral-Derivative (PID), (2) Sliding Mode Control (SMC), (3) Fuzzy Logic Control (FLC), and (4) Artificial Neural Network (ANN). The analysis was conducted under varying renewable energy penetration levels, load conditions, and disturbances.

Key findings revealed that while all strategies demonstrated effective control over power fluctuations, the performance varied significantly under different scenarios. Under low to moderate RE penetration levels, PID exhibited robust stability and minimal overshoot, making it suitable for grid systems with predictable renewable energy generation patterns. However, its performance deteriorated under high RE penetration levels due to the increased complexity of the system dynamics.

SMC demonstrated superior adaptability and robustness in handling non-linear and complex systems, making it an ideal choice for grids with high RE penetration levels or unpredictable renewable energy generation patterns. FLC, on the other hand, offered a balance between computational complexity and performance, demonstrating consistent results under various conditions. ANN showed the highest potential for learning and adapting to dynamic grid conditions but required extensive training data and computational resources.

Implications of this study extend beyond the academic sphere, offering valuable insights for policymakers, utility companies, and researchers involved in the development and implementation of smart grids. The results provide a foundation for selecting optimal control strategies based on specific grid characteristics, renewable energy penetration levels, and operational requirements. Furthermore, this research underscores the importance of integrating advanced control strategies to ensure stable and resilient smart grids capable of accommodating increasing RE integration.

Future research should focus on developing hybrid control strategies that leverage the strengths of multiple approaches to optimize power stability and grid resilience under diverse conditions. Additionally, practical implementation of these strategies in real-world smart grid systems is essential for validating their effectiveness and paving the way towards a sustainable energy future.
