Title: Investigating the Impact of Dietary Diversity on Mental Health among Adolescents in Developing Countries: A Cross-Sectional Study

Abstract

Background Context: The interplay between diet, nutrition, and mental health has garnered increasing attention in global health research. In developing countries, where adolescents face unique challenges related to food security and mental health, understanding the relationship between dietary diversity and mental health is crucial. This study aimed to examine the association between dietary diversity and mental health among adolescents in developing countries.

Research Methodology: A cross-sectional study was conducted in six diverse regions of four developing countries (Bangladesh, Kenya, Nigeria, and Tanzania). A total sample size of 3,000 adolescents aged 12-18 years was selected using stratified random sampling. Data were collected through face-to-face interviews utilizing the Household Food Insecurity Access Scale (HFIAS) to assess dietary diversity and the Strengths and Difficulties Questionnaire (SDQ) to measure mental health. Descriptive statistics, chi-square tests, and multivariate logistic regression analyses were performed using SPSS version 25.

Key Findings: The findings revealed that a higher dietary diversity score was significantly associated with better mental health outcomes (OR = 1.67; 95% CI: 1.30-2.14). Specifically, adolescents consuming foods from four or more food groups had lower odds of having poor mental health compared to those consuming fewer than four food groups. Additionally, the study found that food insecurity was significantly associated with poorer mental health (OR = 2.35; 95% CI: 1.76-3.14).

Implications: The results underscore the importance of promoting dietary diversity and addressing food insecurity to improve mental health among adolescents in developing countries. Policy interventions should focus on school feeding programs, nutrition education, and food assistance initiatives that aim to increase access to a variety of nutrient-dense foods. Further research is needed to elucidate the underlying mechanisms linking dietary diversity, food security, and mental health in this population. This study contributes to the growing body of evidence supporting the integration of nutrition interventions into mental health programs, particularly in resource-limited settings.
