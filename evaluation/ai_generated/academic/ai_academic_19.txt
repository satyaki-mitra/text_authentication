Title: Investigating the Impact of Environmental Enrichment on Cognitive Function in Rodent Models: A Systematic Review

Abstract

Background Context: The exploration of environmental enrichment (EE) as a means to enhance cognitive function in rodents has garnered significant attention within the scientific community. EE, defined as the provision of complex and stimulating physical and social environments, is postulated to promote neural plasticity and improve learning and memory abilities. This systematic review aims to synthesize current evidence regarding the effects of environmental enrichment on cognitive function in rodent models.

Research Methodology: A comprehensive literature search was conducted across multiple databases, including PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science, and PsycINFO, employing a combination of relevant keywords and medical subject headings (MeSH). Inclusion criteria were set for studies that utilized rodent subjects, implemented environmental enrichment interventions, and assessed cognitive function using standardized behavioral tests. A total of 157 articles met the inclusion criteria and underwent a rigorous screening process to ensure methodological quality and relevance to the research question.

Key Findings: The findings indicate that environmental enrichment significantly improves various aspects of cognitive function in rodents, including spatial learning and memory, object recognition, and novelty exploration. These enhancements were observed across multiple brain regions, with a particular focus on the hippocampus, prefrontal cortex, and amygdala. Furthermore, the study revealed that EE's effectiveness is contingent upon factors such as duration, complexity, and age of the enrichment intervention.

Implications: The findings provide robust evidence supporting the use of environmental enrichment as a valuable tool for enhancing cognitive function in rodents. These results have significant implications for understanding the neural mechanisms underlying learning and memory processes and may inform the development of novel therapeutic strategies for neurodegenerative disorders, such as Alzheimer's disease. Additionally, this research underscores the importance of providing enriched environments for laboratory animals to promote their physical and psychological well-being and reduce stress-induced alterations in cognitive function.

Keywords: Environmental Enrichment, Cognitive Function, Rodents, Learning, Memory, Neuroplasticity, Neurodegenerative Disorders
