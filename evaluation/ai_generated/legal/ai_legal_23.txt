Case Title: Smith v. Johnson

Court: Supreme Court of New York, County of Albany

Docket Number: 2021-CV-03456

Judgment Date: February 18, 2022

In the matter of Smith v. Johnson, the plaintiff, John Smith, brought an action against the defendant, Jane Johnson, alleging breach of contract and negligence. The dispute arose from a written agreement dated October 1, 2021, between the parties for the sale and purchase of real property located at 123 Main Street, Albany, New York (the "Property").

The plaintiff, John Smith, claimed that he had paid the defendant, Jane Johnson, the agreed-upon purchase price in full on October 30, 2021, and that the defendant failed to transfer ownership of the Property as stipulated in the agreement. The plaintiff further alleged that the defendant's actions constituted a breach of contract and caused him damages.

In her answer to the complaint, the defendant, Jane Johnson, admitted that she had received the purchase price but denied any breach of contract. She contended that she was unable to transfer ownership of the Property due to an undisclosed encumbrance on the title. The defendant asserted that she had made reasonable efforts to remove the encumbrance and sought a reasonable extension of time to complete the transfer of ownership.

Following discovery, the court held a non-jury trial on February 15, 2022, at which both parties presented evidence and arguments. The court found that the defendant had breached the contract by failing to transfer ownership of the Property within the agreed timeframe, despite having received the purchase price in full. The court further determined that the defendant's failure to disclose the encumbrance on the title before entering into the agreement constituted fraudulent concealment.

In considering the damages suffered by the plaintiff as a result of the defendant's breach, the court found that the plaintiff had incurred expenses for property inspections and legal fees, and was unable to secure alternative housing during the delay caused by the defendant's breach.
