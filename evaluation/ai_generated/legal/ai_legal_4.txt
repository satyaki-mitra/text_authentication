Case Title: Smith v. Johnson

Court of Appeals, State of New York, Appellate Division Third Judicial Department

Docket Number: A-67895

Decision Date: March 12, 2023

Judge: Honorable Jane R. Solomon

Facts:
The plaintiff, Mr. Smith, brought an action against the defendant, Ms. Johnson, alleging breach of contract and negligence in connection with a real estate transaction. The parties entered into a written agreement for the sale of property located at 123 Elm Street, whereby Ms. Johnson agreed to sell the property to Mr. Smith for a specified price, subject to certain conditions, including the completion of a home inspection and the execution of a mortgage.

In this case, Mr. Smith asserts that Ms. Johnson failed to comply with the conditions set forth in the agreement, specifically by failing to secure necessary permits for improvements made to the property and by concealing material defects during the home inspection. As a result, Mr. Smith claims that he suffered damages, including additional costs for repairs and diminished value of the property.

Ms. Johnson denies these allegations and argues that she fulfilled her obligations under the contract, contending that any issues with the property were disclosed during the home inspection process. Furthermore, Ms. Johnson maintains that Mr. Smith is liable for breach of contract due to his failure to complete the mortgage financing as required by the agreement.

Issue:
1. Whether Ms. Johnson breached the contract by failing to secure necessary permits and concealing material defects during the home inspection process, entitling Mr. Smith to damages.
2. Whether Mr. Smith breached the contract by failing to complete mortgage financing as required, allowing Ms. Johnson to assert a claim for damages against him.

Holding:
1. The Court finds that Ms. Johnson is liable for breach of contract, as she failed to secure necessary permits and concealed material defects during the home inspection process. This breach caused Mr. Smith to incur additional costs for repairs and suffer a diminished value of the property, entitling him to recover damages.
