Case Summary:

In the matter between the plaintiff, John Smith (hereinafter "Plaintiff"), and the defendant, Jane Johnson (hereinafter "Defendant"), this Court addresses a dispute concerning a breach of contract.

The Plaintiff alleges that on March 1, 2021, the parties entered into an agreement for the sale of real property located at 123 Main Street, New York, NY (the "Property"). Pursuant to the terms of said agreement, the Defendant agreed to sell and transfer the Property to the Plaintiff for a total purchase price of $500,000.

The Plaintiff asserts that despite his performance in fulfilling all obligations under the contract, including payment of the purchase price in full on April 1, 2021, the Defendant has failed to transfer ownership of the Property to the Plaintiff, thereby breaching their agreement. The Plaintiff seeks specific performance, damages for breach of contract, and costs of action.

In response, the Defendant contends that she is unable to transfer the Property due to a lien on the property discovered after the execution of the contract, and thus claims that the breach was caused by an unforeseen contingency beyond her control. The Defendant denies any liability for breaching the contract and requests that the Court dismiss the Plaintiff's claim or grant judgment in her favor.

This Court finds it necessary to determine whether:

1) The Defendant has breached the contract by failing to transfer ownership of the Property to the Plaintiff;
2) If a breach has occurred, whether the Defendant is responsible for damages and specific performance; and
3) If an unforeseen contingency exists that justifies non-performance under the terms of the contract.

In order to establish a claim for breach of contract, the Plaintiff must prove the existence of a valid contract, the Defendant's failure to perform their obligations, and resulting damages.
