Title: An Unusual Case of Immunoglobulin A Nephropathy in a Pediatric Patient

Background: Immunoglobulin A (IgA) nephropathy, also known as Berger's disease, is the most common primary glomerulonephritis worldwide. It primarily affects young adults but can occur in pediatric patients. Here, we present an unusual case of IgA nephropathy in a 10-year-old child.

Case Presentation: A previously healthy 10-year-old boy presented with a two-week history of hematuria and edema. His medical history was unremarkable, except for an episode of streptococcal pharyngitis one month prior. Physical examination revealed pitting edema on both lower extremities, and urinalysis showed red blood cells and hematuria with normal protein levels.

Investigations: Renal function tests were within normal limits. Urine IgA level was significantly elevated (205 mg/dL, normal range < 67 mg/dL). Serum complement levels were normal. Kidney biopsy showed mesangial proliferative glomerulonephritis with predominantly IgA deposits, consistent with a diagnosis of IgA nephropathy.

Treatment: The patient was initiated on oral prednisone (1 mg/kg per day) and aspirin (80 mg/day). ACE inhibitors were also started for blood pressure control.

Outcome and Follow-up: After two months of treatment, the patient's hematuria had resolved, and edema was significantly reduced. Renal function remained normal. The patient is currently under close follow-up with regular monitoring of urine protein levels and renal function tests.

Conclusion: This case underscores the importance of considering IgA nephropathy in pediatric patients presenting with hematuria, even in the absence of a known predisposing factor. Early diagnosis and appropriate management can lead to a favorable prognosis, as demonstrated in this case. Further studies are needed to elucidate the etiology and optimal treatment strategies for IgA nephropathy in pediatric patients.
