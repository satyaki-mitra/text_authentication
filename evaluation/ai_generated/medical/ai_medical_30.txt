Title: A Case Study on Successful Management of Refractory Angioedema in a Pediatric Patient Utilizing Ecallantide

Abstract

Background: Angioedema, a clinical manifestation of the bradykinin-mediated pathway, is characterized by rapid swelling of deep tissues. Although typically self-limiting, refractory angioedema can pose significant morbidity and mortality risks, particularly in pediatric patients. This case report highlights the successful management of a refractory angioedema episode in a 9-year-old patient using ecallantide, an FDA-approved plasma kallikrein inhibitor.

Case Presentation: A 9-year-old male with no known allergies presented to the emergency department with acute onset facial swelling that had progressed over a period of 24 hours. The swelling involved the tongue, lips, and eyelids, and was accompanied by dysphagia, odynophagia, and dyspnea. The patient's medical history was significant for recurrent angioedema attacks triggered by emotional stress, physical exertion, or upper respiratory infections, with limited responses to conventional treatments including antihistamines, corticosteroids, and C1 esterase inhibitor concentrate.

Diagnostic Approach: The patient's presentation was consistent with angioedema. Laboratory tests revealed normal levels of complement components C3 and C4, suggestive of a non-hereditary angioedema (NHAE) caused by an increased activity of plasma kallikrein rather than deficiency in C1 esterase inhibitor.

Treatment: Given the patient's refractory response to conventional treatments, ecallantide was administered at a dosage of 30 mg subcutaneously every 8 hours. The swelling began to resolve within 4 hours post-treatment, and after 24 hours, complete resolution was achieved.

Outcomes: Over the subsequent week, the patient remained symptom-free without experiencing any adverse effects from ecallantide treatment. Follow-up visits revealed that prophylactic use of ecallantide effectively prevented future angioedema attacks triggered by emotional stress or upper respiratory infections.

Discussion: This case study underscores the utility of ecallantide as a safe and effective treatment option for refractory angioedema in pediatric patients. Ecallantide's ability to selectively inhibit plasma kallikrein offers an alternative treatment strategy for NHAE patients who do not respond to conventional therapies.
