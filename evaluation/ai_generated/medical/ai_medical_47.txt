Title: A Case Study on Successful Management of Acute Pancreatitis Complicated by Severe Hypertriglyceridemia

Abstract

Background: Acute pancreatitis (AP) is a prevalent gastrointestinal disorder, with hypertriglyceridemia (HTG) being a significant risk factor. This case study presents the successful management of an atypical case of AP complicated by severe HTG.

Case Presentation: A 45-year-old male presented to the emergency department with a four-day history of persistent abdominal pain, nausea, and vomiting. The patient reported no significant medical history but admitted to excessive alcohol consumption and a high-fat diet. On admission, laboratory results revealed an amylase level of 1500 U/L (normal range: 26-110 U/L) and lipase level of 3000 U/L (normal range: 13-60 U/L), indicating acute pancreatitis. Moreover, a triglyceride level of 1500 mg/dL (normal range: 40-160 mg/dL) was recorded, suggesting secondary AP due to severe HTG. The patient's physical examination was unremarkable except for epigastric tenderness and guarding. Imaging studies including abdominal ultrasound and computed tomography confirmed the diagnosis of AP.

Diagnostic Approach: Given the high triglyceride levels, a thorough lipid profile was conducted to rule out other potential etiologies such as monogenic dyslipidemias or secondary causes of HTG. The patient was also screened for other comorbidities that could contribute to AP, including autoimmune diseases and medication use.

Treatment: The patient was admitted to the hospital and managed with supportive care, including intravenous fluids, analgesia, and nutritional support. Given the severe HTG, plasmapheresis was initiated to reduce triglyceride levels below 1000 mg/dL, making it safe to administer pancreatic enzyme replacement therapy (PERT). The patient's alcohol consumption was also strongly discouraged, and a low-fat diet was recommended.

Outcomes: After five days of plasmapheresis and strict management of the underlying causes, the patient's triglyceride levels decreased to 900 mg/dL, allowing for PERT administration. The patient's abdominal pain improved significantly, and he was discharged on hospital day seven with instructions to continue a low-fat diet and avoid alcohol.
