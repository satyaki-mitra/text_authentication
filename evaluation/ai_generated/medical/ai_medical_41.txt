Title: A Case Study on Successful Management of Severe Psoriatic Arthritis through Adalimumab Therapy

Abstract

Background: Psoriatic arthritis (PsA) is a chronic inflammatory disease characterized by joint pain, swelling, and skin lesions. This case study highlights an effective management strategy for severe PsA utilizing Adalimumab, a Tumor Necrosis Factor-α (TNF-α) inhibitor.

Case Presentation: A 45-year-old Caucasian female presented with a six-year history of polyarticular arthritis and psoriatic plaques on the elbows, knees, and scalp. The patient reported persistent pain, morning stiffness, and functional limitation in daily activities. Dactylitis, enthesitis, and sacroiliac joint involvement were also observed. The Disease Activity Score-28 (DAS28) was 6.4, indicating high disease activity.

Diagnostic Approach: The diagnosis of PsA was confirmed based on the CASPAR criteria (Clinical vs. radiographic axial versus peripheral arthritis, Nail psoriasis, Objective joint tenderness and swelling, Psoriasis family history, Asymmetrical arthritis, Reiter's-like extra-articular manifestations) and MRI findings showing synovitis and enthesitis.

Treatment: The patient was initiated on Adalimumab (40mg every other week) following the failure of methotrexate therapy. After two months, the dose was adjusted to 40mg weekly due to inadequate response. Simultaneously, topical corticosteroids and calcipotriol were used for skin lesions management.

Outcomes: Following six months of Adalimumab treatment, the patient's DAS28 score decreased significantly to 3.2, indicating a good clinical response (minimal disease activity). The patient reported significant reduction in joint pain and functional improvement, with no new or worsening skin lesions. No adverse events related to Adalimumab were reported during the observation period.

Discussion: This case demonstrates the effectiveness of Adalimumab in managing severe PsA. Early introduction of a TNF-α inhibitor such as Adalimumab can lead to significant improvements in disease activity, joint pain, and functional status in patients with PsA who have an inadequate response to conventional therapy. However, careful monitoring for potential adverse events is necessary when using biologic agents.
