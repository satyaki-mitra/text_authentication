Title: A Case Study on Successful Management of Severe Acute Pancreatitis Complicated by Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome (ARDS)

Abstract

Background: Acute pancreatitis (AP) is a common acute abdominal disorder with varied presentations and outcomes. A severe form, necrotizing pancreatitis, can complicate with systemic inflammatory response syndrome (SIRS), multi-organ dysfunction, and even death. This case study presents an exemplary management of a patient with severe acute pancreatitis complicated by Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome (ARDS).

Case Presentation: A 52-year-old male presented to the emergency department with epigastric abdominal pain, nausea, and vomiting for three days. His medical history included hypertension and hyperlipidemia. On physical examination, tenderness was noted in the upper abdomen with a white blood cell count of 16.5 x 10^9/L (normal range: 4-11 x 10^9/L) and serum amylase level of 278 U/L (normal range: 30-110 U/L). Abdominal ultrasound revealed a swollen pancreas with peripancreatic fluid collections, confirming the diagnosis of acute necrotizing pancreatitis.

Subsequently, the patient developed progressive hypoxemia and required mechanical ventilation due to ARDS. The treatment strategy included aggressive fluid resuscitation, broad-spectrum antibiotics, nutritional support, pain management, and intensive care unit monitoring. A computed tomography-guided percutaneous drainage of the pancreatic necrosis was performed to alleviate pressure on other organs and reduce inflammation.

During hospitalization, the patient's condition improved gradually. The serum amylase level decreased to 85 U/L after five days and reached normal levels after ten days. The white blood cell count normalized within a week. The patient was successfully weaned off mechanical ventilation on the eleventh day due to significant improvement in oxygenation index.

Discussion: Early diagnosis, aggressive management, and timely interventions played crucial roles in the successful recovery of this patient. The utilization of percutaneous drainage for pancreatic necrosis not only reduced the pressure on other organs but also facilitated infection control. The early detection and management of ARDS, a life-threatening complication of severe acute pancreatitis, were crucial in this patient's survival.
