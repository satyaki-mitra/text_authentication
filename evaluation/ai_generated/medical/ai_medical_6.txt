Title: A Case Study on Refractory Angina: Successful Management with Coronary Artery Bypass Grafting

Background: Refractory angina is a challenging condition characterized by persistent chest pain at rest despite maximal medical therapy, often leading to significant morbidity and reduced quality of life.

Case Presentation: A 65-year-old male patient presented with a four-year history of progressive exertional angina, worsening over the past six months. The patient had a significant cardiac history, including a myocardial infarction five years prior and subsequent coronary artery stenting. On examination, the patient displayed signs of chronic ischemia with an ejection fraction of 30%. Stress testing revealed reversible perfusion defects in the anterior and lateral walls of the left ventricle, suggesting extensive coronary artery disease.

Intervention: Given the refractory nature of the angina despite maximal medical therapy, a decision was made to proceed with elective coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG). The procedure involved harvesting left internal mammary artery and saphenous vein grafts for revascularization of the left anterior descending artery, obtuse marginal branch, and right coronary artery.

Outcome: Postoperatively, the patient demonstrated marked improvement in angina symptoms, with complete resolution reported at one-month follow-up. The patient was discharged on standard post-CABG medications and advised to maintain a heart-healthy lifestyle. At six-month follow-up, echocardiography showed improved left ventricular function (ejection fraction 45%).

Conclusion: This case highlights the successful management of refractory angina through coronary artery bypass grafting in a patient with extensive coronary artery disease. The procedure provided significant relief from symptoms and improved cardiac function, emphasizing the importance of surgical intervention when maximal medical therapy is insufficient. Further long-term follow-up will be necessary to assess the durability of these improvements.
