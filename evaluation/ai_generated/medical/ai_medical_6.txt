Title: A Case Study of Successful Management of Refractory Angina Pectoris Through Coronary Artery Bypass Grafting

Abstract

Background: Refractory angina pectoris (RAP) is a challenging clinical condition characterized by persistent anginal symptoms refractory to optimal medical therapy. This case report presents the successful management of a patient with RAP through coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG).

Case Presentation: A 65-year-old male, non-smoker, with a history of diabetes mellitus type 2 and hypertension, presented with recurrent episodes of anginal chest pain despite optimal medical therapy. His symptoms were rated as Canadian Cardiovascular Society (CCS) class IV, and his electrocardiogram (ECG) revealed prolonged ST-segment depression in the anterior leads. Transthoracic echocardiography demonstrated severe three-vessel coronary artery disease (CAD) with left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) of 40%. The patient was diagnosed with RAP due to the extensive CAD and persistent anginal symptoms despite optimal medical therapy.

Diagnostic Approach: Given the refractory nature of the patient's symptoms, an invasive diagnostic approach was pursued. Coronary angiography confirmed the severe three-vessel CAD, with the left anterior descending (LAD) and circumflex arteries showing 90% and 80% stenosis respectively, and the right coronary artery (RCA) exhibiting total occlusion.

Treatment: The patient was scheduled for CABG. During surgery, a left internal mammary artery (LIMA) graft was anastomosed to the LAD, and saphenous vein grafts were used to bypass the occluded RCA and the 80% stenosis in the circumflex artery.

Outcomes: Post-operatively, the patient's anginal symptoms significantly improved, and he was discharged with CCS class I symptoms. Follow-up ECG showed normal sinus rhythm with no ST segment depression. The LVEF increased to 55%. At six-month follow-up, the patient reported continued symptomatic improvement and a significant quality of life enhancement.

Discussion: This case highlights the successful management of RAP through CABG in a patient with severe three-vessel CAD who failed to respond to optimal medical therapy. The improved symptoms and increased LVEF post-operatively underscore the importance of revascularization in such cases.
