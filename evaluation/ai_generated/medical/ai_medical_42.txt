Title: A Case Study on Successful Management of Severe Psoriatic Arthritis with Biologic Therapy: Adalimumab

Abstract

Background: Psoriatic arthritis (PsA) is a chronic inflammatory joint disease associated with psoriasis, affecting approximately 0.3-1% of the general population. The disease can manifest as a wide spectrum of symptoms, ranging from mild joint pain to severe polyarthritis, leading to significant morbidity and reduced quality of life (QoL). This case report aims to illustrate the successful management of a patient with severe PsA using biologic therapy - Adalimumab.

Case Presentation: A 45-year-old Caucasian male presented with a 10-year history of psoriasis plaques on his elbows, knees, and scalp, accompanied by progressive polyarthritis over the past three years. He reported morning stiffness lasting more than one hour, swollen and painful joints, fatigue, and reduced mobility in both hands and feet. The modified Health Assessment Questionnaire (mHAQ) score was 2.5, indicating high disability. Laboratory findings revealed elevated erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR), C-reactive protein (CRP), and rheumatoid factor (RF). X-ray imaging showed joint space narrowing, bone erosion, and osteophyte formation consistent with PsA. Skin biopsy confirmed psoriasis.

Diagnostic Approach: Given the patient's history of psoriasis, joint symptoms, and laboratory findings, the diagnosis of PsA was made based on the Classification Criteria for Psoriatic Arthritis (CASPAR) and American College of Rheumatology (ACR)/European League Against Rheumatism (EULAR) guidelines.

Treatment: Initially, the patient was treated with methotrexate, but due to inadequate response and intolerable side effects, the treatment regimen was switched to Adalimumab, a tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNFα) inhibitor. The patient received subcutaneous Adalimumab 40mg every other week.

Outcomes: After 12 weeks of therapy, the patient reported significant improvement in joint pain and morning stiffness, with a reduction in mHAQ score to 1.0. X-ray imaging revealed stabilization of joint damage. The patient continued Adalimumab treatment for two years, maintaining clinical remission with minimal side effects.
