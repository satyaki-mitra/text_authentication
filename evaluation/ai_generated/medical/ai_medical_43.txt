Title: A Case Study of Successful Management of Refractory Gastrointestinal Stromal Tumor (GIST) with Imatinib and Sunitinib

Abstract

Background: Gastrointestinal stromal tumors (GISTs) are the most common mesenchymal neoplasms of the gastrointestinal tract, accounting for approximately 1% of all gastrointestinal malignancies. The majority of GISTs express the activated c-KIT tyrosine kinase receptor and/or platelet-derived growth factor receptor alpha (PDGFR-α), making them susceptible to targeted therapy.

Case Presentation: A 65-year-old female presented with a three-month history of progressive abdominal pain, nausea, and weight loss. Computed tomography (CT) scan revealed a large, heterogeneously enhancing mass in the proximal jejunum, measuring 12 cm x 8 cm. Endoscopic ultrasound confirmed a submucosal tumor with irregular margins, consistent with a GIST. Immunohistochemical staining for CD117 and DOG1 was positive, confirming the diagnosis of a GIST. The patient had an unfavorable tumor site (midgut) and a high mitotic rate (20 mitoses per 50 high-power fields), indicating a high risk of recurrence according to the National Institutes of Health (NIH) risk stratification system.

Diagnostic Approach: The patient was started on Imatinib, a selective inhibitor of KIT and PDGFR, at a dose of 400 mg daily. After two months, an CT scan showed no significant change in the size of the tumor, suggesting resistance to Imatinib therapy. The patient was then switched to Sunitinib, another multikinase inhibitor that targets KIT and PDGFR.

Treatment: The patient tolerated Sunitinib well with minimal side effects. After four months of treatment, a follow-up CT scan showed a significant reduction in the size of the tumor (6 cm x 4 cm) and no new lesions, indicating a response to Sunitinib therapy.

Outcomes: The patient has been on Sunitinib for one year with stable disease and minimal side effects. She reports improvement in her symptoms and quality of life. Regular follow-ups are scheduled to monitor the tumor and manage any potential complications or side effects.
