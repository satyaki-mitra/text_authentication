Title: A Case Study on Successful Management of Refractory Gastroparesis Using Botulinum Toxin Type A Injection

Abstract

Background: Gastroparesis, a motility disorder characterized by delayed gastric emptying in the absence of mechanical obstruction, presents a significant challenge to clinicians due to its complex etiology and refractory nature. This case report describes the successful management of a patient with refractory gastroparesis utilizing botulinum toxin type A (BoNT-A) injection therapy.

Case Presentation: A 45-year-old female, diagnosed with diabetes mellitus type 1 for 30 years, presented with chronic postprandial nausea, vomiting, and abdominal pain of 6 months' duration. She had a history of gastroparesis, which was initially managed conservatively with dietary modifications, prokinetic agents, and antiemetics but showed no significant improvement. On examination, her weight was 50 kg (BMI: 19.8), blood glucose level was 160 mg/dL, and abdominal examination revealed mild epigastric tenderness without rebound or guarding. Gastrointestinal workup including upper gastrointestinal series, gastric emptying scintigraphy, and endoscopy were unremarkable except for delayed gastric emptying. A diagnosis of refractory gastroparesis was made based on the patient's symptoms and diagnostic findings.

Diagnostic Approach: The patient underwent a 16-gauge endoscopic ultrasound (EUS) guided BoNT-A injection. Using real-time ultrasound guidance, 100 units of BoNT-A were injected into the pyloric sphincter and proximal antrum.

Treatment: The patient received the BoNT-A injection in an outpatient setting under conscious sedation. Post-procedure, she was advised to maintain a liquid diet for 24 hours followed by gradual advancement of solids over the next few days. Prokinetic agents and antiemetics were gradually tapered off.

Outcomes: Immediately post-procedure, the patient reported significant relief from nausea and vomiting. Gastric emptying scintigraphy performed 4 weeks after the procedure showed improved gastric emptying (51% at 60 minutes compared to baseline of 20%). The patient continued to experience symptomatic improvement, allowing for the complete withdrawal of prokinetic agents and antiemetics by the 8th week.
