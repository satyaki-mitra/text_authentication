Title: A Rare Case of Pseudoxanthoma Elasticum Presenting with Ocular Complications

Abstract

We present an unusual case of a 45-year-old female patient, who was admitted to our institution for the management of ocular complications. The patient had been experiencing progressive visual disturbances in both eyes for the past six months. On initial examination, the patient's ophthalmologic evaluation revealed angioid streaks and multiple yellowish plaques around the neck and axillae, which were suggestive of Pseudoxanthoma Elasticum (PXE).

The patient's medical history was unremarkable except for hypertension, which was under control with medication. Dermatological consultation confirmed the diagnosis of PXE, based on the characteristic features and distribution of lesions. Ophthalmological workup revealed bilateral retinal pigment epithelial changes and choroidal neovascularization, suggesting advanced ocular involvement.

The patient was managed with a multidisciplinary approach involving ophthalmology, dermatology, and internal medicine. Ocular complications were managed with anti-vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) injections, while systemic management included controlling hypertension and monitoring for cardiovascular and renal involvement, as these are common complications associated with PXE.

This case highlights the importance of early recognition and multidisciplinary management in patients with PXE to minimize ocular morbidity and prevent systemic complications. Further research is needed to develop effective treatment strategies for this rare, progressive, and genetically determined disorder.

Keywords: Pseudoxanthoma Elasticum, Ocular Complications, Anti-VEGF Therapy, Multidisciplinary Management
