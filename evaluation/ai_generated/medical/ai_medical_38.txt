Title: A Case Study on Successful Management of Severe Acute Pancreatitis Complicated by Sepsis in a Patient with Gallstone Disease

Abstract

Background: Acute pancreatitis (AP) is a common acute abdominal disease, often associated with gallstones. The severity of AP can range from mild edematous to life-threatening necrotizing forms. This case report presents the successful management of a patient with severe acute pancreatitis complicated by sepsis, secondary to gallstone disease.

Case Presentation: A 58-year-old female presented to the emergency department with a three-day history of epigastric abdominal pain radiating to her back, accompanied by nausea and vomiting. The patient's medical history included hypertension, type II diabetes mellitus, and gallstones. Initial laboratory results showed elevated amylase (1500 U/L) and lipase (2000 U/L), supporting the diagnosis of acute pancreatitis. The patient was categorized as having severe AP based on the Revised Atlanta Criteria due to persistent abdominal pain, increased white blood cell count (14.5 x 10^9/L), and a serum lactate level above 2 mmol/L.

Diagnostic Approach: Computed tomography (CT) scan of the abdomen revealed necrotizing pancreatitis with peripancreatic fluid collection. The patient was also found to have cholelithiasis and choledocholithiasis. A percutaneous transhepatic cholangiogram was performed, confirming the obstruction of the common bile duct by a large stone.

Treatment: The patient was admitted to the intensive care unit for close monitoring and supportive management, including fluid resuscitation, pain control, and nutritional support with total parenteral nutrition (TPN). Antibiotics were initiated due to the development of sepsis. Endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP) was performed for stone extraction and stenting of the common bile duct.

Outcomes: The patient responded well to the initial management and showed improvement in abdominal pain, vital signs stabilization, and resolution of sepsis. ERCP successfully removed the obstructing stone from the common bile duct. However, the necrotizing pancreatitis necessitated a prolonged hospital stay for ongoing supportive care. The patient was eventually discharged in good condition after two months, with follow-up appointments scheduled for monitoring of pancreatic healing and resolution of the peripancreatic fluid collection
