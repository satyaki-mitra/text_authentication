Title: A Rare Case of Pseudoxanthoma Elasticum Manifesting as Recurrent Retinal Detachment

Case Presentation:

A 52-year-old Caucasian female presented with a three-month history of intermittent floaters and peripheral vision loss in her left eye. The patient had no significant past medical history, but reported a family history of similar visual symptoms among close relatives. Upon initial examination, slit lamp biomicroscopy revealed angioid streaks emanating from the optic disc, suggestive of Pseudoxanthoma Elasticum (PXE). Funduscopic examination confirmed multiple retinal tears and detachments in both eyes, with the left eye demonstrating a more pronounced detachment.

Investigations:

Dermatological evaluation revealed yellowish-brown plaques on the neck and axillae, consistent with Calcification of Plaque Xanthomata, another feature of PXE. Laboratory findings were unremarkable. X-ray and CT scans showed calcifications in the elastic tissue of the aorta and other large arteries, confirming systemic involvement. Genetic testing for mutations in ABCC6 gene, the most common genetic cause of PXE, was positive.

Treatment:

The patient underwent multiple vitrectomies with silicone oil tamponade to repair the retinal detachments in both eyes. Due to the recurrent nature of the detachments and the systemic involvement of PXE, regular ophthalmologic follow-ups were scheduled along with management for potential cardiovascular complications.

Discussion:

This case highlights the importance of recognizing Pseudoxanthoma Elasticum (PXE), a rare and often underdiagnosed multisystem disorder, in patients presenting with retinal detachments. The disease is characterized by calcification and fragmentation of elastic fibers in the skin, eyes, and cardiovascular system. Early recognition can help in managing ophthalmologic complications and preventing potential cardiovascular events.
