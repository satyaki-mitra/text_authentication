Title: A Case Study on Successful Management of Acute Pancreatitis Complicated by Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome (ARDS)

Abstract

Background: Acute pancreatitis (AP) is a common gastrointestinal disorder, often precipitated by gallstones or alcohol abuse. A complication of AP, Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome (ARDS), can significantly worsen the prognosis. This case report highlights an unusual presentation and successful management of AP complicated by ARDS in a 58-year-old male patient.

Case Presentation: The patient presented to the emergency department with severe epigastric abdominal pain, nausea, and vomiting. His medical history included hypertension, hyperlipidemia, and chronic alcohol consumption. Initial laboratory findings revealed a serum amylase level of 1500 U/L (normal range: 28-136 U/L) and lipase level of 4000 U/L (normal range: 13-60 U/L), consistent with AP. Chest X-ray revealed bilateral infiltrates, suggesting the development of ARDS.

Diagnostic Approach: Further investigations included abdominal ultrasound, which confirmed the diagnosis of AP. The patient was also diagnosed with ARDS based on the Berlin Definition, including acute onset of respiratory failure, bilateral opacities on chest imaging not fully explained by heart failure or fluid overload, and no signs of left atrial hypertension.

Treatment: The patient was immediately admitted to the intensive care unit (ICU) for supportive care, including oxygen therapy, mechanical ventilation, and intravenous fluids. Nutritional support was initiated with total parenteral nutrition due to the severity of his condition. Antibiotics were administered to prevent secondary infections, and analgesics were provided for pain management.

Outcomes: Over the course of one week, the patient's respiratory status improved significantly, with a gradual reduction in ventilator support. His serum amylase and lipase levels also began to decrease, indicating resolution of AP. By the end of the second week, the patient was extubated, and he was discharged from the ICU on the third week. After a month of hospitalization, he was discharged home with instructions for alcohol cessation and follow-up appointments with a gastroenterologist and pulmonologist.
