Title: A Case Report of Successful Treatment of Recalcitrant Pemphigus Vulgaris with Rituximab

Abstract

Background: Pemphigus vulgaris (PV), a rare autoimmune blistering disease characterized by intraepidermal acantholysis, can lead to debilitating mucocutaneous erosions. The mainstay of treatment involves immunosuppressive agents such as corticosteroids, azathioprine, and mycophenolate mofetil. However, these therapies often have significant side effects and may be insufficient in managing recalcitrant cases. Rituximab, a chimeric monoclonal anti-CD20 antibody, has shown promise in treating PV due to its ability to deplete B cells responsible for producing pathogenic autoantibodies.

Case Presentation: A 64-year-old Caucasian male presented with a two-month history of painful oral and nasal ulcers accompanied by erosions on the trunk, extremities, and genitalia. A biopsy revealed epidermal acantholysis consistent with PV. Despite high-dose prednisone, azathioprine, and dapsone therapy, the patient's condition worsened, leading to severe mucosal involvement and an inability to eat or drink comfortably.

Diagnostic Approach: The diagnosis of PV was confirmed through direct immunofluorescence (DIF) analysis, which demonstrated IgG and C3 deposits at the epidermal-dermal junction. The presence of circulating autoantibodies against desmoglein 1 and 3 was also detected by enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA).

Treatment: Given the patient's recalcitrant PV and significant side effects from conventional treatments, a decision was made to initiate rituximab therapy. The patient received four weekly infusions of 375 mg/m² Rituximab.

Outcomes: Following the first cycle of rituximab, the patient experienced a rapid improvement in symptoms, with complete resolution of oral and nasal ulcers within two weeks. Mucocutaneous erosions also began to heal, and the patient reported significantly improved quality of life. DIF analysis after one month revealed no detectable IgG or C3 deposits at the epidermal-dermal junction, confirming the success of rituximab treatment.
