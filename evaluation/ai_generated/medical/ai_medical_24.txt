Title: A Case Study on the Successful Management of Acute Pancreatitis with Early Endoscopic Retrograde Cholangiopancreatography (ERCP)

Abstract

Background: Acute pancreatitis (AP) is a common gastrointestinal disorder characterized by inflammation of the pancreas. The severity varies from mild, self-limiting disease to life-threatening complications. Early identification and appropriate management are crucial for favorable outcomes. This case report presents an unusual presentation of AP in a young patient successfully managed with early ERCP intervention.

Case Presentation: A 32-year-old male presented to the emergency department with a one-day history of severe epigastric abdominal pain radiating to his back, accompanied by nausea and vomiting. The patient had no significant past medical history, was a non-smoker, and consumed alcohol occasionally. On physical examination, the abdomen was tender in the epigastric region with localized guarding and rebound tenderness. Laboratory tests revealed elevated serum amylase (1500 U/L; normal range 26-98 U/L) and lipase (1400 U/L; normal range 13-60 U/L), confirming the diagnosis of AP. The Bismuth criteria were used to classify the disease as moderately severe due to the presence of localized peripancreatic fluid collection on abdominal ultrasound.

Diagnostic Approach: Given the patient's young age and moderately severe AP, an endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP) was performed within 24 hours of admission to identify any potential biliary cause and to provide therapeutic intervention if necessary. ERCP revealed a common bile duct stone causing obstructive pancreatitis.

Treatment: The common bile duct stone was successfully extracted, and a stent was placed for biliary drainage. The patient was managed conservatively with intravenous fluids, analgesia, and nasogastric suction. Pancreatic enzyme replacement therapy was initiated to manage nutritional support.

Outcomes: Post-ERCP, the patient's abdominal pain significantly improved, and laboratory values of amylase and lipase gradually normalized over the next five days. The patient was discharged on hospital day 7 with a plan for elective cholecystectomy in three months. Follow-up at one month post-discharge revealed complete resolution of symptoms, and normalization of biochemical markers.

Discussion: This case highlights the importance of early ERCP intervention in managing acute pancreatitis
