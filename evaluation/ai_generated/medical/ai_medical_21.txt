Title: A Case Study on the Successful Management of Severe Pemphigus Vulgaris in an Elderly Patient

Abstract

Background: Pemphigus vulgaris (PV) is a rare autoimmune blistering disorder characterized by intraepidermal acantholysis and autoantibodies against desmoglein 3 and 1. The disease primarily affects middle-aged adults, with the elderly population being underrepresented in current literature. This case report highlights an uncommon occurrence of severe PV in an elderly patient and the successful management strategy employed.

Case Presentation: An 80-year-old Caucasian female presented to our dermatology clinic with a three-week history of painful mucocutaneous bullous lesions and erosions on the oral mucosa, nasolabial folds, and extremities. On examination, multiple flaccid bullae with hemorrhagic crusts were observed, consistent with PV (Figure 1). Direct immunofluorescence of a skin biopsy revealed intercellular IgG deposits (Figure 2), confirming the diagnosis. The patient's comorbidities included hypertension, diabetes mellitus, and chronic kidney disease.

Diagnostic Approach: Given the patient's age and atypical presentation, a thorough workup was performed to rule out other potential causes of bullous lesions, such as epidermolysis bullosa acquisita, bullous pemphigoid, and linear IgA dermatosis. Laboratory tests, including complete blood count (CBC), comprehensive metabolic panel (CMP), and autoimmune panel, were unremarkable. Indirect immunofluorescence testing confirmed the presence of anti-desmoglein 3 and 1 antibodies, further supporting the diagnosis of PV.

Treatment: Due to the patient's advanced age, multiple comorbidities, and severe disease presentation, a stepwise treatment approach was initiated. Prednisone 60 mg daily was started, followed by the addition of azathioprine 150 mg daily for immunosuppression after one month due to inadequate response to corticosteroids alone. Intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG) therapy was also initiated at a dose of 2 g/kg divided over two days, repeated every three weeks. The patient's lesions were treated with topical antimicrobial and protective ointments to prevent secondary infections and promote healing.

Outcomes: After six months of treatment, the patient experienced a significant reduction in bullous lesions and erosions (Figure 3).
