Title: A Rare Case of Recurrent Angioleiomyoma: Diagnostic and Management Challenges

Abstract:

We present an unusual case of a 48-year-old female patient who presented with recurrent, painful subcutaneous nodules over a period of eight years. The initial diagnosis was misclassified as lipoma due to the similarity in clinical presentation. However, upon excisional biopsy, histopathological examination revealed the lesions to be Angioleiomyomas, a rare type of smooth muscle tumor.

The patient initially presented with a solitary nodule on her right forearm, which was misdiagnosed as lipoma and managed conservatively. Over the next eight years, multiple similar lesions appeared on various parts of her body, causing significant cosmetic disfigurement and occasional pain. The recurrence and unusual location of these lesions prompted a re-evaluation, leading to the correct diagnosis.

The patient underwent complete surgical excision of all visible lesions under local anesthesia. Postoperative recovery was uneventful. Given the rarity and potential for recurrence of Angioleiomyomas, regular follow-ups are recommended along with prompt intervention for any new or recurrent lesions.

This case underscores the importance of a thorough differential diagnosis, especially for uncommon conditions that can mimic more common ones. It also highlights the need for surgical excision as the most effective management strategy for Angioleiomyomas, particularly in cases of recurrence and cosmetically bothersome lesions.

Keywords: Angioleiomyoma, Smooth muscle tumor, Recurrent, Diagnosis, Management, Surgical excision
