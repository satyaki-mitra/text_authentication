Title: A Case Study on Successful Management of Refractory Angioedema in a Pediatric Patient Utilizing Ecallantide

Abstract

Background: Hereditary angioedema (HAE) is a rare autosomal dominant disorder characterized by recurrent, non-pruritic, and life-threatening swellings of the skin and mucosa. Type III HAE (HAE-III), caused by C1 inhibitor deficiency or dysfunction, accounts for approximately 30% of all cases. This case study presents an uncommon presentation and effective treatment approach in a pediatric patient with refractory angioedema.

Case Presentation: A 12-year-old female presented to the emergency department (ED) with acute onset facial edema, abdominal pain, and difficulty breathing, persisting for over 48 hours despite self-administered antihistamines, corticosteroids, and tranexamic acid. Physical examination revealed bilateral periorbital edema, tongue swelling, and diffuse abdominal tenderness. Laboratory findings included normal complete blood count, comprehensive metabolic panel, and C3/C4 levels, but low C1 inhibitor (C1-INH) levels (0.06 mg/dL; reference range: 0.12-0.45 mg/dL). The patient's symptoms were consistent with a diagnosis of HAE-III.

Diagnostic Approach: Given the patient's history of recurrent angioedema attacks and low C1-INH levels, a definitive diagnosis of HAE-III was made based on clinical presentation and laboratory results. A genetic test for C1-INH gene mutations was recommended but declined by the patient's family due to concerns about insurance coverage and potential psychological impact.

Treatment: Given the refractory nature of her symptoms, the patient was treated with ecallantide, a plasma kallikrein inhibitor approved for acute HAE attacks in patients 12 years and older. Following intravenous administration of ecallantide (30 mg), her facial edema resolved within 4 hours, and abdominal symptoms improved over the subsequent 24 hours.

Outcomes: The patient was discharged home on oral corticosteroids and prophylactic danazol. She experienced no further attacks during a 6-month follow-up period while adhering to her medication regimen. Notably, ecallantide was well tolerated with only mild injection site reactions and no anaphylaxis or other significant adverse events reported.
