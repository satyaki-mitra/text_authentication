Title: A Case Study on Successful Management of Acute Myeloid Leukemia in an Elderly Patient: An Unusual Response to FLAG-IDA Chemotherapy Regimen

Abstract

Acute Myeloid Leukemia (AML) is a hematological malignancy characterized by the rapid proliferation of abnormal myeloblasts, impairing normal hematopoiesis. The incidence of AML increases significantly with age, and optimal treatment strategies for elderly patients remain a challenge due to their increased susceptibility to chemotherapy-related toxicities. This case report highlights an unusual response to the FLAG-IDA (Fludarabine, Cytarabine, Idarubicin, and Ara-C) chemotherapy regimen in an 80-year-old female patient with previously untreated AML.

Case Presentation:

An 80-year-old Caucasian woman presented to the emergency department with a two-week history of fatigue, recurrent episodes of respiratory infections, and easy bruising. Physical examination revealed pallor, ecchymosis, and splenomegaly. Laboratory investigations revealed leukocytosis (white blood cell count: 45.6 x10^9/L), anemia (hemoglobin: 7.8 g/dL), and thrombocytopenia (platelet count: 30 x10^9/L). Bone marrow aspiration and biopsy confirmed AML with a high blast percentage (85%). Cytogenetic analysis revealed the presence of t(8;21)(q22;q22), a favorable prognostic marker.

Diagnostic Approach:

The patient was initially evaluated for infection due to her clinical presentation. However, laboratory results indicated a hematological malignancy. Further evaluation included complete blood count, peripheral blood smear, bone marrow aspiration and biopsy, cytogenetic analysis, and flow cytometry. The patient was subsequently diagnosed with AML based on these findings.

Treatment:

Given the patient's advanced age and comorbidities (hypertension, diabetes mellitus, and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease), a standard induction therapy with an anthracycline-based regimen was deemed high-risk.
