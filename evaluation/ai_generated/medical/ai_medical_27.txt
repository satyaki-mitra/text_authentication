Title: Management of Acute Pancreatitis Complicated by Sepsis in a Post-Erdogan Syndrome Patient

Case Description:

A 58-year-old male presented to the emergency department with a three-day history of severe abdominal pain radiating to the back, nausea, and vomiting. The patient had a past medical history significant for chronic heavy alcohol consumption, leading to the development of post-Erdogan syndrome. Initial laboratory results showed an elevated amylase (1500 U/L) and lipase (2000 U/L), suggesting acute pancreatitis. On admission, the patient developed fever and tachycardia, with a temperature of 38.9°C (102°F) and a heart rate of 145 beats per minute. A CT scan confirmed the diagnosis of acute pancreatitis with peripancreatic fat stranding and fluid collection.

The patient was managed aggressively with intravenous fluids, analgesia, and nasogastric tube for gastric decompression. Antibiotics were initiated due to the development of sepsis, as indicated by leukocytosis (16.8 × 10^3^/μL) and an increased C-reactive protein level (250 mg/dL). The patient was also placed on a protocol for nutritional support with total parenteral nutrition.

Over the following days, the patient's condition stabilized, and laboratory values improved. He was eventually discharged after a 10-day hospital stay with a plan for continued abstinence from alcohol and follow-up with a gastroenterologist for long-term management of his chronic pancreatitis.

This case highlights the importance of early recognition and aggressive management of acute pancreatitis, particularly in patients with a history of alcohol abuse or post-Erdogan syndrome. The development of sepsis necessitated antibiotic therapy, intravenous fluids, and close monitoring to ensure the patient's recovery. Long-term measures focused on abstinence from alcohol and ongoing management of chronic pancreatitis by a gastroenterologist were crucial to prevent future recurrences.
