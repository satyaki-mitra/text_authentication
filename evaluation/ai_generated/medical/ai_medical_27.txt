Title: A Case Study on the Successful Management of Severe Acute Pancreatitis Complicated by Sepsis in a Pediatric Patient

Abstract

Background: Acute pancreatitis (AP) is an inflammatory condition of the pancreas, with pediatric cases accounting for approximately 5-10% of all AP incidents. Severe acute pancreatitis (SAP) can lead to systemic complications, such as sepsis, necessitating aggressive and timely intervention. This case report presents a successful management approach in a pediatric patient with SAP complicated by sepsis.

Case Presentation: A 10-year-old male presented to the emergency department (ED) with a three-day history of persistent abdominal pain, vomiting, and fever. The patient's medical history was significant for no prior illnesses. Initial laboratory results revealed leukocytosis (white blood cell count: 18.5 x 10^3/μL), elevated amylase (276 U/L) and lipase (930 U/L), and a C-reactive protein level of 250 mg/dL, suggestive of AP. A bedside ultrasound confirmed the presence of peripancreatic fluid collections, supporting a diagnosis of SAP. The patient subsequently developed signs of sepsis, including hypotension and tachycardia, necessitating intensive care unit (ICU) admission.

Diagnostic Approach: Upon ICU admission, further investigations were initiated to identify the source of sepsis. Blood, urine, and abdominal fluid cultures were obtained, with empiric broad-spectrum antibiotics commenced based on local guidelines. Computed tomography (CT) scan of the abdomen confirmed SAP with signs of necrotizing pancreatitis and peripancreatic fluid collections. Magnetic resonance cholangiopancreatography (MRCP) was performed to evaluate for associated biliary pathology, revealing a normal biliary tree.

Treatment: The patient received supportive care in the ICU, including intravenous fluids, pain management, and nutritional support via a nasogastric tube. The antibiotic regimen was modified based on culture results, which revealed Enterococcus faecium bacteremia. The patient underwent two sessions of percutaneous drainage of the peripancreatic fluid collections to address the source of infection and facilitate resolution of sepsis.
