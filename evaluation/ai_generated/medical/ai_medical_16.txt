Title: A Rare Case of Refractory Pemphigus Foliaceus with Successful Treatment using Intravenous Immune Globulin (IVIG)

Abstract:

We present an unusual case of a 54-year-old Caucasian female who was admitted to our dermatology department due to an extensive, treatment-resistant pemphigus foliaceus (PF) rash over her entire body. The patient's medical history included a recent diagnosis of Graves' disease and a subsequent remission following radioactive iodine therapy three months prior.

Upon admission, the patient presented with widespread erythematous papules, vesicles, and bullae, accompanied by scaling and crusting lesions on her face, trunk, and extremities. Skin biopsy results confirmed a diagnosis of PF, as demonstrated by acantholysis in suprabasal epidermis with neutrophilic infiltration.

Initially, treatment consisted of high-dose oral corticosteroids (prednisone 60 mg daily) and azathioprine (150 mg daily). Despite intensive therapy over a period of two months, the patient's condition worsened, with new lesions emerging and a significant increase in itching and pain.

Given the refractory nature of the disease, we initiated an intravenous immune globulin (IVIG) regimen at 2 g/kg over five days. Following the first IVIG infusion, the patient reported immediate relief from itching and a reduction in new lesions' appearance. Over the subsequent weeks, the patient's condition continued to improve, with complete resolution of symptoms observed after four IVIG treatments (every three weeks).

To our knowledge, this is one of the few reported cases of PF successfully treated with IVIG, particularly in a patient with a concurrent autoimmune disorder. This case highlights the importance of considering alternative treatment options for refractory PF and emphasizes the potential benefits of IVIG therapy in managing this debilitating disease.

Keywords: Pemphigus Foliaceus, Refractory, Intravenous Immune Globulin (IVIG), Autoimmune Disorder, Treatment Resistance
