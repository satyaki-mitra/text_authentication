Title: A Case Study on Successful Management of Severe Acute Pancreatitis Complicated by Sepsis in a Pediatric Patient

Abstract

Background: Severe acute pancreatitis (SAP) is a life-threatening condition, particularly in pediatric patients due to their susceptibility to rapid progression and complications. Early recognition, aggressive management, and timely intervention are crucial for favorable outcomes.

Case Presentation: A 10-year-old male patient presented to the emergency department with a three-day history of persistent abdominal pain, vomiting, and fever. The patient's medical history was unremarkable except for a recent gallstone diagnosed two weeks ago but not yet treated due to COVID-19-related hospital delays. On examination, the patient appeared acutely ill with a temperature of 38.5°C, tachycardia (heart rate: 140 bpm), and abdominal tenderness with guarding and rebound tenderness. Laboratory investigations revealed leukocytosis (white blood cell count: 18,000 cells/mm³) and elevated serum amylase (1,500 U/L) and lipase (2,000 U/L). The patient was diagnosed with SAP based on Atlanta Criteria. Further workup revealed sepsis secondary to cholangitis due to obstructed common bile duct caused by the gallstone.

Diagnostic Approach: Initial imaging included abdominal ultrasound and computed tomography (CT) scan, which confirmed pancreatitis with signs of necrosis and cholelithiasis with common bile duct obstruction. Magnetic resonance cholangiopancreatography (MRCP) was performed to further assess the biliary tree.

Treatment: The patient was immediately managed in the pediatric intensive care unit (PICU). Supportive care included fluid resuscitation, pain management, and nutritional support. Antibiotics were initiated based on sepsis workup results. Endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP) was performed for bile duct decompression and stone extraction.

Outcomes: The patient's condition stabilized following ERCP and antibiotic therapy. The patient remained in the PICU for a week before being transferred to the general ward. The patient made a full recovery with no residual complications.
