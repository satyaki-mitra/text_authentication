Title: A Novel Case of Acute Intermittent Porphyria Presenting as Abdominal Crisis: A Diagnostic Challenge and Management Approach

Abstract

Acute Intermittent Porphyria (AIP) is a rare, autosomal dominant metabolic disorder characterized by neurovisceral attacks. This case report aims to highlight the diagnostic challenges and management strategies in a 45-year-old female patient who presented with an acute abdomen, leading to a delayed diagnosis of AIP.

Case Presentation:

The patient, a 45-year-old Caucasian female, presented to the emergency department with severe abdominal pain, nausea, vomiting, constipation, and peripheral neuropathy for three days. She reported no history of similar episodes but had a family history of unexplained ailments and premature deaths. On physical examination, she was afebrile with tachycardia (120 bpm) and hypertension (180/100 mmHg). Abdominal exam revealed diffuse tenderness and guarding without rebound or rigidity. Initial laboratory investigations showed leukocytosis (WBC 15.6 x 10^3/μL), elevated CRP (12 mg/L), and normocytic anemia (Hb 9 g/dL). Abdominal CT scan revealed multiple appendiceal wall thickenings suggestive of appendicitis, leading to an initial laparoscopic appendectomy. However, post-operative course was complicated by persistent abdominal pain, recurrent vomiting, and worsening neuropathy.

Diagnostic Approach:

Given the atypical presentation and poor response to initial treatment, further investigations were pursued. Urine porphyrins analysis revealed increased delta-aminolevulinic acid (ALA) and porphobilinogen (PBG), leading to a suspicion of porphyria. Fecal porphyrins analysis confirmed the diagnosis of AIP. Genetic testing revealed a mutation in the gene encoding hydroxymethylbilane synthase (HMBS).

Treatment and Outcomes:

The patient was managed with hematin therapy (intravenous iron sucrose) and glucose loading, followed by carbohydrate-restricted diet. Symptomatic treatment for neuropathy included magnesium sulfate and analgesics. The patient showed significant improvement within a week, with resolution of abdominal pain, normalization of hematological parameters, and recovery from neuropathy.
