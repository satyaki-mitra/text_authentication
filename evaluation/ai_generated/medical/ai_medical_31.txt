Title: A Case Study on Successful Management of Severe Acute Pancreatitis with Early Endoscopic Retrograde Cholangiopancreatography (ERCP)

Abstract

Background: Acute pancreatitis (AP) is a common inflammatory condition characterized by acinar cell injury and autodigestion of the pancreas. Severe acute pancreatitis (SAP) accounts for approximately 20% of all cases, with a high mortality rate if not promptly diagnosed and managed effectively.

Case Presentation: A 58-year-old male patient presented to the emergency department with severe epigastric abdominal pain radiating to his back, accompanied by nausea, vomiting, and fever for three days. Laboratory findings revealed an elevated serum amylase (1200 U/L; normal range: 25-140 U/L) and lipase (780 U/L; normal range: 13-60 U/L). The patient's white blood cell count was 16.5 x 10³/μL (normal range: 4.5–11.0 x 10³/μL), C-reactive protein (CRP) level was 238 mg/L (normal range: <5 mg/L), and aspartate transaminase (AST) and alanine transaminase (ALT) were elevated at 164 U/L (normal range: 10–40 U/L) and 229 U/L (normal range: 8–50 U/L), respectively. Ultrasound showed a swollen pancreas with peripancreatic fluid collection, suggestive of SAP. The patient was immediately admitted to the intensive care unit for close monitoring and aggressive supportive management.

Diagnostic Approach: Given the clinical presentation and laboratory findings, the diagnosis of SAP was confirmed. To further assess the etiology and identify potential complications, computed tomography (CT) scan with contrast enhancement and magnetic resonance cholangiopancreatography (MRCP) were performed. The CT scan revealed pancreatic necrosis and peripancreatic fluid collections consistent with SAP, while MRCP demonstrated a common bile duct stone as the potential causative factor.
