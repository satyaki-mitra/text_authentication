Title: A Case Study of Successful Treatment of Refractory Giant Cell Tumor of the Tendon Sheath with Denosumab

Abstract

Background: Giant cell tumors (GCTs) are uncommon, aggressive benign skeletal neoplasms that predominantly affect the epiphysis of long bones in young adults. Giant cell tumor of the tendon sheath (GCT-TS) is a rare subtype arising from synovial tissue, accounting for 1-6% of all soft tissue sarcomas. Refractory GCT-TS cases pose significant challenges due to their propensity for recurrence and limited treatment options. This case report presents the successful treatment of refractory GCT-TS with Denosumab, a monoclonal antibody targeting RANKL (Receptor Activator of Nuclear Factor Kappa-B Ligand).

Case Presentation: A 50-year-old female presented with a three-year history of progressive swelling and pain in her left wrist. Clinical examination revealed a tender, firm, warm mass on the dorsal aspect of the wrist. Imaging studies, including X-rays, MRI, and CT scans, confirmed a large GCT-TS involving the extensor tendon sheaths of the third and fourth fingers. Despite two surgical excisions followed by adjuvant radiotherapy, the patient experienced recurrence, necessitating an alternative treatment approach.

Diagnostic Approach: Given the refractory nature of the tumor, a multidisciplinary team was consulted to discuss potential treatment options. After reviewing the literature, Denosumab was chosen due to its demonstrated efficacy in preventing bone resorption and inhibiting GCT growth. The patient provided informed consent for Denosumab therapy.

Treatment: The patient received a loading dose of 120 mg Denosumab followed by monthly maintenance doses of 60 mg. After three months, a significant reduction in tumor size was observed, and the patient reported substantial pain relief. The treatment continued for six months with no signs of recurrence or adverse effects.

Outcomes: At the twelve-month follow-up, the patient showed no evidence of disease. The wrist had returned to near-normal function, allowing her to resume daily activities without significant discomfort. This case demonstrates the potential utility of Denosumab in treating refractory GCT-TS cases, offering a promising alternative for patients with limited options.
