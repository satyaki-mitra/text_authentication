Title: An Unusual Case of Pneumocystis jirovecii Pneumonia in an Immunocompetent Individual

Abstract:

We present an intriguing and rare case of Pneumocystis jirovecii pneumonia (PJP) in a 45-year-old otherwise healthy, non-smoking female with no known underlying immunodeficiency conditions. The patient presented with a two-week history of progressive dry cough, fever, and fatigue. A chest X-ray revealed bilateral interstitial and alveolar infiltrates, suggesting an atypical pneumonia.

Initial workup, including complete blood count (CBC), comprehensive metabolic panel (CMP), and sputum culture, were unremarkable. Due to the atypical presentation, a bronchoalveolar lavage (BAL) was performed which revealed numerous cysts suggestive of Pneumocystis jirovecii. The diagnosis was further confirmed by PCR analysis from BAL fluid.

The patient was immediately initiated on high-dose trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole (TMP-SMX) therapy, with a rapid improvement in symptoms and resolution of radiographic abnormalities within two weeks.

This case highlights the importance of considering PJP in the differential diagnosis of atypical pneumonia, even in immunocompetent individuals. It underscores the need for broad-spectrum diagnostic testing, particularly when the clinical presentation is not consistent with the known risk factors for PJP.

Keywords: Pneumocystis jirovecii, Pneumocystis jirovecii pneumonia, atypical pneumonia, immunocompetent individual, trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole.
