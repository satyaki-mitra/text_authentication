Title: A Case Study on Successful Management of Severe Acute Pancreatitis in a Pediatric Patient

Abstract

Background: Acute pancreatitis (AP) is an inflammatory condition of the pancreas, often caused by gallstones or alcohol abuse. In pediatric patients, AP is relatively uncommon but can have severe complications if not properly managed. This case report presents a successful management approach to a rare case of severe acute pancreatitis in a 12-year-old patient with no known risk factors.

Case Presentation: A previously healthy 12-year-old male presented to the emergency department with sudden onset of severe abdominal pain, vomiting, and fever. Physical examination revealed tender abdomen with localized guarding in the epigastric region. Laboratory tests showed elevated serum amylase (1500 U/L) and lipase (2000 U/L), suggesting acute pancreatitis. Imaging studies, including ultrasound, computed tomography (CT), and magnetic resonance cholangiopancreatography (MRCP), were performed to identify the cause and severity of the condition. The CT scan revealed a diffuse enlargement of the pancreas with peripancreatic fat stranding, consistent with necrotizing acute pancreatitis.

Diagnostic Approach: Given the patient's severe symptoms and imaging findings, the diagnosis of severe acute pancreatitis was confirmed. The etiology remained unclear due to the absence of known risk factors. Further investigations including serum triglycerides, autoimmune markers, and genetic testing were performed but showed no abnormalities.

Treatment: The patient was immediately admitted to the pediatric intensive care unit for supportive care and management of complications. Intravenous fluids were administered to maintain adequate hydration and electrolyte balance. Nutritional support was initiated with total parenteral nutrition (TPN) due to the inability to tolerate enteral feeds. Pain management included intravenous opioids and non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs). Enzyme replacement therapy with oral pancreatic enzymes was started to reduce pancreatic secretion and prevent further damage.
