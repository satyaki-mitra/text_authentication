Title: An Uncommon Case of Immunoglobulin G4-Related Sclerosing Orchitis: A Diagnostic Challenge

Abstract

We present an unusual case of a 56-year-old male patient who presented with acute onset of scrotal pain and swelling. The patient had no significant past medical history, and his physical examination revealed a firm, non-tender, right testicular mass. Laboratory findings were unremarkable, including normal tumor markers. Ultrasound imaging demonstrated a hypoechoic lesion within the right testis.

Given the atypical presentation and inconclusive initial workup, further investigations were pursued. Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) revealed a homogeneously enhancing mass with surrounding edema, suggestive of an inflammatory process. A diagnosis of immunoglobulin G4-related sclerosing orchitis was considered due to the patient's absence of infectious symptoms and the characteristic MRI findings.

Serum IgG4 levels were significantly elevated (207 mg/dL, normal range: 10-135 mg/dL). The diagnosis was confirmed with a testicular biopsy revealing lymphoplasmacytic infiltration and abundant IgG4-positive plasma cells.

The patient was treated with glucocorticoids, resulting in rapid resolution of symptoms and reduction in testicular size within two weeks. This case highlights the importance of considering immunoglobulin G4-related diseases in the differential diagnosis of scrotal masses, even in the absence of systemic symptoms.

Keywords: Immunoglobulin G4-Related Disease, Sclerosing Orchitis, Scrotal Mass, Diagnostic Challenge, Glucocorticoids Treatment.
