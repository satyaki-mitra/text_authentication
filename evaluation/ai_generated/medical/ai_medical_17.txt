Title: A Case Study on the Successful Management of Acute Pancreatitis in a Postoperative Gastric Bypass Patient

Abstract

Background: Acute pancreatitis (AP) is a common inflammatory condition of the pancreas, often occurring post-gastric bypass surgery. Early recognition and appropriate management are crucial to prevent complications and improve outcomes.

Case Presentation: A 58-year-old female with a BMI of 37.2 kg/m² presented to the emergency department (ED) with acute abdominal pain, nausea, and vomiting three days post-gastric bypass surgery. The patient had no history of pancreatitis or significant comorbidities other than morbid obesity. On admission, laboratory tests revealed elevated serum amylase (130 IU/L; normal range: 25-96 IU/L) and lipase (784 U/L; normal range: 13-60 U/L). Abdominal ultrasound showed a heterogeneous pancreas with peripancreatic fluid collection, suggestive of acute pancreatitis.

Diagnostic Approach: The diagnosis was confirmed with magnetic resonance cholangiopancreatography (MRCP), which revealed mild dilatation of the pancreatic duct and peripancreatic fat stranding, consistent with AP. The etiology was attributed to post-gastric bypass surgery due to early onset and resolution of symptoms after surgery.

Treatment: The patient was managed conservatively in the intensive care unit (ICU) with fluid resuscitation, pain management, and nutritional support. Octreotide was initiated to control pancreatitis-induced secretory diarrhea. Antibiotics were administered for suspected secondary infection, based on the presence of leukocytosis (12.5 × 10^9/L; normal range: 4.0-11.0 × 10^9/L) and elevated C-reactive protein (CRP) level (172 mg/L; normal range: <5 mg/L).

Outcomes: The patient's abdominal pain improved significantly within 48 hours of initiating treatment. Serum amylase and lipase levels normalized on the fifth day, and CRP level decreased to 60 mg/L by the seventh day. The patient was discharged from the hospital on the tenth day with oral pancreatic enzyme supplementation and a follow-up appointment for further management.
