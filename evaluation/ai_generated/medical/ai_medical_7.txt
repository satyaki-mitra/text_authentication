Title: A Case Study of Successful Treatment of Refractory Primary Sjogren's Syndrome with Rituximab

Abstract

Background: Primary Sjogren's syndrome (pSS) is an autoimmune disorder characterized by lymphocytic infiltration of the exocrine glands, leading to dryness of the eyes and mouth. Refractory pSS, defined as a lack of response to standard therapy, poses significant challenges for clinicians. This case report highlights the successful treatment of refractory pSS with Rituximab, a chimeric monoclonal antibody targeting CD20-positive B cells.

Case Presentation: A 58-year-old female presented with symptoms consistent with pSS, including xerostomia, keratoconjunctivitis sicca, arthralgias, and fatigue of three-year duration. The patient had failed multiple lines of therapy, including hydroxychloroquine, azathioprine, mycophenolate mofetil, and prednisone. Laboratory results showed positive anti-SSA antibodies, low Schirmer's test scores, and decreased unstimulated and stimulated whole saliva flow rates, confirming the diagnosis of pSS.

Diagnostic Approach: The patient underwent a comprehensive evaluation to determine the extent of her disease activity and assess the need for alternative treatment options. This included a complete blood count, comprehensive metabolic panel, erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR), C-reactive protein (CRP), anti-double stranded DNA antibodies, complement levels, antinuclear antibody (ANA) titer, and rheumatoid factor. Imaging studies such as magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) of the salivary glands and ultrasound were also performed to evaluate glandular involvement.

Treatment: Given the refractory nature of the patient's pSS, a decision was made to initiate treatment with Rituximab. The patient received four infusions of 1000 mg each, administered two weeks apart.

Outcomes: Following the initiation of Rituximab therapy, the patient reported significant improvements in her symptoms. Laboratory tests showed decreased ESR and CRP levels, normalization of complement levels, and a reduction in ANA titer. The Schirmer's test scores increased, and both unstimulated and stimulated whole saliva flow rates improved significantly. MRI and ultrasound imaging revealed a marked decrease in the extent of lymphocytic infiltration in the salivary glands.
