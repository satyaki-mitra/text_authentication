Title: A Case Study on Successful Treatment of Severe Acute Pancreatitis with Early Intervention and Nutrition Support

Abstract

Background: Acute pancreatitis (AP) is a common gastrointestinal disorder, often caused by gallstones or alcohol abuse. The disease spectrum ranges from mild, self-limiting cases to severe, life-threatening illnesses requiring intensive care management. Early diagnosis and appropriate treatment are crucial for favorable outcomes.

Case Presentation: A 58-year-old male presented to the emergency department with severe abdominal pain, nausea, and vomiting. The patient reported a history of chronic alcohol abuse but denied any gallstones or family history of pancreatic diseases. Physical examination revealed a tender, distended abdomen, and laboratory tests showed elevated serum amylase (1200 U/L) and lipase (800 U/L), confirming the diagnosis of acute pancreatitis. The patient was classified as severe acute pancreatitis (SAP) due to persistent abdominal pain, hypotension, and elevated C-reactive protein (CRP).

Diagnostic Approach: Computed tomography (CT) scan revealed a dilated pancreatic duct without evidence of necrosis. Serum calcium, glucose, and electrolytes were normal, while lactic acid was slightly elevated at 2.3 mmol/L. The patient was managed in the intensive care unit with fluid resuscitation, analgesia, and antibiotics for presumed infection.

Treatment: Early enteral nutrition (EN) was initiated via nasojejunal tube due to concerns about gut ischemia. Intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG) was administered to reduce the risk of pancreatic autoimmune attacks and modulate the inflammatory response. The patient received prophylactic enoxaparin for venous thromboembolism and was closely monitored for infection, renal, and respiratory complications.

Outcomes: Over a week, the patient's abdominal pain subsided, blood tests showed improvement (amylase 300 U/L, lipase 200 U/L), and CRP decreased from 150 mg/L to 70 mg/L. The patient was gradually weaned off analgesia and antibiotics, and the nasojejunal tube was removed after confirming oral intake tolerance. The patient was discharged home in good condition, with a follow-up appointment scheduled for monitoring pancreatic function.
