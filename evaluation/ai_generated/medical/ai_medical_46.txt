Title: A Case Study on Successful Management of Refractory Gouty Arthritis with Allopurinol in a Recalcitrant Patient

Abstract

Background: Gout is a common form of inflammatory arthritis characterized by recurrent episodes of acute arthritis, often in the first metatarsophalangeal joint. The management of gout involves both symptomatic and urate-lowering therapy (UL Therapy). This case report presents an unusual instance of refractory gouty arthritis managed successfully with Allopurinol in a recalcitrant patient, who had shown resistance to other UL therapies.

Case Presentation: A 65-year-old male presented with chronic, recurrent attacks of gouty arthritis, despite being on a maximum tolerated dose of febuxostat for over two years. The patient's medical history was significant for hypertension, hyperlipidemia, and type 2 diabetes mellitus. He had experienced multiple hospitalizations due to persistent gout flares, each lasting an average of four weeks. Physical examination revealed swelling, tenderness, and erythema in the right first metatarsophalangeal joint. Laboratory results demonstrated serum uric acid levels of 10.8 mg/dL (normal range: 3.4-7.2 mg/dL), negative rheumatoid factor, and normal C-reactive protein (CRP) level. Plain radiographs revealed chronic tophaceous gouty arthropathy.

Diagnostic Approach: The patient's history of recurrent acute gouty arthritis attacks coupled with the laboratory findings and radiographic evidence supported a diagnosis of refractory gouty arthritis.

Treatment: Given the patient's resistance to febuxostat, it was decided to initiate Allopurinol therapy at a dose of 300 mg daily. The initial dosage was increased gradually to minimize the risk of allergic reactions or other adverse effects. Simultaneously, colchicine and non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) were used for symptomatic relief during gout flares.

Outcomes: After three months on Allopurinol therapy, the patient reported a significant reduction in the frequency and severity of gout attacks. The serum uric acid level was consistently maintained below 6 mg/dL. Over the following year, the patient experienced no further hospitalizations due to gouty arthritis, with only occasional mild flares requiring brief courses of colchicine and NSAIDs.
