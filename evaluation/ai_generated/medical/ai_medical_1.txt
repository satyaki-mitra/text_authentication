Title: An Uncommon Case of Pemphigus Foliaceus with Atypical Presentations

Abstract:

We present an intriguing case study of a 58-year-old Caucasian male who presented to our dermatology clinic with a three-week history of multiple, itchy, and erythematous papules on the scalp that rapidly progressed to large, flaccid bullae. The lesions were initially misdiagnosed as impetigo due to their superficial location and similar appearance.

Upon further examination, the patient exhibited widespread desquamation, particularly over the face, trunk, and extremities, which is an atypical presentation of pemphigus foliaceus (PF). PF is a rare autoimmune blistering disorder characterized by the production of IgG autoantibodies against desmoglein 1 in the stratum spinosum. The diagnosis was confirmed by direct immunofluorescence (DIF) and indirect immunofluorescence (IIF), which demonstrated intercellular IgG deposition on the epidermis.

The patient was initiated on systemic corticosteroids, which led to a significant improvement in his symptoms within two weeks. Subsequent tapering of the dosage was performed over six months under close dermatological supervision to minimize potential side effects. This case serves as a reminder that PF can present atypically and emphasizes the importance of DIF and IIF in confirming the diagnosis, particularly in cases where the clinical presentation is not straightforward.

Keywords: Pemphigus Foliaceus, Autoimmune Blistering Disorders, Direct Immunofluorescence, Indirect Immunofluorescence, Systemic Corticosteroids.
