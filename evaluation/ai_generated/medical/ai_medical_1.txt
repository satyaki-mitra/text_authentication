Title: A Case Study on Successful Management of Refractory Gouty Arthritis with Allopurinol and Colchicine Combination Therapy

Abstract

Background: Recalcitrant gout, characterized by frequent and severe acute attacks, is a challenging condition that necessitates an effective therapeutic approach. This case report demonstrates the successful management of refractory gouty arthritis in a 65-year-old male patient using a combination therapy of Allopurinol and Colchicine.

Case Presentation: The patient, with a 20-year history of gout, presented with recurrent, debilitating attacks affecting multiple joints over the past year despite maximally tolerated doses of colchicine and nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs). His medical history included hypertension, diabetes mellitus type 2, and chronic kidney disease stage III. Laboratory investigations revealed serum uric acid level of 9.5 mg/dL, creatinine of 1.8 mg/dL, and white blood cell count of 12.5 x 10^3/μL. Radiographic imaging showed erosive changes in the right first metatarsophalangeal joint indicative of chronic gouty arthritis.

Diagnostic Approach: The patient was diagnosed with refractory gout based on the presence of recurrent acute attacks, tophus formation, and elevated serum uric acid levels despite aggressive pharmacological treatment.

Treatment: Upon admission, the patient's symptoms were managed with a short-term course of high-dose corticosteroids. Subsequently, Allopurinol was initiated at a dose of 100 mg daily and gradually increased to 300 mg in divided doses over two weeks. Concurrently, Colchicine was administered at an initial dose of 1.2 mg twice daily, later adjusted based on the patient's response and tolerability.

Outcomes: After four weeks of Allopurinol and Colchicine combination therapy, the patient reported a significant reduction in the frequency and severity of gout attacks. Serum uric acid level decreased from 9.5 mg/dL to 6.2 mg/dL, and the patient was able to discontinue corticosteroids. After three months, the patient remained symptom-free with no further gout attacks, and serum uric acid levels were maintained below 6.5 mg/dL.
