Title: An Unusual Case of Recurrent Pneumomediastinato-pulmonary Fistula Post-Esophageal Perforation

Abstract:

We present an extraordinary case report of a 58-year-old male patient, a non-smoker and with no significant past medical history, who presented with acute onset of chest pain and shortness of breath. The patient was initially misdiagnosed with pneumonia due to his initial symptoms and radiographic findings. However, further investigations revealed an esophageal perforation caused by a retropharyngeal abscess.

The patient underwent emergency esophagectomy with gastric pull-up reconstruction. Postoperatively, the patient developed recurrent pneumomediastinum and persistent air leak from the left lower lobe of the lung, suggesting the presence of a pneumomediastinato-pulmonary fistula (PMPF). The PMPF was managed conservatively with chest tube drainage and oxygen therapy.

Despite the initial successful resolution of the PMPF, the patient experienced another episode of chest pain and shortness of breath four months post-surgery. Repeat computed tomography (CT) scan confirmed recurrent pneumomediastinum and a new PMPF from the right lower lobe of the lung. The patient underwent successful thoracotomy with repair of the fistula, followed by chest tube drainage and pleurodesis.

This case highlights the rare but potentially life-threatening complication of esophageal perforation leading to recurrent PMPF, underscoring the importance of prompt recognition, appropriate management, and careful follow-up in such cases. The successful resolution of this challenging case emphasizes the role of surgical intervention in managing complex air leak complications.

Keywords: Esophageal Perforation, Pneumomediastinum, Pneumomediastinato-Pulmonary Fistula, Recurrent Air Leak, Thoracotomy, Esophagectomy
