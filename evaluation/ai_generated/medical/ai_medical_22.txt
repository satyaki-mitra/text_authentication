Title: A Case Study on Successful Management of Severe Acute Pancreatitis Using Early Prophylactic ERCP and Novel Antibiotic Regimen

Abstract

Background: Severe acute pancreatitis (SAP) is a life-threatening condition characterized by systemic inflammatory response syndrome, organ failure, and high morbidity and mortality rates. Early endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP) and appropriate antibiotic therapy are crucial for managing SAP. This case study presents an unusual instance of successful management of SAP using early prophylactic ERCP and a novel antibiotic regimen.

Case Presentation: A 58-year-old male, with no significant past medical history, presented to the emergency department with acute onset epigastric abdominal pain radiating to his back, nausea, and vomiting for two days. His initial laboratory results showed elevated serum amylase (1400 U/L; normal range: 25-130 U/L) and lipase (900 U/L; normal range: 13-60 U/L), white blood cell count (WBC) of 18,000 cells/mm³ (normal range: 4.5-11.0 cells/mm³), and C-reactive protein (CRP) of 200 mg/L (normal range: <5 mg/L). Computed tomography (CT) scan revealed a swollen pancreas with peripancreatic fat stranding, consistent with SAP.

Diagnostic Approach: Given the patient's clinical presentation and laboratory findings, a diagnosis of SAP was established. The patient underwent an urgent ERCP due to his severe symptoms, high CRP level, and concern for choledocholithiasis based on imaging.

Treatment: During ERCP, a common bile duct stone was identified and removed. The patient was initiated on a novel antibiotic regimen consisting of meropenem and metronidazole due to the potential for infectious complications in SAP. Supportive care including fluid resuscitation, pain management, and nutritional support were also provided.

Outcomes: The patient showed immediate improvement following ERCP with resolution of abdominal pain and decreased WBC count within 24 hours. His CRP level peaked at day three post-admission (350 mg/L) before gradually declining, and he was discharged on day seven with normalization of his laboratory values and no complications.
