Title: A Rare Case of Chronic Idiopathic Urticaria in a Pediatric Patient

Background: Chronic idiopathic urticaria (CIU) is an uncommon condition characterized by recurrent episodes of pruritus and wheals lasting more than six weeks without identifiable causes. It primarily affects adults, with fewer than 5% of cases reported in pediatric patients.

Case Presentation: A 12-year-old female presented to the clinic with a three-month history of daily pruritic hives and angioedema involving her face, limbs, and trunk. The symptoms were not associated with specific triggers or allergens. Physical examination revealed bilateral periorbital edema and multiple erythematous papules and wheels. Laboratory investigations, including complete blood count, liver function tests, thyroid function tests, and comprehensive metabolic panel, were within normal limits. Skin prick testing for common allergens was negative. A skin biopsy confirmed the diagnosis of CIU.

Intervention: The patient was treated with a daily oral antihistamine (cetirizine 10 mg) and intermittent short-term corticosteroids (prednisone 20 mg/day for five days every three weeks). After six months of treatment, the frequency and severity of symptoms significantly decreased.

Conclusion: This case underscores the importance of considering CIU in pediatric patients with persistent unexplained hives, even though it is relatively uncommon in this age group. Early recognition and appropriate management can significantly improve the quality of life for these patients. Further research is needed to understand the underlying mechanisms and develop more effective treatment strategies for CIU.
