Title: A Case Study on the Successful Management of Refractory Status Epilepticus in a Pediatric Patient Using High-Dose Levetiracetam

Abstract

Background: Refractory status epilepticus (RSE) is a life-threatening neurological emergency characterized by persistent, uncontrolled seizures despite adequate antiepileptic drug (AED) therapy. RSE poses significant challenges in pediatric patients due to their developing central nervous system and unique pharmacokinetics. This case report demonstrates the successful management of RSE in a 7-year-old male using high-dose levetiracetam.

Case Presentation: The patient, a previously healthy 7-year-old boy, presented to the emergency department with a 3-hour history of tonic-clonic seizures. Despite initial treatment with lorazepam and phenytoin, his seizures persisted, meeting criteria for RSE. Given his young age and continuous seizure activity, high-dose levetiracetam was initiated at 100 mg/kg per day, divided into three doses. The patient's seizure activity ceased within hours of the initial dose, and he was discharged home on a maintenance dose of 60 mg/kg per day.

Diagnostic Approach: Diagnosis of RSE was based on the International League Against Epilepsy (ILAE) criteria, which include continuous seizure activity for more than 5 minutes or two or more seizures without recovery of consciousness between events. Initial workup included a comprehensive metabolic panel, complete blood count, toxicology screen, and electroencephalogram (EEG). Brain imaging was deferred due to the patient's stable clinical status and response to treatment.

Treatment: The initial management involved benzodiazepines and phenytoin, which were ineffective in controlling seizures. High-dose levetiracetam was chosen for its rapid onset of action, minimal drug interactions, and favorable safety profile in pediatric patients.

Outcomes: The patient's seizure activity ceased within hours of the initial high-dose levetiracetam administration. He was discharged home with no residual neurological deficits and has remained seizure-free for over six months since the initial event, with gradual tapering of his AED regimen.
