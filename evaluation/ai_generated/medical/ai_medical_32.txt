Title: Unusual Presentation of Giant Cell Arteritis in a Young Adult: A Case Study

Background: Giant cell arteritis (GCA), also known as temporal arteritis, is a granulomatous vasculitis primarily affecting large and medium-sized arteries. It typically affects individuals over the age of 50, with a peak incidence in those over 70. The disease's presentation in younger adults is rare but can lead to severe complications if misdiagnosed or delayed in treatment.

Case Presentation: A 38-year-old Caucasian female presented to the emergency department with a 2-week history of progressive, right-sided headache, jaw claudication, and visual symptoms, including blurred vision, diplopia, and photophobia. The patient denied fever, weight loss, or any recent infections. Upon physical examination, there was tenderness over the temporal artery on the affected side. Laboratory results showed a C-reactive protein (CRP) level of 200 mg/L (normal range: <5 mg/L), and erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR) of 140 mm/h (normal range: <30 mm/h). A temporal artery biopsy was performed, confirming the diagnosis of GCA. The patient was immediately started on high-dose corticosteroids and closely monitored for potential complications.

Discussion: This case highlights the importance of considering Giant cell arteritis in patients with symptoms suggestive of temporal arteritis, even in younger age groups. The atypical presentation in this young patient underscores the need for a high index of suspicion and early diagnosis to prevent potential vision loss or other severe complications. Treatment with corticosteroids led to rapid improvement in the patient's symptoms.

Conclusion: This case report emphasizes that Giant cell arteritis can occur in younger individuals, and clinicians should consider this diagnosis in patients presenting with symptoms such as headache, jaw claudication, or visual disturbances, even when these symptoms are atypical for the usual patient demographic. Early recognition and appropriate treatment are crucial to minimize potential complications associated with GCA.
