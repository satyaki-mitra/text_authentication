Title: A Case Study on Successful Management of Severe Refractory Psoriasis through Ustekinumab Therapy

Abstract

Background: Psoriasis is a chronic immune-mediated inflammatory skin condition characterized by the rapid proliferation of keratinocytes, leading to the formation of thick, scaly, erythematous plaques. In this case study, we report the successful treatment of a refractory psoriasis patient with Ustekinumab, an interleukin (IL)-12/IL-23 inhibitor.

Case Presentation: A 48-year-old male presented with extensive plaque psoriasis involving 80% of his body surface area (BSA) for the past three years. The patient had previously been treated with topical corticosteroids, phototherapy, methotrexate, and adalimumab, all of which provided temporary relief but failed to achieve complete remission. The patient's quality of life was significantly impaired due to severe pruritus, pain, and social embarrassment. Physical examination revealed thick, erythematous, scaly plaques on the scalp, elbows, knees, and trunk, with nail psoriasis in several digits. Dermatology Life Quality Index (DLQI) score was 24 out of 30, indicating severe impairment.

Diagnostic Approach: The diagnosis of psoriasis was based on the characteristic clinical presentation, family history, and histopathological findings of parakeratosis, acanthosis, and Munro's microabscesses. Psoriasis Area and Severity Index (PASI) score was 46 out of 72, indicating severe psoriasis.

Treatment: Given the patient's refractory nature to conventional treatments, Ustekinumab therapy was initiated at a dose of 45 mg per kilogram body weight, administered subcutaneously every 12 weeks.

Outcomes: After two doses of Ustekinumab, there was a marked improvement in the patient's symptoms. By week 12, PASI score reduced to 10, and DLQI score dropped to 4. By week 24, PASI score further decreased to 2, and the patient reported significant relief from pruritus and pain. The patient remained in remission at the 52-week follow-up, with a PASI score of 1.
