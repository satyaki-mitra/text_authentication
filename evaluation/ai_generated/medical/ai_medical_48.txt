Title: A Case Study on Successful Management of Malignant Pleural Mesothelioma Using Immunotherapy: PD-L1 Inhibitor Pembrolizumab

Abstract

Background: Malignant pleural mesothelioma (MPM) is an aggressive malignancy primarily associated with asbestos exposure, carrying a dismal prognosis. Traditional treatment modalities offer limited efficacy and significant toxicity. The advent of immunotherapy provides a promising avenue for improved outcomes.

Case Presentation: A 65-year-old male smoker with no known asbestos exposure presented with progressive shortness of breath, chest pain, and a persistent dry cough of three months' duration. Physical examination revealed decreased breath sounds on the left side, and chest X-ray confirmed a left pleural effusion. Thoracentesis revealed an exudative pleural fluid with cytology positive for mesothelial malignant cells. Imaging studies showed a thickened visceral pleura and irregular consolidation of the lower left lobe consistent with MPM. Tissue biopsy confirmed epithelioid subtype MPM, and PD-L1 expression was 50%.

Diagnostic Approach: Given the patient's history, clinical presentation, and imaging findings, a diagnosis of MPM was made. Staging workup included a PET scan, which revealed no distant metastasis (stage IIIB). Genetic profiling showed no actionable mutations. The patient was deemed a suitable candidate for immunotherapy based on PD-L1 expression ≥50%.

Treatment: The patient was initiated on pembrolizumab at a dose of 200 mg every three weeks. After two cycles, the patient reported improvement in symptoms and a partial response was confirmed on follow-up imaging. Toxicity was minimal with no grade ≥3 adverse events reported.

Outcomes: The patient completed six cycles of pembrolizumab with ongoing disease control at one year (assessed by CT scan). The patient remains symptom-free and continues on maintenance therapy.

Discussion: This case highlights the successful management of MPM using immunotherapy, specifically pembrolizumab. While traditional treatments offer limited efficacy, PD-L1 inhibitors have shown promise in improving outcomes for patients with high PD-L1 expression. Further research is warranted to assess the long-term efficacy and optimal sequencing of immunotherapy with other treatment modalities in MPM management.

Keywords: Malignant pleural mesothelioma, Immunotherapy, Pembrolizumab, PD-L1 inhibitor, Case study
