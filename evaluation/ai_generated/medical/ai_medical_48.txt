Title: A Rare Case of Recurrent Mastocytosis with Systemic Manifestations and Successful Management

Case Presentation:

A 48-year-old Caucasian female, non-smoker, with no significant past medical history, presented to the emergency department (ED) for a third episode of acute abdominal pain, flushing, diarrhea, and shortness of breath over the past three months. The symptoms were recurrent, lasting for several hours each time, and had been initially misdiagnosed as gastrointestinal disorders.

On physical examination, the patient was alert but visibly flushed, with multiple urticarial lesions on her trunk and extremities. Upon palpation, a firm, mobile, and nontender mass was detected in the left supraclavicular fossa. Laboratory results showed an elevated serum tryptase level (30 ng/mL; reference range: 2-11 ng/mL), eosinophilia (2.5 x 10^9 cells/L; reference range: 0.2-0.6 x 10^9 cells/L), and anemia (Hb: 8.5 g/dL; reference range: 12-16 g/dL).

A bone marrow aspiration revealed a high percentage of mast cells (>25% of total nucleated cells) with spindle-shaped morphology and dense granules, confirming the diagnosis of systemic mastocytosis. The patient was subsequently diagnosed with Mastocytosis with Systemic Manifestations (SM-AHN) based on WHO 2016 criteria, due to the presence of the major criterion (demonstration of a clonal hematopoietic neoplasm by cytogenetic or molecular genetic studies) and one minor criterion (anemia).
