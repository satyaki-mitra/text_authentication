Title: A Case Study on Successful Management of Acute Pancreatitis with Surgical Intervention in a Critically Ill Patient

Abstract

Background: Acute pancreatitis (AP) is an inflammatory condition of the pancreas, often causing abdominal pain and systemic inflammation. Severe acute pancreatitis (SAP) can progress to organ failure and mortality if left untreated. This case report highlights a successful management approach in a critically ill patient with SAP requiring surgical intervention.

Case Presentation: A 58-year-old male presented to the emergency department with acute onset epigastric abdominal pain, nausea, and vomiting for three days. The patient had a history of chronic alcohol consumption and was diagnosed with gallstones four years ago. Laboratory results revealed an elevated serum amylase (1500 U/L) and lipase (2400 U/L). Abdominal ultrasound demonstrated a dilated pancreatic duct, while computed tomography (CT) scan confirmed the diagnosis of SAP with necrotizing pancreatitis and acute cholecystitis. The patient's condition deteriorated with the onset of respiratory failure, requiring mechanical ventilation, and renal failure, necessitating dialysis.

Diagnostic Approach: The patient was admitted to the intensive care unit (ICU) for close monitoring and supportive management. Initial treatment included intravenous fluids, analgesia, and nutritional support with total parenteral nutrition (TPN). Endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP) was attempted but unsuccessful due to the patient's critical condition.

Treatment: Given the patient's deteriorating condition and evidence of necrotizing pancreatitis, open necrosectomy was performed on day 10 of hospitalization. A drain was inserted to manage the post-operative collection. The patient received antibiotics and intensive care to manage organ failures.

Outcomes: After two weeks of ICU care, the patient's condition stabilized, with improvement in respiratory and renal functions. The patient was weaned off ventilation and dialysis, and the drain output decreased significantly. On day 21, a second-look laparotomy was performed to evaluate the necrosis, revealing a well-healed surgical site with minimal residual collection. The drain was removed, and the patient was transferred to the ward for further recovery. After one month of hospitalization, the patient was discharged home in stable condition.
