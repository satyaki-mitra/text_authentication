Title: A Case Study on Successful Management of Refractory Gastroesophageal Variceal Bleeding in a Cirrhotic Patient

Abstract

Background: Gastroesophageal variceal bleeding (GEVB) is a life-threatening complication in patients with cirrhosis, carrying high morbidity and mortality rates. This case report presents an uncommon yet successful management approach for refractory GEVB in a cirrhotic patient.

Case Presentation: A 58-year-old male with a ten-year history of alcoholic cirrhosis presented with hematemesis and melena, indicative of acute GEVB. The patient had prior treatment for GEVB including endoscopic band ligation (EBL) and terlipressin infusion, but the bleeding persisted. Upon admission, his MELD score was 18, Child-Pugh class C, and hemoglobin level was 7.5 g/dL. On initial endoscopy, large esophageal varices with active bleeding were observed.

Diagnostic Approach: Given the refractory nature of the GEVB despite conventional treatment, a transjugular intrahepatic portosystemic shunt (TIPS) was considered as an alternative management option. Pre-procedure imaging confirmed portal hypertension with esophagogastric varices and no contraindications for TIPS.

Treatment: The patient underwent a successful TIPS procedure resulting in immediate hemostasis. Postoperatively, the patient was managed with prophylactic antibiotics, antifungals, and lactulose to prevent complications such as sepsis, encephalopathy, and spontaneous bacterial peritonitis.

Outcomes: The patient's hemoglobin level gradually increased over the following days, and he was discharged on hospital day 10 with a stable MELD score of 14, Child-Pugh class B, and a hemoglobin level of 12 g/dL. At six-month follow-up, the patient remained asymptomatic without further episodes of GEVB.

Discussion: This case highlights the successful application of TIPS for the management of refractory GEVB in a cirrhotic patient. The high morbidity and mortality associated with refractory variceal bleeding underscore the importance of exploring alternative treatment strategies such as TIPS. This case report adds to the growing body of literature supporting the use of TIPS in managing refractory GEVB, particularly in patients who do not respond to conventional therapies.
