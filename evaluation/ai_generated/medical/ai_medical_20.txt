Title: An Unusual Case of Recurrent Cholestatic Jaundice in a Young Adult

Abstract

A 25-year-old Caucasian male presented to the emergency department with a three-week history of progressive pruritus, dark urine, and pale stools. The patient reported no significant medical history but did mention a family history of gallstones and liver disease. On physical examination, the patient appeared jaundiced, with scleral icterus and palmar erythema. Laboratory results revealed a total bilirubin level of 15.4 mg/dL (normal range: 0.2-1.3 mg/dL), alanine aminotransferase (ALT) of 68 U/L (normal range: <45 U/L), aspartate aminotransferase (AST) of 97 U/L (normal range: <40 U/L), and alkaline phosphatase (ALP) of 310 U/L (normal range: 46-125 U/L). Ultrasound imaging revealed a normal gallbladder, but liver biopsy showed cholestatic hepatitis with ductular reaction and bile plugs.

Further evaluation included magnetic resonance cholangiopancreatography (MRCP), which demonstrated no obvious obstructive lesions in the biliary tree. A liver-specific immunoglobulin G antibody test was positive, suggesting an autoimmune etiology. The patient was initially treated with prednisone and ursodeoxycholic acid, but his symptoms persisted. Following a multidisciplinary consultation, a diagnosis of primary biliary cholangitis (PBC) was made, and the patient was started on ursodiol and obeticholic acid.

After six months of treatment, the patient's bilirubin levels had decreased to 2.1 mg/dL, ALT was within normal limits, and pruritus had resolved. The case highlights the importance of recognizing PBC, especially in young patients with a family history of liver disease, as early diagnosis can lead to improved outcomes through targeted therapy.
