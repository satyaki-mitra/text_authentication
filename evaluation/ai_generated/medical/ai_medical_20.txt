Title: A Case Study on Successful Management of Refractory Gastrointestinal Stromal Tumor (GIST) Responding to Imatinib Therapy Following Progression after Sunitinib Treatment

Abstract

Background: Gastrointestinal stromal tumors (GISTs) are the most prevalent mesenchymal neoplasms in the gastrointestinal tract, with an incidence of approximately 15 cases per million individuals annually. The standard first-line therapy for advanced or metastatic GIST is imatinib, a tyrosine kinase inhibitor (TKI). However, primary or secondary resistance to imatinib can lead to disease progression, necessitating alternative treatment strategies. This case report presents an unusual response of refractory GIST to re-challenge with imatinib following progression after sunitinib treatment.

Case Presentation: A 62-year-old male presented with a 4-month history of intermittent abdominal pain and weight loss. Imaging studies revealed a 10 cm mass in the gastric antrum, leading to a tentative diagnosis of GIST. The patient was initially treated with imatinib at a dose of 400 mg daily, which resulted in partial response. However, after 12 months, computed tomography (CT) scans showed disease progression. Sunitinib was then initiated at 50 mg daily for three weeks followed by one week off-cycle. After six months, CT scans indicated progressive disease, and imatinib was re-introduced at the same dose as the initial treatment. Surprisingly, after two months, the patient exhibited a complete response to the re-challenge with imatinib, as confirmed by subsequent imaging studies.

Diagnostic Approach: The diagnosis of GIST was based on histological examination of the tumor biopsy specimen, demonstrating spindle cells with a characteristic immunohistochemical profile (positive for CD117 and CD34, negative for S-100 protein). Fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) confirmed the presence of a platelet-derived growth factor receptor alpha (PDGFRA) exon 18 mutation, which is associated with imatinib sensitivity.

Treatment: The initial treatment consisted of imatinib at a dose of 400 mg daily for six months, followed by sunitinib at 50 mg daily for six months due to disease progression. Upon re-challenge, the patient was restarted on imatinib at the same dose as the initial therapy.
