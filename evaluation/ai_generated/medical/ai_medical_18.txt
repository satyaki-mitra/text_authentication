Title: A Case Study on Successful Management of Severe Acute Pancreatitis with Early Surgical Intervention

Abstract

Background: Severe acutepancreatitis (SAP) is a life-threatening condition characterized by systemic inflammation caused by acute pancreatitis. The management of SAP remains challenging due to its unpredictable course and potential complications. Early surgical intervention, although controversial, has been suggested in certain cases for the prevention and treatment of complications.

Case Presentation: A 58-year-old male with a history of alcohol abuse presented to the emergency department with severe epigastric pain radiating to the back, nausea, and vomiting for three days. Initial laboratory tests revealed a serum amylase level of 2000 U/L (normal range: 30-140 U/L) and a lipase level of 6000 U/L (normal range: 13-60 U/L), consistent with acute pancreatitis. The patient's condition worsened, developing respiratory failure, hypotension, and abdominal distention. Computed tomography (CT) scan of the abdomen confirmed the diagnosis of necrotizing pancreatitis with walled-off pancreatic necrosis (WOPN). The patient was managed conservatively initially, but due to persistent sepsis and organ failure, an open necrosectomy was performed on the 14th day of hospitalization. The postoperative course was complicated by a pancreatic fistula that was managed conservatively. The patient was discharged on the 35th day with complete resolution of symptoms and normal laboratory values.

Discussion: Early surgical intervention in SAP remains controversial due to the risk of exacerbating inflammation and increasing morbidity. However, in this case, the patient's rapid deterioration despite conservative management necessitated early surgery. The successful outcome underscores the potential benefits of early surgical intervention in select cases of SAP with refractory organ failure or evidence of necrosis on imaging. Further research is needed to define the specific indications for early surgical intervention and to determine its optimal timing and technique.

Keywords: Acute pancreatitis, severe acute pancreatitis, necrotizing pancreatitis, walled-off pancreatic necrosis, early surgical intervention, open necrosectomy, pancreatic fistula.
