Title: An Unusual Case of Recurrent Angioedema in a Pediatric Patient with Undiagnosed Primary Immunodeficiency

Case Presentation:

A 9-year-old female patient, previously healthy, presented to the Emergency Department with recurrent episodes of facial and limb swelling over the past six months. The symptoms typically manifested as sudden onset, painless, and lasted for several hours without any apparent triggers. The patient had no history of allergies or adverse reactions to food, medications, or insect stings. She reported normal bowel movements and denied any respiratory symptoms, fever, or joint pain.

Physical Examination:

On presentation, the patient appeared well-nourished with no signs of distress. Swelling was observed on her lips, eyelids, and both hands, consistent with previous descriptions by the patient's parents. The skin was non-pitting, warm, and erythematous, suggesting angioedema. No lymphadenopathy or organomegaly was noted.

Investigations:

Complete blood count (CBC), comprehensive metabolic panel (CMP), thyroid function tests (TFT), and urinalysis were within normal limits. C3 and C4 levels were found to be low, suggesting a possible immunodeficiency disorder. The patient underwent further diagnostic testing, including immunoglobulin quantification and lymphocyte subsets analysis, which revealed severe deficiencies in IgG, IgA, and IgM levels, along with a decreased percentage of B cells.

Diagnosis:

The patient was diagnosed with Common Variable Immunodeficiency (CVID) based on the laboratory findings and recurrent angioedema episodes.

Treatment:

The patient was started on intravenous immunoglobulin replacement therapy at a dose of 400mg/kg every three weeks. She was also prescribed antihistamines for symptomatic relief during acute attacks of angioedema.
