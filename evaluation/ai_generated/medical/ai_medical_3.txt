Title: A Rare Case of Chronic Eosinophilic Pneumonia Misdiagnosed as Asthma: A Case Study

Abstract

Background: Chronic eosinophilic pneumonia (CEP) is a relatively uncommon interstitial lung disease characterized by recurrent or persistent symptoms, peripheral blood eosinophilia, and bilateral infiltrates on chest radiography. Its clinical presentation often overlaps with asthma, leading to misdiagnosis and delayed treatment.

Case Presentation: A 45-year-old female, non-smoker, presented with persistent dry cough, fatigue, and intermittent shortness of breath for the past six months. She had been previously diagnosed with asthma and managed with inhaled corticosteroids and bronchodilators without significant improvement. Physical examination revealed normal vital signs, normal oxygen saturation, and bilateral crackles on auscultation. A chest X-ray showed bilateral peripheral infiltrates. A high-resolution computed tomography (HRCT) of the lungs demonstrated ground-glass opacities, interlobular septal thickening, and centrilobular nodules, suggestive of CEP. A bronchoalveolar lavage (BAL) was performed which revealed an eosinophil count greater than 25%. The patient was started on oral corticosteroids with subsequent improvement in symptoms.

Discussion: This case underscores the importance of considering CEP in patients with persistent respiratory symptoms and peripheral blood eosinophilia, even if they have been previously diagnosed with asthma. Delayed diagnosis can lead to prolonged symptoms, increased morbidity, and potential misuse of asthma medications. Early recognition and appropriate management can significantly improve patient outcomes.

Keywords: Chronic Eosinophilic Pneumonia, Asthma Misdiagnosis, Interstitial Lung Disease, Eosinophilia, Corticosteroids.
