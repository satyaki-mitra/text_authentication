Title: A Case Study on Successful Management of Severe Acute Pancreatitis with Early Intervention and Intensive Care

Abstract

Background: Severe acute pancreatitis (SAP) is a life-threatening inflammatory condition associated with high morbidity and mortality. Early diagnosis and aggressive management are crucial for optimal outcomes. This case report highlights an instance of SAP successfully managed through timely intervention, intensive care, and a multidisciplinary approach.

Case Presentation: A 52-year-old male patient presented to the emergency department with a three-day history of persistent epigastric abdominal pain, nausea, and vomiting. The pain was severe, constant, and radiated to his back. He had no history of alcohol abuse or family history of pancreatitis. Physical examination revealed a tender, distended abdomen with guarding and rebound tenderness in the epigastric region. Laboratory results showed elevated serum amylase (1500 U/L) and lipase (2000 U/L), suggestive of acute pancreatitis. A computed tomography scan confirmed the diagnosis, revealing diffuse enlargement of the pancreas with peripancreatic fat stranding and fluid collections, consistent with SAP.

Diagnostic Approach: Given the patient's presentation and laboratory results, a diagnosis of SAP was made. The Balthazar CT Severity Index (CTSI) score, which evaluates the extent of pancreatic and peripancreatic changes on CT scan, was 10, indicating severe disease. The Ranson criteria and APACHE II scores were also elevated, further emphasizing the severity of the condition.

Treatment: The patient was admitted to the intensive care unit for close monitoring and supportive management. Intravenous fluids were administered to correct dehydration, and analgesia was provided to manage pain. Nutritional support was initiated with total parenteral nutrition (TPN) due to the patient's inability to tolerate enteral feeding. Antibiotics were started to prevent complications from possible secondary infection. Endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP) was performed to manage any potential biliary obstruction and to assess for the presence of pancreatic duct disruption, which could indicate the need for surgery.

Outcomes: The patient's condition stabilized over the next 48 hours. ERCP revealed no evidence of biliary obstruction or pancreatic duct disruption, and the patient did not require surgical intervention.
