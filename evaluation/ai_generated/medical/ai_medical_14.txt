Title: A Case Study of Successful Management of Recurrent Pericarditis Using Colchicine in a Pediatric Patient

Abstract

Background: Recurrent pericarditis is a challenging condition, particularly in pediatric patients, due to its potential for progressive and debilitating symptoms. Traditional treatment options may be limited by side effects, necessitating the exploration of alternative therapies. This case study presents a successful management approach using colchicine in a pediatric patient with recurrent pericarditis.

Case Presentation: A 14-year-old male presented with recurring episodes of chest pain, fever, and dyspnea over the course of six months. On physical examination, pericardial friction rub was audible, and electrocardiogram (ECG) revealed diffuse ST-segment elevation and PR depression, consistent with acute pericarditis. Echocardiography confirmed a thickened pericardium without evidence of tamponade or constrictive pericarditis. The patient was initially treated with nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) and colchicine; however, the symptoms persisted, and a course of corticosteroids was initiated due to the severity of symptoms. Despite this, the patient continued to experience recurrent pericarditis episodes, necessitating multiple hospitalizations.

Intervention: Given the lack of efficacy with traditional therapies and the potential for long-term corticosteroid side effects, a switch to high-dose colchicine (2 mg/kg/day in divided doses) was implemented. After one week, the patient reported significant improvement in symptoms, and follow-up ECG showed resolution of ST-segment elevation and PR depression. Over the subsequent six months, the patient remained symptom-free with no further pericarditis episodes, maintained on colchicine monotherapy.

Discussion: This case highlights the potential benefit of high-dose colchicine in managing recurrent pericarditis in a pediatric patient. Colchicine's anti-inflammatory properties have been well established, but its use in pericarditis has been limited due to concerns about gastrointestinal side effects and lack of data in pediatric patients. However, our case suggests that high-dose colchicine may be an effective and well-tolerated alternative to traditional treatments in select cases of recurrent pericarditis.

Future research should focus on larger, controlled studies investigating the efficacy and safety profile of high-dose colchicine in pediatric patients with recurrent pericarditis, as well as exploring optimal dosing regimens to minimize side effects.
