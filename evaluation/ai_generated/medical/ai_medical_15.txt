Title: A Case Study on Successful Management of Refractory Myasthenia Gravis with Plasmapheresis and Rituximab

Abstract

Background: Myasthenia gravis (MG) is an autoimmune neuromuscular disorder characterized by fluctuating muscle weakness, fatigability, and impaired neuromuscular transmission. Refractory MG, resistant to first-line immunosuppressive therapies, poses a significant challenge in clinical practice. This case report presents the successful management of a refractory MG patient through plasmapheresis and Rituximab treatment.

Case Presentation: A 65-year-old female patient presented with symptoms of diplopia, ptosis, dysphagia, and generalized muscle weakness. The patient's medical history included hypertension, type II diabetes mellitus, and a recent diagnosis of thymoma. The Myasthenia Gravis Foundation of America (MGFA) classification indicated MGFA class III due to bulbar, ocular, and limb involvement. Despite treatment with prednisone, azathioprine, and pyridostigmine, the patient's symptoms persisted and worsened over several months.

Diagnostic Approach: The diagnosis of MG was confirmed through a positive Edrophonium test, Tensilon test, and increased serum anti-acetylcholine receptor (AChR) antibodies. Computerized tomography (CT) scan confirmed the presence of a thymoma, which supported the diagnosis of thymoma-associated MG.

Treatment: Due to the refractory nature of the patient's MG, plasmapheresis was initiated as an urgent intervention to remove circulating antibodies and improve neuromuscular transmission. Subsequently, Rituximab, a chimeric monoclonal antibody targeting CD20+ B cells, was administered intravenously at a dosage of 375 mg/m² weekly for four weeks to deplete the pathogenic B cells responsible for producing AChR antibodies.

Outcomes: Following plasmapheresis and Rituximab treatment, the patient experienced significant improvement in symptoms, with complete resolution of diplopia and ptosis after two weeks and near-complete resolution of dysphagia and limb weakness after four weeks. The patient's MGFA classification was downgraded to class II, and serum AChR antibodies decreased significantly from 40 nmol/L at baseline to 1 nmol/L post-treatment.
