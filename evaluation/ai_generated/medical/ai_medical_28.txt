Title: A Case Study on the Successful Management of Refractory Gastroesophageal Variceal Hemorrhage in a Cirrhotic Patient

Abstract

Background: Gastroesophageal variceal hemorrhage (GVH) is a life-threatening complication in patients with cirrhosis, carrying high morbidity and mortality rates. This case report presents an unusual but successful management strategy for refractory GVH in a cirrhotic patient who failed initial endoscopic and pharmacological interventions.

Case Presentation: A 58-year-old male with a history of alcoholic liver disease (Child-Pugh Class B) and esophageal varices presented with hematemesis and melena, indicating active GVH. Initial endoscopic band ligation was unsuccessful due to extensive bleeding, followed by failed sclerotherapy sessions due to rebleeding. The patient was subsequently treated with terlipressin, a vasoactive intestinal peptide analogue, in combination with prophylactic antibiotics and transjugular intrahepatic portosystemic shunt (TIPS) placement.

Diagnostic Approach: Diagnosis was established through esophagogastroduodenoscopy (EGD), which revealed large esophageal varices with active bleeding. Laboratory investigations confirmed the presence of portal hypertension, with an elevated international normalized ratio (INR) and thrombocytopenia.

Treatment: Given the refractory nature of the GVH, the patient was administered terlipressin to improve hemostasis through splanchnic vasoconstriction. Concurrently, prophylactic antibiotics were initiated to prevent bacterial translocation and spontaneous bacterial peritonitis (SBP). As a last resort, TIPS procedure was performed to reduce portal pressure and decrease variceal bleeding.

Outcomes: Following the combined therapy, the patient experienced rapid hemostasis with stabilization of vital signs and resolution of melena. No further episodes of GVH were reported during the 6-month follow-up period. The TIPS procedure was effective in lowering portal pressure, as evidenced by a reduction in INR levels.

Discussion: This case highlights an alternative treatment strategy for refractory GVH. Terlipressin, combined with prophylactic antibiotics and TIPS placement, effectively controlled the acute bleeding episode and prevented further rebleeding. Further research is warranted to determine the optimal dosage regimen and duration of terlipressin therapy in managing refractory GVH, as well as its impact on long-term survival.
