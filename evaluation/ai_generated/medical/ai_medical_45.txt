Title: A Case Study on Successful Management of Severe Pemphigus Vulgaris Using Rituximab: A Novel Approach

Abstract

Background: Pemphigus vulgaris (PV), a rare autoimmune blistering disorder, is characterized by intraepidermal acantholysis due to autoantibodies against desmoglein 1 and 3. Traditional treatment regimens often involve high-dose corticosteroids and immunosuppressants, which carry significant risks of side effects and limited long-term efficacy. This case report aims to demonstrate the successful management of a refractory PV case using rituximab.

Case Presentation: A 58-year-old Caucasian male presented with a four-month history of extensive mucocutaneous blistering, erosions, and crusting, predominantly affecting the oral cavity, nasal labia, and genitalia (Fig. 1). Nikolsky's sign was positive, and direct immunofluorescence (DIF) revealed IgG and C3 deposits along the epidermal-dermal junction, confirming a diagnosis of PV (Fig. 2). Despite treatment with high-dose prednisone, azathioprine, and mycophenolate mofetil, the patient's condition worsened, and he developed severe oral and nasal ulcerations, recurrent infections, and debilitating pain.

Intervention: Given the refractory nature of the patient's PV to conventional therapy, rituximab was initiated at a dose of 375 mg/m2 weekly for four weeks (total dose of 1500 mg). Within two weeks of the first infusion, the patient reported significant improvement in oral and nasal pain, and healing of ulcerations was observed (Fig. 3). Over the next eight weeks, the patient's condition continued to improve, with complete resolution of symptoms at the end of the treatment course (Fig. 4).

Discussion: This case highlights the successful use of rituximab in managing refractory PV. Rituximab is a chimeric monoclonal antibody targeting CD20-positive B cells, which are implicated in the pathogenesis of PV due to their role in producing autoantibodies against desmoglein proteins. The rapid response observed in this case suggests that rituximab may offer a novel approach for managing refractory cases of PV, potentially reducing the need for high-dose corticosteroids and other immunosuppressants with their attendant risks and side effects.
