Title: A Case Study on Successful Management of Recurrent Severe Pseudomonas aeruginosa Pneumonia in a Cystic Fibrosis Patient

Abstract

Background: Pseudomonas aeruginosa (P. aeruginosa) is a significant opportunistic pathogen often complicating respiratory disorders, especially in cystic fibrosis (CF) patients. This case study reports an unusual presentation and successful management strategy of a recurrent severe P. aeruginosa pneumonia in a CF patient.

Case Presentation: A 30-year-old male with a known history of CF presented with an acute exacerbation of the disease characterized by increased cough, purulent sputum production, and progressive dyspnea over two weeks. His medical history included frequent hospitalizations due to recurrent P. aeruginosa pneumonia. On admission, laboratory tests revealed elevated inflammatory markers (CRP: 150 mg/L, WBC: 13.5 G/L), and sputum culture confirmed the presence of P. aeruginosa sensitive to ceftazidime. Chest X-ray showed bilateral patchy infiltrates, consistent with pneumonia.

Diagnostic Approach: Given the patient's history of recurrent P. aeruginosa infection and severe disease course, additional investigations were carried out. Sputum analysis for P. aeruginosa biofilm formation revealed a high density, suggesting chronic colonization and potentially antimicrobial resistance mechanisms. A genetic test confirmed the CFTR mutation (F508del).

Treatment: The patient was initially treated with intravenous ceftazidime, along with inhaled tobramycin, airway clearance therapy, and supportive care. However, considering the high density of P. aeruginosa biofilm in sputum samples, we introduced aerosolized polymyxin B (aPMB) as an adjunctive therapy. After 10 days of aPMB administration, the patient showed significant improvement in clinical symptoms and inflammatory markers.

Outcomes: The patient was discharged with improved oxygen saturation levels and reduced respiratory distress. Follow-up sputum cultures after two weeks revealed a significant decrease in P. aeruginosa density, suggesting the effectiveness of the combined therapy. The patient continued regular home treatments, including aPMB and routine CF medications, reporting no exacerbations at 6-month follow-up.
