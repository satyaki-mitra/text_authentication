Title: A Rare Case of Chronic Intestinal Pseudo-Obstruction in a Pediatric Patient

Abstract:

We present an unusual case of a 12-year-old male patient who was admitted to our pediatric department due to recurrent abdominal pain, vomiting, and constipation for the past six months. The patient's medical history was unremarkable with no previous surgeries or significant familial medical issues.

Upon initial examination, the patient appeared malnourished and showed signs of severe dehydration. Laboratory tests revealed an elevated white blood cell count (WBC: 15.2 x 10^3/µL) and a slightly increased C-reactive protein (CRP: 12 mg/dL). Abdominal X-ray showed multiple air-fluid levels, suggesting mechanical obstruction. However, contrast enema revealed no evidence of anatomic obstruction.

A diagnosis of Chronic Intestinal Pseudo-Obstruction (CIPO) was made based on the absence of a mechanical cause for the symptoms and the response to nasogastric suction and intravenous fluids. The patient was managed conservatively with nasogastric suction, total parenteral nutrition, and pain control.

After four weeks of hospitalization, the patient showed significant improvement in symptoms and was discharged with a plan for regular follow-ups, dietary modifications, and drug therapy (e.g., erythromycin) to aid intestinal motility.

This case underscores the importance of considering rare conditions such as CIPO in pediatric patients presenting with symptoms suggestive of mechanical obstruction but lacking definitive radiological findings. Early recognition and appropriate management can lead to improved outcomes for these patients.
