Title: A Case Study on Successful Management of Acute Pancreatitis Complicated by Sepsis in a Post-ERCP Patient

Abstract

Background: Acute pancreatitis (AP) is a common gastrointestinal disorder, often induced by endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP). The complications of AP, such as sepsis, can lead to significant morbidity and mortality. This case report illustrates an effective management approach for acute pancreatitis complicated by sepsis in a post-ERCP patient.

Case Presentation: A 65-year-old male with a history of choledocholithiasis presented with epigastric abdominal pain, nausea, and vomiting for three days following an uncomplicated ERCP procedure. The initial laboratory results revealed leukocytosis (14.5 x 10^3/μL), elevated serum amylase (257 U/L) and lipase (896 U/L), and C-reactive protein (CRP, 212 mg/L). The patient was diagnosed with acute pancreatitis based on the Atlanta criteria. On hospital day three, the patient developed fever (39°C), tachycardia, hypotension, and respiratory failure, indicative of sepsis. Computed tomography (CT) scan revealed necrotizing pancreatitis with signs of infection. The patient was immediately administered broad-spectrum antibiotics, fluids for resuscitation, and vasopressors for blood pressure support.

Diagnostic Approach: Given the progression to sepsis, a percutaneous drainage of the necrotic collection under CT guidance was performed on hospital day four. A sample from the drainage site was sent for microbiology culture. The patient was also monitored closely for organ failure and received intensive care unit (ICU) management.

Treatment: The patient responded well to the aggressive treatment, with resolution of sepsis symptoms within 48 hours. The antibiotics were adjusted based on the microbiology culture results, which identified Enterococcus faecium as the causative organism. The patient remained in the ICU for ten days and was transitioned to a regular ward after stabilization.

Outcomes: The patient made a full recovery and was discharged after 15 days of hospitalization. Follow-up CT scan revealed resolution of the necrotic collection, and the patient reported no persisting symptoms at the three-month follow-up visit.
