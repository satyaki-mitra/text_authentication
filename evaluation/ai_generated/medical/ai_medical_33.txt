Title: A Case Study on Successful Management of Malignant Pleural Mesothelioma with Pembrolizumab: An Unusual Response in an Elderly Patient

Abstract

Background: Malignant pleural mesothelioma (MPM) is a rare and aggressive neoplasm primarily linked to asbestos exposure, with a dismal prognosis. The standard treatment regimen consists of chemotherapy, surgery, or radiation therapy; however, the response rates are modest, and the survival benefits are limited. Immunotherapeutic agents, such as pembrolizumab, have shown promising results in some cases, but their efficacy in elderly patients is less established.

Case Presentation: A 78-year-old retired shipyard worker, with a 40-year history of asbestos exposure, presented with progressive dyspnea and non-productive cough for three months. Physical examination revealed decreased breath sounds on the left side, and chest X-ray showed a left pleural effusion. Thoracentesis confirmed a diagnosis of MPM, and a subsequent positron emission tomography-computed tomography (PET-CT) scan demonstrated multiple nodules in both lungs and mediastinal lymphadenopathy. The patient was classified as Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group Performance Status 2 (ECOG PS 2).

Given the advanced age of the patient, poor performance status, and extensive disease, the initial treatment plan consisted of palliative care to manage symptoms. However, after an informed discussion regarding potential benefits and risks, the patient opted for immunotherapy with pembrolizumab due to its favorable safety profile. The patient received an initial dose of 200 mg intravenously every three weeks.

Follow-up assessments revealed a remarkable response to treatment. After two cycles of pembrolizumab, the patient's dyspnea and cough significantly improved (ECOG PS 1), and a repeat PET-CT scan showed a substantial decrease in the size of lung nodules and resolution of mediastinal lymphadenopathy. The patient continued to respond well to treatment for 18 months, with minimal side effects and no disease progression.

Discussion: This case report highlights an unusual response to pembrolizumab in an elderly patient with extensive MPM. While the majority of immunotherapy trials exclude older adults due to concerns about safety and efficacy, our case demonstrates that this treatment modality may be beneficial for some elderly patients with good performance status. Future research should focus on identifying biomarkers predictive of response to immune checkpoint inhibitors in MPM and establishing optimal treatment strategies for elderly patients.
