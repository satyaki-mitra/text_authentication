Title: A Rare Case of Pseudoxanthoma Elasticum Presenting with Retinal Detachment

Abstract

We report an unusual case of a 52-year-old Caucasian male who presented to our ophthalmology department with a complaint of blurred vision in his left eye for the past two weeks. The patient's medical history was significant for no known systemic diseases, but he reported a family history of premature cataract formation and cardiovascular issues.

Upon examination, slit-lamp biomicroscopy revealed angioid streaks radiating from the optic disc in the left eye. Funduscopic exam showed yellowish plaques around the neck of the retinal vessels, characteristic of planar calcifications in Pseudoxanthoma Elasticum (PXE). Indirect ophthalmoscopy confirmed a posterior pole detachment in the same eye. The right eye showed no abnormalities.

The patient was diagnosed with Pseudoxanthoma Elasticum, which is an uncommon, autosomal recessive disorder characterized by calcification and fragmentation of elastic fibers in various tissues such as skin, retina, and cardiovascular system. This condition can lead to complications like choroidal neovascularization, retinal detachment, and arterial aneurysms.

The patient was referred for further management involving vitrectomy and scleral buckling surgery to repair the retinal detachment. Genetic counseling was also provided due to the hereditary nature of the disease and potential risks for family members.

This case highlights the importance of considering rare conditions like Pseudoxanthoma Elasticum in patients presenting with symptoms suggestive of retinal disorders, especially when there is a family history of related complications. Early diagnosis can aid in timely management, potentially reducing morbidity and preserving visual function.

Keywords: Pseudoxanthoma Elasticum, Retinal Detachment, Angioid Streaks, Genetic Counseling
