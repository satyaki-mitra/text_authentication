Title: A Case Study on Successful Management of Severe Acute Pancreatitis Using Prophylactic Plasminogen Activator and Early Enteral Nutrition

Abstract

Background: Severe acute pancreatitis (SAP) is a life-threatening inflammatory condition associated with high morbidity and mortality. Traditional management strategies primarily focus on supportive care, fluid resuscitation, pain control, and nutritional support. However, the search for more effective interventions continues to drive research in this field.

Case Presentation: A 58-year-old male presented to the emergency department with a four-day history of persistent epigastric abdominal pain radiating to his back, accompanied by vomiting and fever. Laboratory investigations revealed a serum amylase level of 1,600 IU/L (normal range: 25-140 IU/L) and lipase level of 3,000 IU/L (normal range: 13-60 IU/L), suggestive of pancreatitis. Computed tomography confirmed the diagnosis of SAP with necrotizing features.

Diagnostic Approach: Given the severity of the patient's condition, a thorough diagnostic workup was conducted to assess the extent of organ damage and complications. Transabdominal ultrasonography revealed pancreatic necrosis. Magnetic resonance cholangiopancreatography (MRCP) was performed to exclude other potential etiologies such as gallstones or tumors.

Treatment: The patient was immediately started on prophylactic alteplase, a plasminogen activator, to prevent complications related to pancreatic necrosis such as infection and abscess formation. Early enteral nutrition (EN) was initiated within 24 hours of hospital admission to minimize catabolic stress, maintain gut integrity, and promote recovery. Pain control was achieved using a combination of opioids and non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs).

Outcomes: The patient's clinical condition improved significantly over the course of 7 days, with gradual resolution of abdominal pain and normalization of laboratory values. Following a period of close observation and management of complications, the patient was discharged on hospital day 14 with no signs of residual pancreatitis or complications.
