Title: The Green Flash Phenomenon: A Meteorological Marvel

The Green Flash phenomenon is an intriguing optical effect often observed near sunrise or sunset, particularly over clear and calm horizons. This captivating event displays a brief, vibrant green burst at the upper limb of the setting or rising sun. Despite its common occurrence in popular lore, the scientific explanation behind this natural spectacle remains fascinating.

The Green Flash phenomenon is primarily caused by atmospheric refraction and dispersion of sunlight. As light passes through Earth's atmosphere, it encounters varying densities of air that cause it to bend or refract. The bending of light depends on the wavelength of each color, with shorter wavelengths (such as blue and violet) being refracted more than longer ones (like red and orange).

During sunset, the sunlight must pass through a significant portion of the Earth's atmosphere to reach an observer on the ground. As the sun dips lower in the sky, the density of the atmosphere increases, causing the shorter wavelengths of light to be refracted more than the longer ones, leaving only red and orange colors visible at the horizon.

However, as the sun approaches the horizon, a layer of denser air known as the "temperature inversion" can form just above it. This layer has cooler temperatures and higher atmospheric pressure compared to the surrounding air, resulting in a decrease in refraction for all wavelengths of light. The decreased refraction allows some of the scattered blue and green light to reach the observer's eye once again.

In some conditions, when the temperature inversion occurs at just the right altitude and the atmosphere remains stable, the sun appears to flash a brilliant green color for a brief moment before disappearing below the horizon. This stunning event is known as the Green Flash.

The duration of the Green Flash can vary from a mere split second to several seconds, making it an elusive and captivating sight for those lucky enough to witness it. The phenomenon's visibility depends on various factors, including the clarity of the atmosphere, the presence of atmospheric dust or humidity, and the exact angle at which the sun sets or rises.
