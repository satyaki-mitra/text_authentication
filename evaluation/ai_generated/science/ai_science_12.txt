Title: The Green Flash Phenomenon: An Explanation of a Fascinating Solar Observation

Introduction:
The Green Flash is an elusive and captivating optical phenomenon that occurs at sunset, often leaving spectators in awe. This enigmatic event is observed as a brief burst of bright green light on the upper rim of the setting sun. The phenomenon is not well understood, but it can be explained by various scientific theories.

Explanation:
The Green Flash phenomenon occurs due to a combination of atmospheric refraction, dispersion, and scattering of sunlight. As the sun sets below the horizon, the Earth's atmosphere bends the light rays passing through it, causing the sun to appear larger than its actual angular size. This effect is known as mirage or twilight anomaly.

When the sun nears the horizon, it is still above the dense layer of the atmosphere called the turbid layer, which contains small dust particles and water droplets that scatter sunlight in various ways. As the sun dips below this layer, the shorter wavelengths of light (blue and violet) are scattered out of our line of sight, while the longer wavelengths (red, orange, and yellow) continue to refract and bend towards the observer.

However, as the sun sets even further, it reaches a point where only red light remains in the direct path, and the green and blue light are refracted above the horizon. At this moment, if conditions align just right—including a clear and stable atmosphere, a dry heat haze, and a specific angle of solar elevation—these remaining green and blue rays can interact with each other in a phenomenon known as superprism effect or sodium dispersion.

In the sodium dispersion theory, the green light interacts with a rare sodium layer present in the Earth's atmosphere, specifically at an altitude of about 60-90 kilometers (37-56 miles). This layer is composed of sodium atoms from meteorites that have disintegrated and mixed with the air. When the green light encounters these sodium atoms, it undergoes a process called resonance fluorescence, where energy is absorbed and re-emitted at specific wavelengths, resulting in a burst of bright green light—the Green Flash.
