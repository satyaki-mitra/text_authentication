Title: A Comparative Analysis of Ensemble Learning Techniques for Imbalanced Classification Problems

Abstract:

This study explores the application and comparison of various ensemble learning techniques in addressing imbalanced classification problems, a common challenge in machine learning that often leads to biased models. Ensemble methods combine multiple learning algorithms to improve performance and reduce overfitting, particularly beneficial for complex and challenging datasets.

We focus on three popular ensemble techniques: Bagging, Boosting, and Stackening. Bagging (Bootstrap Aggregation) reduces variance by training different models on randomly sampled subsets of the original dataset. Boosting sequentially trains models to correct the errors of previous models, gradually reducing bias but potentially increasing variance. Stackening (also known as Stacking Generalization or Blending) combines multiple learning algorithms in a multi-layered structure to further improve performance and adaptability.

To evaluate their effectiveness on imbalanced datasets, we perform experiments using several benchmark datasets with significant class imbalance. We measure the accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score, and area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC-ROC) for each technique. The results demonstrate that ensemble learning methods significantly outperform individual base learners in addressing imbalanced classification problems.

Furthermore, we find that Boosting algorithms, such as AdaBoost and Gradient Boosting Decision Trees (GBDT), generally perform better than Bagging or Stacking when dealing with high-dimensional datasets with severe class imbalance. However, in scenarios where overfitting is a concern, Stackening offers a flexible solution for balancing model complexity and generalization performance.

This research provides insights into the application of ensemble learning techniques to tackle imbalanced classification problems. Understanding their unique strengths and weaknesses can help practitioners make informed decisions when selecting an ensemble method for specific machine learning tasks.
