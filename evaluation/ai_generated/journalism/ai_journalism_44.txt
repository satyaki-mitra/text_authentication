Title: Landmark Climate Agreement Signed by Over 190 Countries

In an unprecedented move towards global cooperation, over 190 countries have signed the Paris Agreement at the United Nations Climate Change Conference in New York City on April 22, Earth Day. The agreement, which aims to combat climate change and its effects, was originally adopted by 196 parties in December 2015 in Paris, France.

The Paris Agreement sets out a long-term goal of keeping the global average temperature increase this century well below 2 degrees Celsius above pre-industrial levels, and to pursue efforts to limit the temperature increase even further to 1.5 degrees Celsius. It also calls for regular national greenhouse gas emissions inventories, climate resilience planning, and a global stocktake of progress every five years.

The signing ceremony was attended by numerous world leaders including US President Barack Obama, Chinese President Xi Jinping, and Indian Prime Minister Narendra Modi, among others. The agreement will enter into force once at least 55 countries, accounting for at least 55% of global greenhouse gas emissions, have ratified it.

The Paris Agreement is a significant step forward in international efforts to address climate change, demonstrating a strong commitment from the global community to work together towards a sustainable future. The next steps will involve the detailed negotiation and implementation of national climate action plans, known as Nationally Determined Contributions (NDCs).

The signing of the Paris Agreement marks a turning point in global efforts to combat climate change, providing a platform for continued international cooperation and action. As President Obama stated during the ceremony, "We know that the cost of not acting on climate change will be far greater than the cost of acting."
