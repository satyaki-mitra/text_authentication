Title: Vaccine Mandate Debate Intensifies as New York City Requires Proof of COVID-19 Vaccination for Indoor Activities

NEW YORK (AP) — A contentious debate over vaccine mandates has escalated in New York City, with Mayor Bill de Blasio announcing a new policy that requires proof of COVID-19 vaccination for indoor activities at restaurants, gyms, and entertainment venues. The move, which will take effect on Aug. 16, is designed to combat the spread of the Delta variant but has sparked criticism from some business owners and civil liberties advocates.

"This is about keeping New York City open," de Blasio said during a press conference on Monday. "We need to do everything we can to prevent another closure of our businesses, which would be devastating."

The policy, the first of its kind in the United States, will apply to anyone aged 12 and older. Children under 12, who are currently not eligible for vaccination, will be exempt. The city will provide free vaccinations at more than 100 sites across the five boroughs in an effort to boost vaccination rates among young people.

The announcement has been met with a mix of support and opposition. Supporters argue that the measure is necessary to protect public health and encourage more New Yorkers to get vaccinated, while opponents claim it infringes on personal freedoms and could harm businesses already struggling to recover from pandemic-related closures.

"This is a heavy-handed approach that will only further strain businesses still reeling from the pandemic," said John Durso Jr., president of Local 834 of the International Brotherhood of Teamsters, which represents numerous restaurant workers. "We need to find ways to encourage vaccinations without penalizing those who have not yet had the opportunity to get vaccinated."

Civil liberties groups have also expressed concerns about potential privacy issues and the practicality of enforcing the mandate. The New York Civil Liberties Union (NYCLU) has called for clear guidelines on how businesses will verify vaccine status, as well as protections against discrimination and potential abuse of data.

"We're deeply concerned about the potential for misuse of this information, particularly given the troubling history of government surveillance in communities of color," NYCLU Executive Director Donna Lieberman said in a statement. "The city must prioritize transparency and accountability as it implements this policy."

De Blasio's office has stated that businesses will be required to scan vaccination cards or use an electronic verification system provided by the city. The city will also offer free training for businesses on how to implement the new policy.
