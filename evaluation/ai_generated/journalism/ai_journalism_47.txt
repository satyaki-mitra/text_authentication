Title: Global Climate Conference Reaches Consensus on Emission Reduction Targets

In an unprecedented move, delegates from over 190 countries have reached a consensus at the recently concluded United Nations Climate Change Conference (COP26) in Glasgow, Scotland. The agreement aims to significantly reduce greenhouse gas emissions and limit global warming to 1.5 degrees Celsius above pre-industrial levels.

The agreement, known as the Glasgow Climate Pact, includes a commitment from countries to revisit and strengthen their national targets for cutting carbon emissions every year until 2030. The pact also encourages the development of plans for achieving net-zero emissions by mid-century.

Leaders hailed the agreement as a significant step forward in global efforts to combat climate change, although some critics argue that the targets are still not ambitious enough to prevent catastrophic environmental damage.

The Glasgow Climate Pact also includes provisions for providing financial assistance to developing countries to help them transition to cleaner energy sources and adapt to the impacts of climate change. Additionally, it calls for regular reporting on progress towards meeting these targets and for increased cooperation between countries in implementing their commitments.

The agreement is non-binding, meaning that individual countries are not legally obligated to meet their targets. However, many leaders expressed hope that the pact will help create a global momentum towards addressing climate change, with some suggesting that it could pave the way for more ambitious action in the future.

This is a developing story and further details are expected to emerge in the coming days as world leaders continue to discuss the implementation of the Glasgow Climate Pact.
