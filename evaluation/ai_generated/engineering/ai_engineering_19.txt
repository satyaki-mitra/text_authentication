Title: Performance Analysis and Design Optimization of the Hybrid Solar-Wind Energy System for Remote Communities

Executive Summary:

This report presents an analysis of the hybrid solar-wind energy system designed for remote communities that lack reliable electricity sources. The aim is to deliver a sustainable, cost-effective, and efficient solution to meet the growing energy demands while minimizing environmental impact. This document discusses the system design, performance analysis, design constraints, and recommendations based on comprehensive simulations and real-world data.

1. System Design Overview

The proposed hybrid solar-wind energy system consists of a photovoltaic (PV) array, wind turbines, battery storage system, power conversion units, and a load controller. The PV array generates electricity during daylight hours, while the wind turbines produce power when winds are favorable. The battery storage system is used to store excess energy produced by both sources and supplies energy during peak demand or periods of low renewable generation.

1. Performance Analysis

To evaluate the system's performance, simulations were conducted using a combination of MATLAB/Simulink and PVWatts software. The average daily power production from the solar array was estimated to be 30 kWh per day with an annual energy yield of approximately 10,500 kWh. Wind turbines, based on a wind speed of 7 m/s, are expected to produce 20 kWh per day, resulting in a combined daily output of 50 kWh.

The system's capacity factor, which measures the ratio of actual energy production and maximum potential energy production, was calculated to be around 34%. This value indicates that the hybrid system is effectively utilizing the available resources, despite the intermittent nature of solar and wind power.

1. Design Constraints

Several design constraints were considered during the development process:

a) Geographical location: The system's suitability is dependent on areas with consistent sunlight and relatively high wind speeds.
b) Climate conditions: The system should be designed to withstand harsh weather conditions, such as extreme temperatures, heavy rainfall, and strong winds.
c) Battery storage capacity: Enough battery storage capacity must be provided to store excess energy during peak production hours for use during low-production periods or high demand.
d) Load requirements: The system should cater to the community's varying power demands, including lighting, heating, and electronic devices.
e) Cost-effectiveness: The design must minimize costs while ensuring a reliable, sustainable energy source for remote communities.
