Title: Management of Postoperative Acute Pancreatitis in a Gastric Bypass Surgery Patient: A Clinical Case Study

Introduction

In the realm of surgical interventions, post-gastric bypass surgery can lead to acute pancreatitis (AP), a pancreas inflammation condition. Early identification and effective management are paramount to reduce complications and enhance recovery outcomes.

Clinical Scenario

A 58-year-old woman, weighing 150.2 pounds with a body mass index of 37.2 kg/m², sought emergency care three days post-gastric bypass surgery due to acute abdominal discomfort, nausea, and vomiting. Her medical history was unremarkable save for morbid obesity; no previous episodes of pancreatitis or significant comorbidities were documented. Initial blood tests indicated increased serum amylase (130 IU/L) and lipase (784 U/L), levels beyond the normal range of 25-96 IU/L for amylase and 13-60 U/L for lipase, respectively. Abdominal ultrasound revealed a pancreas with heterogeneous appearance and peripancreatic fluid collection, indicative of AP.

Diagnosis Strategy

The suspected diagnosis was confirmed through magnetic resonance cholangiopancreatography (MRCP), which showed mild dilation of the pancreatic duct and peripancreatic fat thickening, consistent with AP. The causation was traced back to the recent gastric bypass surgery due to the early onset and subsequent resolution of symptoms post-surgery.
