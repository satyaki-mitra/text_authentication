Title: The Impact of Exogenous Melatonin on Sleep Quality in Shift Work Disorder: A Randomized, Double-Blind Trial

Introduction: Non-standard work schedules often lead to shift work disorder (SWD), a sleep disturbance affecting millions worldwide. This condition, marked by insomnia, excessive daytime drowsiness, and decreased alertness, results in reduced productivity and quality of life for affected individuals. The pineal gland-secreted hormone melatonin, known for its role in regulating circadian rhythms, may offer a potential solution due to its association with sleep regulation.

Research Design: In this randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled study, 150 SWD patients were enrolled based on the International Classification of Sleep Disorders criteria. Subjects were randomly assigned to receive either melatonin (3 mg) or a placebo for eight weeks. To evaluate sleep quality, we used the Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index (PSQI), while the Epworth Sleepiness Scale (ESS) and Stanford Sleepiness Scale (SSS) assessed daytime alertness and functioning, respectively.

Main Results: Participants in the melatonin group displayed significant improvements in sleep quality compared to the placebo group, as shown by lower PSQI scores (p<0.001). Additionally, these participants reported reduced daytime drowsiness (lower ESS and SSS scores; p<0.001) and heightened alertness (higher mean SSS scores; p=0.02). The safety profile of melatonin was favorable, with no significant adverse events observed during the study period.

Conclusion: These findings indicate that exogenous melatonin supplementation could potentially improve sleep quality and alleviate daytime drowsiness in SWD patients. Further investigations are necessary to explore long-term effects, optimal dosage regimens, and underlying mechanisms contributing to its therapeutic benefits. If substantiated, these results may pave the way for more effective management of SWD, resulting in improved sleep health
