Title: Efficiency Optimization and Strategies for Modern Server Farm Cooling Systems: Analysis and Suggestions

In this technical report, we delve into the evaluation of cooling systems within contemporary server farms, pinpoint performance hindrances, and put forth strategies to enhance system efficiency. Our primary objectives are reducing energy consumption, cutting down operational expenses, and boosting overall system dependability.

Firstly, let's examine the system architecture:

A collection of 10 air-cooled computer rooms equipped with precision cooling apparatus, hot/cold aisle separation, and economizers to utilize outdoor air are under scrutiny. The aim is to regulate temperature and humidity within server clusters while minimizing energy waste.

Next, we delve into specifics:

- Ten computer rooms house 5 precision cooling devices each, totalling 50 units.
- A ratio of equal hot and cold aisle compartments exists (1:1).
- Economizers operate within a temperature range of 50°F to 95°F for outdoor air and 72°F to 84°F for indoor air.

Our evaluation revealed several insights:

4. Energy Consumption Assessment

During the hotter seasons, energy consumption rose by approximately 20% due to increased mechanical cooling over economization of outdoor air. This hike in energy consumption resulted in higher operational costs and a less eco-friendly operation.

4.1 Temperature and Humidity Analysis

Although temperature and humidity levels within the server clusters were generally controlled, there were cases where hot/cold aisle separation was insufficient, leading to inconsistencies in readings across different zones of the data center.

Potential limitations in the design have surfaced:

5. Design Challenges

- Extreme temperatures can hamper outdoor air economization.
- Ineffective or neglected hot/cold aisle separation can impact performance.
- Periodic maintenance and replacements of precision cooling devices may inflate costs.
