Title: Managing Persistent, Severe Pseudomonas aeruginosa Lung Infections in a Cystic Fibrosis Patient: An Unusual Case

Introduction:
In the realm of respiratory illnesses, Pseudomonas aeruginosa (P. aeruginosa) stands out as a formidable opportunistic pathogen, particularly in patients with cystic fibrosis (CF). This study presents an atypical manifestation and successful treatment approach for recurrent, severe P. aeruginosa lung infections in a CF patient.

Case Scenario:
A 30-year-old male, known to have CF, experienced a severe flare-up of the disease characterized by increased coughing, thickened mucus production, and worsening breathlessness over a two-week period. His medical history was marked by frequent hospitalizations due to repeated instances of P. aeruginosa lung infections. Upon admission, laboratory tests indicated elevated inflammatory markers (CRP: 150 mg/L, WBC: 13.5 G/L), and sputum culture confirmed the presence of P. aeruginosa, sensitive to ceftazidime. A chest X-ray showed bilateral patchy infiltrates, indicative of pneumonia.

Investigative Approach:
Given the patient's history of recurrent P. aeruginosa infections and the severe progression of his condition, additional tests were conducted. Analysis of sputum revealed a high density of P. aeruginosa biofilm, suggesting chronic colonization and possible antimicrobial resistance mechanisms. A genetic test confirmed the CFTR mutation (F508del).

Therapeutic Intervention:
Initially, the patient was treated with intravenous ceftazidime, in conjunction with inhaled tobramycin, airway clearance therapy, and supportive care.
