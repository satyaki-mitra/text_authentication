Title: A Clinical Study on Effective Management of Severe Pemphigus Vulgaris Employing Rituximab: An Innovative Strategy

Introduction:
Pemphigus vulgaris (PV), an uncommon autoimmune skin disorder, manifests through epidermal acantholysis triggered by autoantibodies targeting desmoglein 1 and 3. Conventional treatment methods typically involve potent corticosteroids and immunosuppressants, posing substantial risks of side effects and limited long-term effectiveness. This case study aims to demonstrate the successful management of an unresponsive PV case utilizing Rituximab.

Case Description:
A 58-year-old male of Caucasian origin endured a four-month period marked by extensive mucocutaneous blistering, lesions, and scabbing, primarily affecting the oral cavity, nasal labia, and genitalia (Illustration 1). The Nikolsky sign was positive, and direct immunofluorescence (DIF) indicated IgG and C3 deposits at the epidermis-dermis junction, leading to a definitive PV diagnosis (Illustration 2). Despite treatment with high-dose prednisone, azathioprine, and mycophenolate mofetil, the patient's condition deteriorated, resulting in severe oral and nasal sores, recurrent infections, and intense pain.

Treatment:
Given the recalcitrant nature of the patient's PV towards traditional therapy, Rituximab was administered at a dosage of 375 mg/m2 per week over four weeks (a total dose of 1500 mg). Within two weeks following the initial infusion, the patient reported significant relief from oral and nasal pain, with observed healing of sores (Illustration 3).
