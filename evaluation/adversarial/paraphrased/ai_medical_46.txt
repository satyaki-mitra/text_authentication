Title: Managing Persistent Gout Symptoms in a Challenging Patient: A Case Study on Allopurinol Therapy

Introduction:
Gout, an inflammatory arthritis type, is characterized by recurring acute attacks, frequently affecting the first metatarsophalangeal joint. Effective management of gout necessitates a combination of symptomatic treatment and urate-lowering therapy (ULT). This case study details the successful management of an otherwise treatment-resistant patient with Allopurinol, a ULT that had previously shown ineffectiveness.

Case Study:
A 65-year-old male, battling hypertension, hyperlipidemia, and type 2 diabetes mellitus, found himself repeatedly hospitalized due to gout flare-ups, despite being on a maximum tolerated dose of febuxostat for more than two years. Examination revealed inflammation, pain, and redness in the right first metatarsophalangeal joint. Laboratory results indicated serum uric acid levels of 10.8 mg/dL (within normal range being 3.4-7.2 mg/dL), negative rheumatoid factor, and a normal C-reactive protein (CRP) level. Radiographic images suggested chronic tophaceous gouty arthropathy.

Diagnosis:
The patient's history of recurrent acute gout attacks, combined with lab results and radiographic evidence, led to the diagnosis of treatment-resistant gout.

Treatment Strategy:
Given the patient's resistance to febuxostat, Allopurinol therapy was initiated at a dose of 300 mg daily, with gradual dosage increases to mitigate potential allergic reactions or side effects. Simultaneously, colchicine and non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) were administered for symptomatic relief during gout flare-ups.
