Title: Effective Management Strategies for Severe Pemphigus Vulgaris in an Elderly Individual: A Case Study

Introduction

In the realm of autoimmune diseases, Pemphigus vulgaris (PV) stands as a rare and complex disorder characterized by epidermal cell disintegration and antibody production against desmoglein 3 and 1. Typically afflicting middle-aged adults, the elderly population often remains underrepresented in existing literature due to its relatively low incidence. The ensuing discussion presents an unconventional instance of severe PV in an elder patient and the successful management approach adopted.

Case Description:

An octogenarian Caucasian female, a resident of our dermatology clinic, had endured three weeks of agonizing mucocutaneous bullous lesions and erosions on her oral mucosa, nasolabial folds, and extremities. Upon physical examination, multiple limp bullae with hemorrhagic crusts were detected, congruent with PV (Figure 1). A skin biopsy subjected to direct immunofluorescence revealed intercellular IgG deposits (Figure 2), thereby validating the diagnosis. The patient's medical history bore complications such as hypertension, diabetes mellitus, and chronic kidney disease.

Investigative Procedures:

Considering the patient's age and atypical manifestation of symptoms, an extensive workup ensued to rule out other potential causes of bullous lesions, including epidermolysis bullosa acquisita, bullous pemphigoid, and linear IgA dermatosis. Routine laboratory tests, including complete blood count (CBC), comprehensive metabolic panel (CMP), and an autoimmune panel, yielded no significant findings. Indirect immunofluorescence testing positively identified anti-desmoglein 3 and 1 antibodies, further substantiating the diagnosis of PV.
