Title: Understanding Deep Q-Networks (DQNs) and Their Application in Decision-Making Games

Deep Q-Networks (DQNs), a branch of Artificial Intelligence (AI), enable agents to make informed decisions by engaging with their surroundings. The objective is to optimize a long-term, cumulative score through trial-and-error interactions within the environment. DQN stands out as one of the most effective strategies in this domain due to its integration of Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) and Q-learning, a value estimation technique.

The fundamental concept behind DQNs revolves around Q(s, a), which signifies the anticipated reward associated with selecting action 'a' in state 's'. The aim is to discover the optimal Q-function that maximizes the total reward.

Using mathematical notation, DQNs can be illustrated via the Bellman equation:

Q(s,a) = Σ[r*R(s,a,s')*(1 - γ)^n] + γ * max_a' Q(s', a')

In this equation,
- s and s' denote states,
- a and a' represent actions,
- r symbolizes the instantaneous reward,
- R(s,a,s') refers to the total reward from state 's' to 's', resulting from action 'a',
- γ (0 < γ < 1) represents the discount factor that emphasizes immediate rewards over future rewards.

Within DQNs, the Q-function is approximated by a neural network. The agent engages with the environment, gathers experiences (state, action, reward, subsequent state), and adjusts the neural network weights based on the Bellman equation through a learning process called temporal difference learning:

Q(s_t, a_t) ≤ Q(s_t, a_t) + α * [r_t + γ * max_a' Q(s_(t+1), a') - Q(s_t, a_t)]

Here,
- α symbolizes the learning rate.
