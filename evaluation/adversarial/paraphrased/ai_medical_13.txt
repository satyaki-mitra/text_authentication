Title: An Illustrative Example of Effective Management of Intractable Esophageal Varix Bleeding via Transjugular Intrahepatic Portosystemic Shunt (TIPS) Procedure in a Patient with Cirrhosis

Introduction: Esophageal varix bleeding is a potentially lethal consequence of portal hypertension, frequently encountered in individuals afflicted by liver cirrhosis. Despite the progression of endoscopic and pharmaceutical treatments, managing intractable esophageal varix hemorrhage remains an arduous task. Transjugular intrahepatic portosystemic shunt (TIPS) has gained recognition as a potential lifesaver in such cases.

Case Scenario: A 62-year-old male patient, with a history of liver cirrhosis caused by alcohol abuse and classified as Child-Pugh Class B, was admitted due to hematemesis and melena, symptoms indicative of esophageal varix bleeding. Despite multiple endoscopic sclerotherapy sessions, the patient continued to experience recurrent bleeding. Upon admission, his Model for End-stage Liver Disease (MELD) score was 18, signifying a high mortality risk.

Investigative Procedures: An initial esophagogastroduodenoscopy confirmed the presence of enlarged esophageal varices and active bleeding. A CT scan with contrast revealed severe portal hypertension, while an angiography further depicted dilated esophageal veins and a portal pressure gradient of 20 mmHg, confirming intractable esophageal varix hemorrhage.

Therapeutic Intervention: Given the patient's resistance to endoscopic therapy and high MELD score, it was decided to undertake a TIPS procedure. The operation was successful, resulting in a decrease of the portal pressure gradient to 12 mmHg. Post-procedural care included antibiotics for infection prevention and vigilant monitoring for hepatic dysfunction-induced encephalopathy.

Results: Following TIPS implantation, the patient's bleeding ceased
