Title: Management of Persistent Pericarditis in Adolescence: A Case Study Employing High-Dose Colchicine

Introduction:
Recurrent pericarditis, particularly in adolescent patients, can present a formidable challenge due to the potential for distressing and debilitating symptoms. Conventional treatment options may be hindered by side effects, thus necessitating exploration of alternative therapies. This case study presents an effective management strategy using high-dose colchicine in an adolescent afflicted with persistent pericarditis.

Case Presentation:
A 14-year-old male experienced recurring bouts of chest pain, fever, and shortness of breath over a six-month period. Upon physical examination, a pericardial rub was audible, and an electrocardiogram (ECG) displayed diffuse ST-segment elevation and PR depression, indicative of acute pericarditis. Echocardiography confirmed a thickened pericardium, but revealed no signs of tamponade or constrictive pericarditis. Initially treated with nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) and colchicine, the symptoms persisted, prompting the administration of corticosteroids due to the severity of symptoms. Nevertheless, the patient continued to experience recurrent pericarditis episodes, necessitating multiple hospitalizations.

Treatment Alteration:
Given the ineffectiveness of traditional therapies and potential long-term side effects from corticosteroids, a switch to high-dose colchicine (2 mg/kg/day divided into doses) was initiated. After one week, the patient reported marked improvement in symptoms, and follow-up ECG showed resolution of ST-segment elevation and PR depression. Over the subsequent six months, the patient remained symptom-free, with no further pericarditis episodes, while maintained on colchicine monotherapy.
