Title: Assessment and Enhancement of a High-Density Data Center Cooling System Utilizing Cryogenic Direct-to-Chip Technology

Summary:
This technical document delves into the design, functionality, and optimization of a state-of-the-art cooling system for high-density data centers, employing Cryogenic Direct-to-chip (CDTC) technology. This innovative system utilizes liquid nitrogen as a refrigerant to tackle the escalating thermal issues in modern data centers.

1. System Components:

The CDTC system comprises various integral parts: liquid nitrogen storage tanks, conduits, cold plates, heat exchangers, and precision air conditioning units (PACUs). The objective is to deliver optimal cooling for high-density computing apparatus by minimizing temperature disparities and energy waste.

2. Key Parameters:

- Liquid Nitrogen Storage Capacity: 500 kg
- Power Dissipation: 15 kW at a maximum permissible temperature rise of 4°C
- Conduit Length: Approximately 300 meters, featuring a 2-inch insulation layer (R-value = 6.8 m²K/W)
- Cold Plate Heat Transfer Area: 75 square meters, composed of a 1m x 1m grid with 20 units
- PACU Capacity: 30 kW, boasting an efficiency of 4.5 COP (Coefficient of Performance)

3. Evaluation of Performance:

The CDTC system's performance was assessed via computational fluid dynamics (CFD) simulations and practical testing. Findings demonstrated that the system could meet the stipulated cooling requirements while maintaining a temperature rise under 4°C. The CFD analysis highlighted that the conduits experienced thermal dissipation of approximately 12% before reaching the cold plates.

4. Design Limitations:

- Safety Aspects: Ensuring adequate pressure release valves, leak detection systems, and emergency shutoff mechanisms to curtail potential liquid nitrogen leaks and related hazards.
- Efficiency Demands: Maximizing the system's COP while minimizing energy usage and thermal losses during conduit distribution.
