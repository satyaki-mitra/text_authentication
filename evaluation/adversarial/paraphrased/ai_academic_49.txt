Title: Assessing the Efficacy of Cognitive Behavioral Therapy in Alleviating Anxiety Disorders Among Adolescents: A Randomized Clinical Trial

Introduction: Adolescence is a critical phase marked by heightened susceptibility to anxiety disorders, negatively affecting their academic, social, and emotional functioning. Although multiple treatment methodologies exist, there is a pressing need for proven effective strategies tailored to this age group. This study aimed to explore the potential of Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT) in managing anxiety symptoms among adolescents.

Research Design: A randomized clinical trial was executed involving 150 participants aged between 12 and 18, diagnosed with an anxiety disorder according to DSM-5 criteria. Participants were randomly distributed into either the CBT group or a waiting control group. The CBT group received 16 weekly one-on-one therapy sessions administered by licensed therapists skilled in CBT practices. Primary evaluation metrics included the Anxiety Disorders Interview Schedule for DSM-5 (ADIS-5) and the State-Trait Anxiety Inventory for Children (STAIC). Statistical analysis employed intention-to-treat principles, with repeated measures Analysis of Variance (ANOVA) used to assess both intragroup and intergroup changes.

Main Results: Post-therapy assessment revealed that the CBT group exhibited substantial reductions in anxiety symptoms relative to the waiting control group (F(1, 148) = 23.76, p < .001). The effect size was considerable (d = 1.23), suggesting a significant enhancement in managing anxiety for the CBT group. Follow-up assessments at three and six months post-therapy demonstrated sustained improvements within the CBT group, with no notable changes observed among the waiting control group.

Conclusions: The results of this study substantiate the effectiveness of CBT as a viable intervention to alleviate anxiety symptoms among adolescents. The substantial effect size and persistent improvements underscore the potential value of integrating CBT into existing treatment options for this demographic.
