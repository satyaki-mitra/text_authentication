Title: Successful Management of Recalcitrant Angioedema in a Pediatric Patient through Ecallantide Administration

Introduction:
Angioedema, a condition marked by the rapid expansion of deep tissue layers, is characterized by its association with the bradykinin-mediated pathway. While usually self-limiting, severe and persistent cases can lead to significant morbidity and mortality risks, particularly in the pediatric population. This report details the successful management of a challenging angioedema episode in a 9-year-old patient, utilizing ecallantide – an FDA-approved plasma kallikrein inhibitor.

Case Description:
A 9-year-old boy with no reported allergies presented to the emergency department due to acute facial swelling that had escalated over a 24-hour period. The swelling affected the tongue, lips, and eyelids, accompanied by difficulties in swallowing, pain while swallowing, and labored breathing. The patient's medical history was marked by recurrent angioedema episodes triggered by emotional stress, physical exertion, or upper respiratory infections. Standard treatments such as antihistamines, corticosteroids, and C1 esterase inhibitor concentrate showed limited effectiveness.

Diagnostic Methodology:
The patient's symptoms matched those of angioedema. Laboratory results indicated normal levels of complement components C3 and C4, suggesting a non-hereditary angioedema (NHAE) caused by an enhanced plasma kallikrein activity rather than a deficiency in C1 esterase inhibitor.

Intervention:
Given the patient's refractory response to conventional treatments, ecallantide was administered intracutaneously at doses of 30 milligrams every eight hours. Swelling began to recede within four hours following treatment, and complete resolution occurred after 24 hours.
