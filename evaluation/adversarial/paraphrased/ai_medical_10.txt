Title: Unconventional Management of Acute Myeloid Leukemia in an Elderly Female Patient Yields Unexpected Results: A Case Study on FLAG-IDA Chemotherapy Regimen

Summary

Acute myelogenous leukemia (AML), a malignancy of blood cells, is marked by the uncontrolled multiplication of abnormal myeloblasts, leading to disrupted hematopoiesis. The prevalence of AML significantly rises with age, and finding optimal treatment strategies for elderly patients is challenging due to their heightened vulnerability to chemotherapy adverse effects. This case study examines an unusual response to the FLAG-IDA (Fludarabine, Cytarabine, Idarubicin, and Ara-C) chemotherapeutic regimen in a 80-year-old woman diagnosed with previously untreated AML.

Case Outline:

An elderly Caucasian woman, aged 80, visited the emergency department due to two weeks of fatigue, recurrent respiratory infections, and easy bleeding. Upon examination, signs of paleness, bruises, and an enlarged spleen were observed. Laboratory tests indicated leukocytosis (white blood cell count: 45.6 x10^9/L), anemia (hemoglobin: 7.8 g/dL), and thrombocytopenia (platelet count: 30 x10^9/L). Bone marrow aspiration and biopsy confirmed AML with a high blast percentage (85%). Cytogenetic analysis revealed the presence of t(8;21)(q22;q22), a favorable prognostic indicator.
