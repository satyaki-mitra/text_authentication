Title: Efficient Management of Debilitating Psoriatic Arthritis Through Biologic Therapy - Adalimumab: An Illustrative Case

Discussion and Case Study

In this narrative, we focus on a noteworthy instance of psoriatic arthritis (PsA) management using biologic therapy, specifically Adalimumab. PsA is an ongoing inflammatory joint condition that occurs concurrently with psoriasis and impacts around 0.3-1% of the general populace. This disease can present as a diverse spectrum of symptoms ranging from mild joint discomfort to severe polyarthritis, causing substantial suffering and reduced quality of life (QoL).

Case Description: A 45-year-old male of Caucasian descent presented with a decade-long history of psoriasis lesions on his elbows, knees, and scalp. Over the past three years, he has experienced worsening polyarthritis characterized by prolonged morning stiffness, swollen and painful joints, lethargy, and reduced mobility in both hands and feet. The patient's condition was severely debilitating, with a modified Health Assessment Questionnaire (mHAQ) score of 2.5, indicating considerable disability. Laboratory investigations indicated elevated levels of erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR), C-reactive protein (CRP), and rheumatoid factor (RF). Radiographic examinations confirmed joint space narrowing, bone erosion, and osteophyte formation typical of PsA. A skin biopsy affirmed the presence of psoriasis.

Diagnostic Procedure: With the patient's history of psoriasis, joint symptoms, and lab results in mind, a diagnosis of PsA was made based on Classification Criteria for Psoriatic Arthritis (CASPAR) and American College of Rheumatology (ACR)/European League Against Rheumatism (EULAR) guidelines.
