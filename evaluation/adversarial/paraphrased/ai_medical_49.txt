Title: Effective Management of Recalcitrant Angina Pectoris via Coronary Artery Bypass Surgery (CABG) - A Case Study

Introduction:
In cardiology, recalcitrant angina pectoris (RAP), marked by persistent chest pain at rest despite optimal medication, poses a substantial challenge. Despite advancements in drug therapies and percutaneous coronary interventions (PCI), a considerable number of patients still experience RAP, necessitating innovative treatments. This case report demonstrates the successful management of recalcitrant angina pectoris using Coronary Artery Bypass Grafting (CABG).

Case Description:
A 65-year-old male patient endured recurrent bouts of chest pain at rest, shortness of breath, and orthopnea over a six-month period. The patient's medical history encompassed hypertension, diabetes mellitus, and a three-vessel coronary artery disease (CAD), which had been treated with PCI two years prior. Upon examination, the patient exhibited symptoms indicative of congestive heart failure (CHF). Echocardiography revealed a left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) of 40%, regional wall motion abnormalities, and moderate-to-severe mitral regurgitation. The patient was under optimal medical management that included beta-blockers, nitrates, ACE inhibitors, and antiplatelet agents. However, the chest pain persisted, and the patient continued to experience acute episodes of decompensated heart failure.

Diagnostic Procedures:
Given the refractory nature of the angina, a cardiac catheterization was undertaken, confirming significant restenosis in the left anterior descending (LAD) artery and the right coronary artery (RCA). The patient's high surgical risk score instigated a multidisciplinary consultation, leading to the decision that CABG would provide the best possible relief from symptoms and enhancement of quality of life.

Treatment Procedure:
The patient underwent off-pump CABG with revascularization of the LAD and RCA.
