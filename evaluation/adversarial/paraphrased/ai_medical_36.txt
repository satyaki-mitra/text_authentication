Title: An Unconventional Management Strategy for Intractable Seizures in a Pediatric Patient: A Combination of Levetiracetam and Ketogenic Diet

Introduction:
Uncontrollable seizures, known as refractory status epilepticus (RSE), pose a significant neurological crisis. Characterized by persistent episodes that resist standard anticonvulsant medications, RSE carries high morbidity and mortality rates. This case study details an unusual treatment approach for a pediatric patient suffering from RSE, employing Levetiracetam and the Ketogenic Diet to achieve seizure control.

Case Description:
A 7-year-old boy was admitted to the emergency department, having endured three days of recurrent generalized tonic-clonic seizures. Despite vigorous pharmaceutical intervention involving midazolam and phenytoin, the seizures persisted, meeting criteria for RSE. A magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) scan revealed bilateral hippocampal sclerosis, hinting at a potential structural cause. Given the intractable nature of the seizures and the patient's underlying neurological condition, it was decided to introduce Levetiracetam as adjunctive therapy and to initiate a Ketogenic Diet.

Within 48 hours of implementing these measures, there was a marked decrease in the frequency of seizures. The Ketogenic Diet was maintained over subsequent months, with regular medication adjustments to maintain seizure control. After three months of combined therapy, the patient was free from seizures and able to discontinue all anticonvulsant medications except for Levetiracetam, which has since provided sustained seizure control.

Discussion:
This case study underscores an uncommon yet promising management strategy for RSE. The combination of Levetiracetam and the Ketogenic Diet may offer an alternative approach for pediatric patients with treatment-resistant epilepsy. Further investigation is needed to understand the underlying mechanisms driving this therapeutic synergy
