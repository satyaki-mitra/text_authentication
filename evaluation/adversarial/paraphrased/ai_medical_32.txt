Title: Successfully Managing Recalcitrant Psoriasis through Ustekinumab Treatment: A Case Study

Introduction

The skin disorder under examination in this study is psoriasis, an immune-driven chronic inflammation that accelerates the growth of keratinocytes, leading to thick, scaly, erythematous plaques. This article presents a case of a treatment-resistant psoriasis patient who experienced success with Ustekinumab, a medication that inhibits interleukins (IL)-12 and IL-23.

Case Illustration: A 48-year-old male struggled with extensive plaque psoriasis affecting approximately 80% of his body for three years prior to this study. Prior treatments, including topical corticosteroids, phototherapy, methotrexate, and adalimumab, alleviated symptoms temporarily but did not achieve full remission. The patient's quality of life was negatively impacted by severe itching, discomfort, and social distress. Examination revealed thick, erythematous, scaly plaques on the scalp, elbows, knees, and torso, as well as nail psoriasis in multiple digits. The Dermatology Life Quality Index (DLQI) score was 24 out of 30, reflecting severe impairment.

Diagnostic Procedures: The diagnosis of psoriasis was established based on the distinct clinical appearance, familial history, and histopathological findings of parakeratosis, acanthosis, and Munro's microabscesses. The Psoriasis Area and Severity Index (PASI) score was 46 out of 72, indicating severe psoriasis.

Treatment Strategy: Due to the patient's resistance to conventional therapies, Ustekinumab treatment was initiated at a dosage of 45 mg per kilogram body weight, administered subcutaneously every twelve weeks.
