Title: Efficient Alleviation of Intractable Angina Pectoris through Coronary Artery Bypass Surgery

Introduction:
The management of Intractable Angina Pectoris (IAP), a recalcitrant clinical condition marked by persistent chest pain despite maximum medical therapy, poses significant challenges. This case study showcases the effective treatment of such a patient through Coronary Artery Bypass Surgery (CABS).

Case Description:
A 65-year-old male, non-smoker and diabetic type 2 patient with hypertension, was experiencing recurrent chest pain despite optimal medication. His symptoms were classified as Canadian Cardiovascular Society (CCS) class IV, and an electrocardiogram (ECG) revealed prolonged ST-segment depression in the anterior leads. Echocardiography demonstrated severe three-vessel Coronary Artery Disease (CAD) with a left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) of 40%. Given these findings, he was diagnosed with IAP due to extensive CAD and refractory symptoms despite optimal medical management.

Diagnostic Procedure:
Given the stubborn nature of the patient's symptoms, an invasive diagnostic procedure was initiated. Coronary angiography confirmed the presence of severe three-vessel CAD with the left anterior descending (LAD) and circumflex arteries showing 90% and 80% blockage respectively, while the right coronary artery (RCA) was completely blocked.

Treatment Strategy:
A surgical intervention through Coronary Artery Bypass Surgery (CABS) was planned for the patient. During surgery, a left internal mammary artery (LIMA) graft was connected to the LAD, and saphenous vein grafts were used to bypass the occluded RCA and the 80% blockage in the circumflex artery.
