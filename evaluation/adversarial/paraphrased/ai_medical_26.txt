Title: Managing Intractable Seizures in a Juvenile Subject Through Elevated Levetiracetam Dosage: A Clinical Illustration

Introduction:
The case study at hand delves into the successful application of high-dose levetiracetam to manage persistent seizures of unyielding nature (referred to as refractory status epilepticus, or RSE) in a 7-year-old male patient. This life-threatening neurological crisis is marked by prolonged and uncontrollable seizure activity that persists despite standard antiepileptic drug (AED) therapy, posing substantial challenges to pediatric patients due to their developing central nervous system and distinctive pharmacokinetics.

Case Exposition:
The presented patient, a formerly healthy young boy, was admitted to the emergency department, reporting a 3-hour duration of tonic-clonic seizures. Despite initial treatment involving lorazepam and phenytoin, his seizure episodes persisted, fulfilling the criteria for RSE. Given his tender age and ongoing seizure activity, high-dose levetiracetam was initiated at 100 mg/kg daily, administered in three separate doses. The patient's seizure activity ceased within hours of the initial dose, allowing for discharge on a maintenance dose of 60 mg/kg per day.

Diagnostic Procedure:
The diagnosis of RSE was based on the International League Against Epilepsy (ILAE) criteria, which encompass continuous seizure activity lasting over five minutes or two or more seizures without regaining consciousness between episodes. The diagnostic workup comprised a detailed metabolic panel, complete blood count, toxicology screening, and electroencephalogram (EEG). Given the patient's stable clinical status and positive response to treatment, brain imaging was postponed.
