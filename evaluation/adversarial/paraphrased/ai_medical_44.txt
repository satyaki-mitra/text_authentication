Title: Success Story of Overcoming Persistent Pemphigus Vulgaris with Rituximab

Introduction:
Pemphigus vulgaris (PV), a seldom-encountered autoimmune blistering ailment, is characterized by epidermal acantholysis. This condition often results in debilitating mucocutaneous fissures. The standard treatment approach employs immunosuppressive agents like corticosteroids, azathioprine, and mycophenolate mofetil; however, these treatments frequently yield substantial side effects and may prove insufficient for refractory instances. Rituximab, a chimeric monoclonal antibody targeting CD20, has displayed potential in treating PV due to its capacity to eliminate B cells responsible for manufacturing pathogenic autoantibodies.

Patient Profile and Initial Symptoms:
A 64-year-old male of Caucasian descent presented with a two-month history of painful oral and nasal sores, accompanied by eruptions on the torso, limbs, and genitalia. Histological analysis confirmed epidermal acantholysis, diagnosing PV. Despite aggressive treatment with high-dose prednisone, azathioprine, and dapsone, the patient's condition deteriorated significantly, leading to severe mucosal involvement and discomfort during eating and drinking.

Diagnostic Procedures:
To confirm the diagnosis of PV, direct immunofluorescence (DIF) analysis was performed, which identified IgG and C3 deposits at the epidermis-dermis junction. Additionally, enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) revealed the presence of autoantibodies against desmoglein 1 and 3 in the circulatory system.
