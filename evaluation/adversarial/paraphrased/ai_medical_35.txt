Title: Effective Control of Intractable Gastric Paralysis: A Case Study Employing Botulinum Neurotoxin Type A

Introduction: The gastrointestinal condition, gastric paralysis - characterized by prolonged gastric emptying devoid of mechanical blockage - poses a formidable challenge for healthcare providers due to its intricate etiology and recalcitrant nature. This report documents the successful management of a patient suffering from intractable gastric paralysis through the administration of Botulinum Neurotoxin Type A (BoNT-A) injections.

Case Study: A 45-year-old female, diagnosed with type 1 diabetes mellitus three decades ago, presented with chronic postprandial nausea, vomiting, and abdominal discomfort persisting for half a year. Her medical history included gastric paralysis, initially treated with dietary adjustments, stimulant drugs, and anti-vomiting medication; however, these interventions yielded minimal improvement. Upon examination, her weight was 50 kg (BMI: 19.8), blood glucose level hovered around 160 mg/dL, and abdominal palpation revealed mild tenderness in the upper abdomen with no signs of distension or muscle tension. Further gastrointestinal investigations including an upper GI series, gastric emptying scan, and endoscopy revealed only delayed gastric emptying as notable findings. Based on her symptoms and test results, a diagnosis of intractable gastric paralysis was established.

Diagnostic Procedure: The patient underwent an endoscopic ultrasound (EUS) guided BoNT-A injection procedure. Employing real-time ultrasound imaging, 100 units of BoNT-A were injected into the pyloric sphincter and proximal antrum.
