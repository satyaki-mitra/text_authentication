Title: Examining the Influence of Enhanced Habitats on Cognitive Growth in Young Rhesus Macaques (Macaca mulatta)

Introduction: The exploration of cognitive growth in non-human primates has been pivotal in furthering our comprehension of human cognition and brain functionality. This study aims to scrutinize the impact of enriched environments on the cognitive development of young rhesus macaques (Macaca mulatta). Previous research indicates that stimulating surroundings can heighten problem-solving skills, spatial recall, and social behavior in numerous species; however, the consequences for juvenile rhesus macaques have predominantly remained uncharted.

Research Design: A total of forty young rhesus macaques were arbitrarily divided into two groups: those in an enriched environment (EEG, n=20) and those in standard accommodations (SHG, n=20). The EEG was furnished with spacious cages containing assorted items for manipulation and exploration, while the SHG contained standard living quarters. Cognitive development was gauged using tasks evaluating problem-solving aptitude, spatial memory, and social engagement. Data were accumulated over a span of twelve months, with statistical analyses being conducted via mixed-effects models to account for multiple measures and individual disparities among subjects.

Primary Findings: The data revealed significant enhancements in cognitive performance among young rhesus macaques residing in enriched environments relative to those in standard accommodations. Specifically, monkeys within the EEG demonstrated superior problem-solving capabilities (F(1,37)=8.20, p<0.01), augmented spatial memory (F(1,37)=6.94, p<0.05), and heightened social engagement (F(1,37)=10.52, p<0.01). These observations indicate that enriched surroundings can stimulate cognitive development in young rhesus macaques across various aspects.
