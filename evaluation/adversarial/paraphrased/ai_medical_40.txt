Title: Managing Severe Acute Pancreatitis Successfully: A Case Study on Prophylactic Plasminogen Activator and Early Enteral Nutrition

Introduction:
Inflammatory conditions of the pancreas, particularly severe acute pancreatitis (SAP), are life-threatening, often leading to high morbidity and mortality rates. Traditional management practices center on supportive care, fluid resuscitation, pain relief, and nutritional support. However, there is a persistent need for more effective interventions, driving ongoing research in this field.

Case Description:
A 58-year-old man visited the emergency department, reporting persistent epigastric abdominal pain that extended to his back, accompanied by vomiting and fever for four days. Laboratory tests showed a serum amylase level of 1,600 IU/L (normal range: 25-140 IU/L) and lipase level of 3,000 IU/L (normal range: 13-60 IU/L), suggestive of pancreatitis. A computed tomography scan confirmed SAP with necrotizing features.

Diagnostic Strategy:
Given the critical state of the patient, comprehensive diagnostic assessments were carried out to determine the extent of organ damage and possible complications. Transabdominal ultrasonography revealed pancreatic necrosis. Magnetic resonance cholangiopancreatography (MRCP) was employed to rule out potential causes like gallstones or tumors.

Treatment Procedures:
The patient received prophylactic alteplase, a plasminogen activator, promptly to prevent pancreatic necrosis-related complications such as infection and abscess formation. Early enteral nutrition (EN) was initiated within 24 hours of admission to minimize catabolic stress, preserve gut integrity, and foster recovery. Pain management involved the use of opioids and non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs).
