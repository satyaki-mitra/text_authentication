Title: Evaluating the Effects of Environmental Enhancement on Cognitive Abilities in Adolescent Rhesus Monkeys (Macaca mulatta)

Introduction: The cognitive abilities of non-human primates, particularly adolescent rhesus monkeys (Macaca mulatta), have garnered significant attention due to their evolutionary relationship with humans and comparable cognitive aptitudes. However, the effects of environmental enhancement on their cognitive development remain unexplored. This study aimed to evaluate the impact of environmental enhancement on the cognitive capabilities of adolescent rhesus monkeys.

Methods: A total of 48 adolescent rhesus monkeys were divided into two groups: an Enriched Environment Group (EEG, n=24) and a Standard Environment Group (SEG, n=24). The EEG was supplied with enrichment elements such as mirrors, puzzle solvers, and social companions, while the SEG inhabited conventional barren cages. Cognitive capabilities were measured using object permanence tests and problem-solving trials. Data were analyzed using a mixed-effects model, with environment group as a fixed variable and individual monkey serving as a random factor.

Results: The findings revealed substantial enhancements in cognitive abilities among adolescent rhesus monkeys housed in enriched environments compared to those in standard environments (F(1, 46) = 23.79, p<0.001). Specifically, the EEG demonstrated a higher success rate in object permanence tests and problem-solving trials, suggesting improved memory retention and problem-solving skills.

Discussion: These results underscore the positive impact of environmental enhancement on cognitive development in adolescent rhesus monkeys. The study emphasizes the importance of supplying cognitively stimulating environments to non-human primates in captivity, as it may potentially improve their wellbeing and contribute to our comprehension of human cognitive development.
