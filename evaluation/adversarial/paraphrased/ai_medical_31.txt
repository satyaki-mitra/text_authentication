Title: Successful Management of Severe Acute Pancreatitis: A Case Study Employing Early Endoscopic Retrograde Cholangiopancreatography (ERCP)

Introduction:
Inflammation of the pancreas, known as acute pancreatitis (AP), is a prevalent condition, characterized by injury to acinar cells and self-digestion of the pancreas. Severe cases account for approximately 20% of all instances, with a high fatality rate if not diagnosed and managed promptly.

Case Report:
A 58-year-old male patient, experiencing intense epigastric abdominal pain spreading to his back for three days, accompanied by nausea, vomiting, and fever, sought emergency care. Blood tests indicated increased serum amylase (1200 U/L; normal range: 25-140 U/L) and lipase (780 U/L; normal range: 13-60 U/L). The white blood cell count was 16.5 x 10³/μL (normal range: 4.5–11.0 x 10³/μL), C-reactive protein (CRP) level was 238 mg/L (normal range: <5 mg/L), and liver enzymes, aspartate transaminase (AST) and alanine transaminase (ALT), were elevated at 164 U/L (normal range: 10–40 U/L) and 229 U/L (normal range: 8–50 U/L), respectively. Ultrasound revealed a swollen pancreas with peripancreatic fluid collection, indicative of severe acute pancreatitis (SAP). Admitted to the intensive care unit for observation and aggressive supportive treatment, the patient's condition was closely monitored.
