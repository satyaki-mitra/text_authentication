Title: Assessing the Effectiveness of Non-Pharmacological Intervention for Depression in Adolescents: A Randomized Controlled Trial

Introduction: Mental health issues, particularly depression, are prevalent among young adults aged between 13 and 18. These disorders significantly impact emotional well-being, academic performance, and future prospects. Conventional pharmacological treatments for adolescent depression have shown limited success and potential side effects, necessitating the exploration of alternative therapeutic strategies. This study evaluates Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT) as a viable non-pharmacological approach to managing depression symptoms in young adults.

Research Methodology: A randomized controlled trial was conducted involving 250 adolescents diagnosed with major depressive disorder according to the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders (DSM-5). The participants were randomly assigned to either a CBT intervention group or a control group that would wait for treatment. The CBT group underwent 16 weekly sessions, each lasting an hour, conducted by licensed psychologists specializing in adolescent mental health. Depression symptoms were measured using the Hamilton Rating Scale for Depression (HRSD) at three timepoints: pre-intervention, post-intervention, and follow-up assessments at 3 and 6 months.

Key Findings: Post-intervention, participants in the CBT group showed a statistically significant reduction in depression symptoms compared to the control group (t(248)=10.79, p<0.001). This advantage was sustained at both follow-up assessments, with the CBT group continuing to display lower HRSD scores than the control group (F(2, 246)=57.32, p<0.001). The findings indicate that CBT is an effective intervention for reducing depression symptoms in young adults and may serve as a valuable alternative or complement to pharmacological treatments.

Conclusions: This study highlights the potential of CBT as a promising non-pharmacological approach for treating depression in young adults. By addressing maladaptive thought patterns and behaviors, CBT may offer a more targeted and personalized approach compared to traditional pharmacological interventions.
