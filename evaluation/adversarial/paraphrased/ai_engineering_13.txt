Title: Evaluation and Optimization of a Decentralized Energy Storage System for Grid Stabilization Purposes

Overview:

This technical document delves into the assessment of a distributed energy storage system's (DESS) design and functionality, specifically engineered for grid stabilization purposes. The DESS, incorporating lithium-ion battery banks and power conversion units, is tactically deployed across multiple grid points to deliver reactive power assistance, frequency regulation, and voltage control services.

System Architecture:

The DESS consists of 126 interconnected modular battery modules, each with a 500 kWh capacity, regulated via bidirectional DC-DC converters. The total system capacity amounts to 63 MWh, operating at a voltage rating of 800 V DC within an environmental temperature range spanning from -20°C to +40°C. For thermal management during high discharge rates or temperature fluctuations, each battery module is equipped with a liquid cooling system.

The power conversion units are optimized for minimal energy loss (less than 3%), facilitating bi-directional energy transfer between the battery banks and the grid, supporting both charging and discharging operations. Each conversion unit connects to two battery modules, offering redundancy in case of component failure.

Evaluation:

Under standard operating conditions, the DESS is designed to react swiftly (within milliseconds) to grid anomalies such as voltage drops or surges, frequency fluctuations, and power imbalances. The system's response speed has been corroborated through simulations and laboratory tests, underscoring its high-performance capabilities.

To quantify the DESS's impact on grid stability, we scrutinized its capacity to provide ancillary services:

1. Frequency Regulation: The DESS can swiftly adjust power output to counterbalance load shifts and generation losses, thereby sustaining a consistent grid frequency within tolerable limits (±0.5 Hz).
2. Voltage Control: By supplying or absorbing reactive power as required, the DESS contributes to maintaining the grid voltage at the designated level (13.8 kV in our case study).
3. Reactive Power Support: The system can offer immediate reactive power support during voltage drops, thereby mitigating the severity and duration of such events.
