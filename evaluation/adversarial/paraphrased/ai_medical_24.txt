Title: Managing Acute Pancreatitis Successfully Through Early Intervention with Endoscopic Retrograde Cholangiopancreatography (ERCP) in an Unusual Case

Introduction: Acute pancreatitis, a frequent inflammation of the pancreas within the gastrointestinal system, can vary in severity from a mild, self-resolving condition to life-threatening complications. Early identification and prompt management are key to achieving favorable results. The following case report describes an atypical occurrence of acute pancreatitis in a young individual who benefited significantly from early ERCP intervention.

Case Description: A 32-year-old male sought emergency care due to a one-day onset of intense epigastric abdominal pain that spread to his back, accompanied by nausea and vomiting. He had no substantial past medical history, did not smoke, and consumed alcohol sparingly. On physical examination, the abdomen was sensitive in the upper region with localized rigidity and signs of rebound tenderness. Blood tests showed increased serum amylase (1500 U/L; normal range 26-98 U/L) and lipase (1400 U/L; normal range 13-60 U/L), confirming the diagnosis of acute pancreatitis. Abdominal ultrasound revealed a moderate amount of fluid collection around the pancreas, classified using the Bismuth criteria.

Investigative Procedures: Given his youth and the moderately severe nature of the pancreatitis, an ERCP was carried out within 24 hours of admission to identify any potential biliary issues and to provide therapeutic intervention if required. The ERCP revealed a stone obstructing the common bile duct, causing acute pancreatitis.

Therapeutic Intervention: The common bile duct stone was successfully removed, and a drainage tube was inserted for biliary drainage.
