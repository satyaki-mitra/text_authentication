Title: Examination of a Distributed Energy Management System for Smart Grid Applications

In this technical document, we delve into the examination of a Distributed Energy Management System (DEMS) tailored for smart grid applications. The objective is to maximize energy distribution and utilization within a local network, thereby increasing efficiency, reducing expenses, and bolstering resilience.

Firstly, we provide an overview of the system design. Comprising multiple interlinked energy sources such as solar panels, wind turbines, and energy storage systems (ESS), this DEMS operates on the principle of smart inverters regulating power flow and transmitting data to a central command unit via wireless communication protocols. The latter processes real-time data from the energy sources and ESS for optimal distribution based on supply and demand dynamics.

Secondly, we detail the specifications of the system components:

a) Energy Sources:
   - Solar Panels: Utilizing monocrystalline silicon panels capable of generating 250W peak power, boasting a maximum efficiency of 17%, and an estimated annual output of 418 kWh/kWp under standard conditions.
   - Wind Turbines: Equipped with horizontal-axis turbines offering a rated power of 1.5 kW, a minimum wind speed threshold of 3 m/s, and a maximum cut-off wind speed of 25 m/s.
   - Energy Storage Systems (ESS): Comprising lithium-ion battery banks featuring a capacity of 60 kWh, a round-trip efficiency of 90%, and an anticipated lifespan of around 10 years.

b) Control Unit:
   - Processor: ARM Cortex-M4F microcontroller delivering a clock speed of 96 MHz with 256 KB flash memory for data storage.
   - Communication Interface: Zigbee RF transceiver enabling low-power, mesh networking between devices.
   - Software: Custom software running on the control unit to manage data processing, optimization algorithms, and communication protocols.

Subsequently, we present the performance analysis of the DEMS, which was simulated using a MATLAB script simulating various situations based on real-world energy consumption patterns and weather conditions. The results reveal that the system can meet the daily energy requirements of an average residential neighborhood during daylight hours, storing excess energy for nighttime
