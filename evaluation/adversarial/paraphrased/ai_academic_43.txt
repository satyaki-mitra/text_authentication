Title: Exploring the Link Between Nutritional Plenty and Psychological Well-being Among Adolescents in Developing Regions: A Cross-Sectional Investigation

Introduction: The interplay among nutrition, diet, and psychological health has emerged as a significant area of interest in global healthcare research. In developing regions, where adolescents face unique challenges related to food availability and psychological well-being, understanding the correlation between nutritional range and psychological health is essential. This study aimed to examine the relationship between nutritional variety and psychological health among adolescents in developing regions.

Research Procedure: A cross-sectional investigation was carried out across six diverse areas within four developing countries (Bangladesh, Kenya, Nigeria, and Tanzania). The sample consisted of 3,000 adolescents aged between 12 and 18 years, selected through stratified random sampling. Data collection involved direct interviews using the Household Food Insecurity Access Scale (HFIAS) to assess nutritional variety and the Strengths and Difficulties Questionnaire (SDQ) to evaluate psychological health. The analysis utilized SPSS version 25, involving descriptive statistics, chi-square tests, and multivariate logistic regression analyses.

Significant Findings: The results demonstrated that a higher nutritional variety score was significantly linked with improved psychological well-being outcomes (OR = 1.67; 95% CI: 1.30-2.14). Specifically, adolescents who consumed food from four or more food groups displayed lower odds of experiencing poor psychological health as compared to those consuming fewer than four food groups. Furthermore, the study indicated that food insecurity was significantly associated with poorer psychological well-being (OR = 2.35; 95% CI: 1.76-3.14).

Conclusions and Implications: The findings highlight the importance of fostering nutritional variety and addressing food insecurity to enhance psychological well-being among adolescents in developing regions.
