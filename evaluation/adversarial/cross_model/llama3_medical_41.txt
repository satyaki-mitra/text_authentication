**Title:** A Rare Case of Primary Cutaneous Amyloidosis Presenting as a Large, Lesion-Like Tumor on the Abdomen: A Clinical and Pathological Analysis

**Abstract:**

Primary cutaneous amyloidosis is an uncommon condition characterized by the deposition of insoluble protein fibers in the skin. We report a rare case of primary cutaneous amyloidosis presenting as a large, lesion-like tumor on the abdomen.

A 65-year-old woman presented with a slowly growing mass on her abdomen that had been increasing in size over the past year. The patient reported no relevant medical history and was not taking any medications. Physical examination revealed a 10 cm × 8 cm irregularly shaped mass on the mid-abdomen, which was firm to palpation.

Biopsy specimens from the lesion showed an abundance of amyloid material, as detected by Congo red staining, in addition to a mild inflammatory infiltrate. The histopathological examination revealed deposition of amyloid fibers within the dermal papillae and subcutaneous tissue.

Immunohistochemical analysis showed positivity for lambda light chain, consistent with primary systemic amyloidosis. Systemic evaluation, including echocardiogram, electromyography, and serum protein electrophoresis, was unremarkable.

The patient underwent excision of the lesion, which resulted in significant clinical improvement. The histopathological examination of the resected specimen confirmed the diagnosis of primary cutaneous amyloidosis.

**Discussion:**

Primary cutaneous amyloidosis is a rare condition that typically presents as small, asymptomatic skin lesions. Our case highlights an unusual presentation of this condition as a large tumor-like lesion on the abdomen.

The histopathological and immunohistochemical findings in our case were consistent with primary cutaneous amyloidosis. The absence of any systemic involvement, including cardiac or renal disease, suggests that this was a localized form of the condition.

**Conclusion:**

Primary cutaneous amyloidosis is an uncommon condition that can present in various ways, including as a large tumor-like lesion on the abdomen. A high index of suspicion and thorough histopathological evaluation are essential for accurate diagnosis and effective management.
