**Clinical Abstract:**

**Title:** Rare Case of Cerebral Amyloid Angiopathy Presenting with Reversible Posterior Leukoencephalopathy Syndrome (RPLS)

**Abstract:**

A 75-year-old woman presented to the emergency department with a sudden onset of severe headache, nausea, and confusion. Computed Tomography (CT) scans revealed bilateral white matter hyperintensities consistent with Reversible Posterior Leukoencephalopathy Syndrome (RPLS). The patient's symptoms resolved with supportive care, but subsequent imaging studies revealed an unusual pattern of cerebral amyloid angiopathy (CAA).

**Clinical Course:**

The patient had a 2-day history of severe headache, nausea, and confusion, which worsened over time. Upon admission, she was found to be disoriented, lethargic, and unable to recall her name or recent events. Neurological examination revealed bilateral papilledema and decreased deep tendon reflexes.

**Imaging Findings:**

Initial CT scans demonstrated bilateral white matter hyperintensities in the posterior brain regions, consistent with RPLS. A subsequent Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) scan showed these lesions had resolved by 48 hours after onset of symptoms.

Subsequent imaging revealed multiple small, rounded lesions throughout the cerebral cortex and basal ganglia, characteristic of CAA. These findings were confirmed on a follow-up MRI scan performed 6 months later, which showed no changes in lesion size or number.

**Pathological Findings:**

An autopsy examination was conducted after the patient's passing from unrelated causes. Histopathological analysis revealed numerous small, rounded deposits of beta-amyloid protein within the cerebral vessels, consistent with CAA.

**Discussion:**

This case presents an unusual combination of RPLS and CAA in a 75-year-old woman. The rapid resolution of RPLS symptoms in response to supportive care is consistent with published literature. However, the subsequent development of CAA lesions is unexpected, as these conditions are typically considered separate entities.

The coexistence of these two conditions may indicate an underlying genetic predisposition or shared pathophysiological mechanisms. Further research is needed to understand the relationship between RPLS and CAA and to identify potential common risk factors.

**Conclusion:**

This case highlights the importance of considering rare neurovascular disorders in the differential diagnosis of sudden-onset headache, nausea, and confusion. The coexistence of RPLS and CAA emphasizes the need for a comprehensive diagnostic approach and further research into the underlying mechanisms driving these conditions.
