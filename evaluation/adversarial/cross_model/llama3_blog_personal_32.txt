**Finding My True North: Reflections on Identity, Authenticity, and the Journey**

As I sit here typing away in my quiet morning routine, I'm reminded of the countless times I've questioned who I am and where I belong. It's as if I've been searching for a missing piece of myself, trying to find my true north.

Lately, I've been reflecting on my journey so far – the twists and turns that have led me to this moment. Growing up, I was always the "good girl" who followed rules, did what others expected, and tried to please everyone else before pleasing myself. It wasn't until I hit rock bottom (literally – I had a major breakdown in college) that I realized just how disconnected from my own desires and values I'd become.

That moment marked the beginning of my quest for authenticity. I started exploring who I was outside of what others expected, taking risks to discover new passions and interests. It wasn't always easy; there were many times when self-doubt crept in and I questioned whether I was making a mistake. But with each step forward, I began to realize that my true identity lay not in pleasing others or conforming to societal norms, but in embracing my unique strengths and quirks.

One of the most liberating experiences for me has been learning to say "no" without apology or explanation. It's amazing how empowering it is to set boundaries and prioritize your own needs – even if that means disappointing others. Of course, this doesn't mean I'm perfect; I still struggle with people-pleasing tendencies and fear of missing out (FOMO). But each time I stand up for myself and assert my desires, I feel like I'm one step closer to finding my true north.

In recent months, I've also been exploring the concept of "found family" – a term coined by Brené Brown that refers to the non-biological relationships we cultivate as a source of belonging and support. For me, this has meant nurturing friendships with people who share similar values, passions, and sense of humor. It's incredible how these connections can fill the void left by our biological families or create new ones in situations where we feel lost or disconnected.

As I continue on my journey to self-discovery, I'm reminded that authenticity is a muscle that needs to be exercised regularly. It takes vulnerability, courage, and a willingness to learn from failures and setbacks. But when I do take the time to listen to my intuition, honor my emotions, and prioritize my own growth – well, that's when I feel most like myself.

In closing, I want to leave you with a quote from Maya Angelou: "You may not control all the events that happen to you, but you can decide not to be reduced by them." As we navigate our own unique journeys, let us strive to find our true north – not in pleasing others or conforming to expectations, but in embracing our quirks, passions, and deepest desires.
