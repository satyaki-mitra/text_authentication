**Case Report:**

**Title:** Unusual Presentation of Cervical Spinal Cord Compression Due to a Large Thyroid Nodule

**Abstract:**

A 62-year-old woman presented to our emergency department with sudden onset of bilateral leg weakness, numbness, and pain radiating from her legs to her feet. She had no prior history of neurological disorders or trauma. Physical examination revealed decreased strength in both legs (Grade 3/5), loss of deep tendon reflexes, and sensory loss below the knees.

Imaging studies, including computed tomography (CT) and magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), revealed a large thyroid nodule compressing the cervical spinal cord at the C4-C5 level. The nodule measured approximately 6 cm in diameter and had calcified features suggestive of a papillary thyroid carcinoma.

The patient underwent emergency decompression surgery, which involved excision of the thyroid nodule and posterior laminectomy to relieve the spinal cord compression. Postoperatively, the patient received radiation therapy to address any potential residual tumor cells.

**Clinical Course:**

The patient made a remarkable recovery, with significant improvement in her leg strength (Grade 4/5) and sensory function within weeks following surgery. She was able to ambulate independently and regain most of her pre-morbid functional status.

**Discussion:**

Cervical spinal cord compression is often caused by degenerative disc disease, herniated discs, or osteophytes. However, in this case, a large thyroid nodule presenting as a cervical spine tumor is an unusual occurrence. The diagnosis was confirmed by imaging studies and histopathological examination of the resected lesion.

Thyroid nodules are common, but compressive lesions of the spinal cord are rare. This case highlights the importance of considering uncommon causes of spinal cord compression in patients with unexplained neurological symptoms. A high index of suspicion and prompt imaging studies can lead to timely diagnosis and treatment, ultimately improving patient outcomes.

**Conclusion:**

This unusual presentation of cervical spinal cord compression due to a large thyroid nodule underscores the need for clinicians to consider rare but treatable causes of spinal cord compression in patients with unexplained neurological symptoms. Early recognition and prompt intervention can significantly improve patient outcomes.

**Key Words:** Cervical Spinal Cord Compression, Thyroid Nodule, Papillary Thyroid Carcinoma, Decompression Surgery.
