Title: Rare Case of Malignant Rhabdomyosarcoma of the Cervical Spine Presenting as Neck Pain and Weakness in an Adult Male

Abstract:

A 42-year-old male presented to our institution with a 2-month history of progressive neck pain, stiffness, and weakness. Initially, he was treated for a presumed musculoskeletal strain, but his symptoms persisted and worsened. He reported no previous trauma or injury to the neck.

Physical examination revealed limited range of motion in flexion and extension, as well as diminished muscle strength in the neck and shoulder girdle. A CT scan demonstrated a large, heterogeneous mass lesion in the cervical spine, measuring 6 cm in diameter, with adjacent bony destruction and soft tissue involvement.

A MRI scan showed a mass occupying the spinal canal, compressing the cord and causing significant neurologic compromise. The patient's symptoms were attributed to spinal cord compression, necessitating urgent decompression surgery.

Intraoperatively, the tumor was found to be infiltrating the vertebrae and surrounding soft tissues. Histopathological examination revealed malignant rhabdomyosarcoma (MRMS), a rare and aggressive sarcoma of striated muscle origin.

The patient underwent an en bloc resection of the affected vertebral body and surrounding tissue, followed by reconstruction with a titanium cage and posterior instrumentation. Postoperatively, he received adjuvant chemotherapy consisting of cyclophosphamide, doxorubicin, and vincristine for six cycles, as well as radiation therapy to the tumor bed.

At 18 months follow-up, the patient experienced significant improvement in his neurologic function and pain, with no evidence of local recurrence or distant metastases. MRMS is a rare entity, accounting for less than 1% of all adult soft tissue sarcomas. Our case highlights the importance of maintaining a high index of suspicion for this diagnosis, particularly in patients presenting with unusual symptoms and signs.

Key words: Malignant rhabdomyosarcoma, cervical spine, neck pain, spinal cord compression, surgical resection, adjuvant therapy.
