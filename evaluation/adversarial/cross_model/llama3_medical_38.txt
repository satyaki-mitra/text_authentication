**Title:** Rare Case of Mucormycosis Presenting as Orbital Cellulitis in an Immunocompetent Individual: A Diagnostic Challenge

**Abstract:**

Mucormycosis is a rare and often fatal fungal infection caused by members of the Mucorales family. While typically affecting immunocompromised individuals, we present a unique case of mucormycosis presenting as orbital cellulitis in an otherwise healthy adult.

A 35-year-old male with no underlying medical conditions or recent travel history presented to our emergency department with a 3-day history of severe right-sided facial pain, swelling, and decreased visual acuity. Physical examination revealed a tense, tender eyelid and conjunctival injection with minimal discharge. Computed Tomography (CT) scans of the orbits showed an abnormal soft tissue density surrounding the optic canal.

Initial laboratory results were unremarkable except for mildly elevated white blood cell count. Despite antibiotic therapy for presumed orbital cellulitis, the patient's symptoms persisted, and the lesion progressed.

Further evaluation revealed a fungal hyphae on histopathological examination of the biopsy specimen, prompting the diagnosis of mucormycosis. The isolate was identified as Rhizopus microsporus, a common species responsible for human disease.

Treatment consisted of intravenous amphotericin B (5 mg/kg/day) and surgical debridement of the affected tissue. The patient's condition improved gradually, with resolution of symptoms and imaging findings over 6 weeks.

Mucormycosis is an uncommon but potentially life-threatening infection that can masquerade as common conditions like orbital cellulitis. Prompt recognition and management are crucial to prevent dissemination and improve outcomes. This case highlights the importance of considering rare etiologies in the differential diagnosis of orbital infections, even in immunocompetent individuals.

**Keywords:** Mucormycosis, Orbital Cellulitis, Fungal Infections, Rhizopus microsporus.
