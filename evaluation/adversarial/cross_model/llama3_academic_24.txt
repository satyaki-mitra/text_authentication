Title: Recent Advances in Epigenetic Regulation of Gene Expression during Cellular Senescence

Summary:

Cellular senescence, a state of permanent cell cycle arrest, plays a crucial role in preventing tumor progression and maintaining tissue homeostasis. Recent studies have highlighted the significance of epigenetic mechanisms in regulating gene expression during senescence. Our research group has made significant strides in elucidating the epigenetic landscape underlying cellular senescence.

Recent findings suggest that histone modifications, particularly trimethylation of histone 3 lysine 27 (H3K27me3), are essential for silencing pro-proliferative genes during senescence. In a study published in Nature Communications, we demonstrated that H3K27me3-enriched chromatin regions are characterized by the presence of polycomb repressive complex 2 (PRC2) and its associated proteins. Notably, PRC2-mediated H3K27me3 trimethylation was found to be dependent on the senescence-associated gene silencing (SAGS) pathway.

Furthermore, our data revealed that DNA methylation also plays a critical role in regulating gene expression during senescence. We identified hypermethylated regions of the genome, which were enriched for genes involved in cell cycle progression and DNA repair. In contrast, hypomethylated regions were found to be associated with pro-inflammatory and stress-response genes.

These findings have significant implications for our understanding of cellular senescence and its dysregulation in age-related diseases. The identification of epigenetic marks as key regulators of gene expression during senescence may lead to the development of novel therapeutic strategies aimed at modulating these mechanisms to promote healthy aging.
