**Title:** Unveiling the Mechanisms of Mitochondrial Dynamics in Neurodegenerative Diseases: Recent Insights from High-Resolution Imaging Studies.

**Summary:** Mitochondria, the powerhouses of eukaryotic cells, play a crucial role in maintaining cellular homeostasis and energy metabolism. In neurodegenerative diseases, such as Alzheimer's, Parkinson's, and Huntington's, mitochondrial dysfunction has been implicated as a key contributor to disease pathology. Recent advances in high-resolution imaging techniques have provided novel insights into the mechanisms underlying mitochondrial dynamics in these conditions.

Using super-resolution microscopy and advanced image analysis tools, researchers have elucidated the complex interplay between mitochondrial morphology, motility, and function in neurodegenerative disease models. Notably, studies have revealed that mitochondrial fragmentation and depolarization are early events in disease progression, preceding overt neuronal degeneration (Kang et al., 2022). This observation suggests that targeting mitochondrial dynamics may offer a therapeutic window for preventing or slowing disease progression.

Further investigations have identified specific molecular players involved in regulating mitochondrial dynamics, including the mitofusins (Mfn1/2) and optic atrophy proteins (OPA1/3) (Wang et al., 2020). These findings provide mechanistic insights into how neurodegenerative diseases may perturb mitochondrial homeostasis, ultimately contributing to neuronal dysfunction and cell death.

The application of high-resolution imaging approaches has also enabled the visualization of mitochondrial network reorganization in response to disease-relevant stimuli. For instance, studies have shown that certain neurotrophic factors can induce mitochondrial network expansion and increased biogenesis (Lee et al., 2019). These findings offer potential avenues for therapeutic intervention by targeting mitochondrial dynamics and promoting healthy cellular function.

In conclusion, recent advances in high-resolution imaging have significantly expanded our understanding of the mechanisms underlying mitochondrial dynamics in neurodegenerative diseases. Further research is needed to fully elucidate the complex interactions between mitochondrial morphology, motility, and function, as well as to identify novel therapeutic targets for mitigating disease progression.
