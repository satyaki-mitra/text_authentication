Title: Rare Case of Adult-Onset Hypertrichosis due to Pituitary Tumor-Induced Hyperestrogenism

Abstract:

We report a rare case of adult-onset hypertrichosis caused by pituitary tumor-induced hyperestrogenism in a 35-year-old female. The patient presented with excessive hair growth on her face, arms, and legs over the course of several months, accompanied by symptoms of galactorrhea (spontaneous milk secretion) and amenorrhea.

Physical examination revealed a thickened scalp with copious amounts of curly hair, as well as increased hair density on the face, arms, and legs. Laboratory tests showed elevated serum estrogen levels (1500 ng/mL; normal range: 10-50 ng/mL) and prolactin levels (100 μg/L; normal range: 5-20 μg/L).

Imaging studies revealed a pituitary macroadenoma compressing the posterior lobe, consistent with hyperestrogenism due to excessive estrogen production. The patient underwent transsphenoidal resection of the tumor, which was confirmed to be a prolactin-secreting adenoma.

Following surgery, serum estrogen and prolactin levels normalized, and the patient's symptoms improved significantly. Within 6 months, hair growth slowed down, and the patient's appearance returned to normal.

This case highlights the importance of considering rare endocrine disorders in patients with unexplained hypertrichosis. Adult-onset hypertrichosis is a rare condition that can be caused by various etiologies, including hormonal imbalances. In this instance, the pituitary tumor-induced hyperestrogenism led to excessive estrogen production, resulting in the characteristic symptom of hypertrichosis.

Our case also underscores the need for a multidisciplinary approach in managing patients with pituitary tumors. A thorough understanding of the patient's symptoms, physical examination findings, and laboratory results is essential for accurate diagnosis and treatment planning. In this patient, the combined expertise of endocrinology, neurosurgery, and dermatology was crucial in achieving a successful outcome.
