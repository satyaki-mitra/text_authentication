**Title:** Unusual Presentation of Neurofibromatosis Type 1: A Case Report

**Abstract:**

Neurofibromatosis type 1 (NF1) is a rare genetic disorder characterized by the growth of benign tumors along nerves, called neurofibromas. While the classical presentation of NF1 includes multiple cafe-au-lait macules and axillary freckling, we report an unusual case of NF1 presenting with a solitary plexiform neurofibroma in the absence of any characteristic cutaneous lesions.

A 35-year-old woman was referred to our institution for evaluation of a slow-growing, painless mass in her right thigh. The lesion had been present for approximately 5 years and had gradually increased in size over time. Physical examination revealed a firm, mobile mass measuring 10 cm in diameter, located just above the knee joint.

Radiological imaging studies, including computed tomography (CT) scan and magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), showed a large, well-circumscribed tumor originating from the right femoral nerve sheath. The lesion was heterogeneously enhanced with contrast agents on both CT and MRI scans.

Histopathological examination of a biopsy specimen revealed a plexiform neurofibroma, characterized by a mixture of Schwann cells, fibroblasts, and perineurial cells within a collagenous stroma. Immunohistochemical staining confirmed the presence of S100 protein, a marker commonly expressed in neurofibromas.

In addition to the solitary plexiform neurofibroma, the patient's medical history was significant for hypertension, which responded well to antihypertensive therapy after diagnosis and treatment of her NF1.

This case highlights the importance of considering NF1 in the differential diagnosis of a solitary soft tissue mass, particularly when radiological imaging studies suggest a nerve sheath origin. The absence of characteristic cutaneous lesions does not rule out the diagnosis, as seen in this unusual presentation. A high index of suspicion and thorough evaluation are essential for accurate diagnosis and management of this rare condition.

**Keywords:** Neurofibromatosis type 1, plexiform neurofibroma, solitary soft tissue mass, nerve sheath origin, hypertension.
