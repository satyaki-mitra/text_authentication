**Case Report: Rare Complication of Cervical Spine Fracture**

Clinical Abstract:

A 35-year-old male presented to the emergency department with severe neck pain and numbness in both arms following a motor vehicle accident. Physical examination revealed limited cervical spine range of motion, bilateral brachial plexus palsy, and a palpable mass at the C5-C6 junction.

Imaging studies, including computed tomography (CT) scans and magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), confirmed a complex fracture-dislocation of the cervical spine, involving the C5-C6 vertebral bodies and posterior elements. The patient's neurological examination was remarkable for a flaccid paralysis of both arms, consistent with a bilateral brachial plexus lesion.

Surgical intervention was initiated to stabilize the fractured cervical spine and relieve the compression on the brachial plexus. A C5-C6 anterior decompression and fusion procedure was performed, utilizing an autologous bone graft and anterior plating.

Intraoperatively, a large hematoma was identified, compressing the C5 nerve root and contributing to the patient's flaccid paralysis. The hematoma was evacuated, and the surrounding soft tissues were thoroughly debrided. Post-operatively, the patient received aggressive physical therapy and occupational therapy to address his neurological deficits.

At six months following surgery, the patient demonstrated significant improvement in his cervical spine range of motion, with a corresponding reduction in neck pain. His bilateral brachial plexus palsy had resolved, as evidenced by normal motor function and sensation in both arms.

This case highlights the importance of prompt recognition and management of complex cervical spine fractures, particularly those involving neural compression. The rare complication of a large hematoma compressing the C5 nerve root emphasizes the need for thorough intraoperative exploration and debridement to optimize patient outcomes.

**Clinical Significance:**

* Complex cervical spine fractures can lead to significant neurological deficits, requiring prompt recognition and intervention.
* Hematoma evacuation and soft tissue debridement may be necessary to alleviate compression on neural structures.
* Aggressive physical therapy and occupational therapy are crucial for optimal patient recovery.
