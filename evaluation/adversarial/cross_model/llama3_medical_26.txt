**Title:** Rare Case of Bilateral Retinopathy and Nephropathy in a Patient with Undiagnosed Celiac Disease: A Clinical Abstract

**Abstract:**

We present a rare and intriguing case of a 35-year-old woman who presented to our hospital with complaints of vision loss, proteinuria, and hematuria. The patient had no significant medical history or family history of kidney disease. Upon physical examination, she was found to have bilateral retinal edema and macular thickening, consistent with a diagnosis of bilateral retinopathy.

Laboratory studies revealed elevated blood pressure (140/90 mmHg), proteinuria (3+ on dipstick), and hematuria (100 red blood cells per high-power field). Renal ultrasound showed mild to moderate left renal cortical thinning. The patient was admitted for further evaluation, including a comprehensive metabolic panel, which revealed elevated serum levels of transaminases (AST 120 IU/L, ALT 90 IU/L) and lactate dehydrogenase (LDH 240 IU/L).

Histopathological examination of the kidney biopsy specimens showed features consistent with membranous nephropathy, characterized by dense subendothelial deposits and IgG/IgM immunoglobulin deposition. The patient's retinal pathology was also evaluated, revealing bilateral retinal vasculitis with perivascular inflammation and microvascular lesions.

After thorough evaluation, a diagnosis of undiagnosed celiac disease was made based on elevated serum levels of antigliadin antibodies (AGA) and tissue transglutaminase antibodies (tTG). The patient was started on a gluten-free diet, which led to significant improvement in her renal function, as evidenced by resolution of proteinuria and hematuria.

This case highlights the importance of considering celiac disease in patients with unexplained bilateral retinopathy and nephropathy. The diagnosis was made possible through a thorough clinical evaluation and laboratory testing, including serological markers for celiac disease. Treatment with a gluten-free diet led to significant improvement in renal function, underscoring the need for vigilance in diagnosing and managing this common yet often overlooked condition.

**Keywords:** bilateral retinopathy, membranous nephropathy, celiac disease, gluten-free diet.
