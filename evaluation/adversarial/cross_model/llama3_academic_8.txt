**Title:** Novel Insights into the Mechanisms of Neuroplasticity: A Review of Recent Findings on Synaptic Pruning and Synaptogenesis

**Abstract:**

Neuroplasticity, the brain's ability to reorganize itself in response to experience or environment, is a crucial mechanism underlying learning and memory. Recent advances in our understanding of synaptic pruning and synaptogenesis have shed new light on the complex processes governing neuroplasticity. This review summarizes the latest findings on these two critical aspects of neural development and function.

**Pruning:**

Synaptic pruning, the elimination of weak or redundant connections between neurons, is a highly conserved process across species. Recent studies have revealed that microRNAs (miRNAs) play a key role in regulating this process. Specifically, miR-132 has been shown to target BDNF and its receptor TrkB, thereby modulating synaptic strength and pruning efficiency (Luo et al., 2020). Furthermore, research has identified the importance of astrocytes in facilitating pruning through the release of glutamate and GABA (Lee et al., 2019).

**Synaptogenesis:**

Conversely, synaptogenesis, the formation of new connections between neurons, is essential for learning and memory consolidation. Recent studies have highlighted the role of Wnt/β-catenin signaling in promoting synaptogenesis (Kim et al., 2020). Additionally, research has shown that experience-dependent activity can induce long-term potentiation (LTP) through the activation of NMDA receptors and subsequent synaptic strengthening (Turrigiano et al., 2019).

**Conclusion:**

In conclusion, recent advances in our understanding of synaptic pruning and synaptogenesis have significantly enhanced our comprehension of neuroplasticity. The identification of miRNAs as key regulators of pruning and the importance of astrocytes in facilitating this process highlight the complex interplay between glial cells and neurons. Furthermore, the discovery of Wnt/β-catenin signaling's role in promoting synaptogenesis underscores the critical importance of experience-dependent activity in shaping neural connections.
