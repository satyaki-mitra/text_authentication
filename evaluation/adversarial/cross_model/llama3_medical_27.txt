**Title:** Rare Case of Eosinophilic Fasciitis Presenting as a Soft Tissue Mass in the Upper Extremity

**Abstract:**

We report an unusual case of eosinophilic fasciitis, a rare autoimmune disorder characterized by inflammation and fibrosis of the deep fascia, presenting as a soft tissue mass in the upper extremity. A 42-year-old woman presented to our emergency department with a two-month history of painless swelling in her right forearm. Physical examination revealed a firm, non-tender mass measuring approximately 10 cm in diameter.

Radiographic imaging studies, including computed tomography (CT) and magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), demonstrated a well-circumscribed, homogeneous mass involving the deep fascia of the forearm, without evidence of bony involvement or distant metastasis. Laboratory investigations showed elevated eosinophil counts (1,200/μL; normal range: 0-400/μL), with normal complete blood count and basic metabolic panel results.

Clinical examination and imaging findings led to a diagnosis of eosinophilic fasciitis, confirmed by histopathological analysis of an excisional biopsy specimen. Histological sections revealed dense inflammatory infiltrates composed of eosinophils and mononuclear cells, accompanied by collagenous tissue and fibrosis.

Treatment with systemic corticosteroids (prednisone 60 mg/day) was initiated, resulting in significant clinical improvement within two weeks, including resolution of pain and swelling. The patient remained on steroids for six months to prevent relapse. At the six-month follow-up, physical examination revealed minimal residual swelling, and laboratory tests showed a return to normal eosinophil count.

Eosinophilic fasciitis is an uncommon condition characterized by inflammatory infiltrates rich in eosinophils and subsequent fibrosis of deep fascia. The rarity of this disorder, combined with the lack of specific diagnostic criteria, can lead to delayed diagnosis and treatment. In our case, the patient's symptoms and imaging findings were initially attributed to other conditions, such as cellulitis or ganglion cyst. Prompt recognition of eosinophilic fasciitis is crucial for prompt initiation of therapy, potentially preventing long-term sequelae.

**Keywords:** Eosinophilic fasciitis, soft tissue mass, upper extremity, autoimmune disorder, corticosteroid treatment.
