**Case Report: Unusual Presentation of Primary Pulmonary Hypertension**

Abstract:

Primary pulmonary hypertension (PPH) is a rare and debilitating condition characterized by increased pressure in the pulmonary arteries. We present an unusual case of PPH in a 35-year-old female who presented with symptoms distinct from those typically associated with this disease.

**Case Report:**

A 35-year-old woman was referred to our institution for evaluation of unexplained dyspnea, fatigue, and chest pain. She had no significant medical history and was not taking any medications. Physical examination revealed tachypnea, bilateral wheezing, and jugular venous distension. Chest radiography demonstrated clear lungs, while electrocardiogram (ECG) showed nonspecific ST-T wave changes.

Echocardiography revealed moderate tricuspid regurgitation, right ventricular hypertrophy, and an estimated pulmonary artery pressure of 80 mmHg. Cardiac catheterization confirmed the diagnosis of PPH, with a mean pulmonary artery pressure of 90 mmHg and a pulmonary vascular resistance index of 9.5 Wood units.

**Clinical Course:**

The patient was treated with anticoagulation therapy and diuretics to manage symptoms and prevent complications. She was referred for lung transplantation evaluation due to her rapidly declining functional status. The patient's clinical course was marked by frequent hospitalizations for worsening dyspnea, syncope, and right heart failure.

**Discussion:**

PPH is a rare condition that typically presents with exertional dyspnea, chest pain, and fatigue. Our case highlights the importance of considering PPH in patients presenting with atypical symptoms, such as our patient's unusual combination of dyspnea, fatigue, and chest pain. The presence of tricuspid regurgitation and right ventricular hypertrophy on echocardiography is a characteristic feature of PPH.

**Conclusion:**

This case underscores the need for a high index of suspicion when evaluating patients with unexplained symptoms that may be related to PPH. Prompt recognition and treatment can improve outcomes in this devastating condition.

**Keywords:** primary pulmonary hypertension, right heart failure, tricuspid regurgitation.
