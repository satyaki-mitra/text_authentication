**Clinical Abstract**

**Title:** Rare Case of Neurofibromatosis Type 1 with Multiple Cutaneous and Skeletal Manifestations: A Diagnostic Challenge

**Abstract:**

We present the case of a 30-year-old female patient who presented to our institution with a complex set of cutaneous and skeletal abnormalities, ultimately diagnosed as neurofibromatosis type 1 (NF1). The patient initially sought medical attention for a suspicious rash on her left arm, which had been evolving over several months. Physical examination revealed multiple café-au-lait macules, axillary freckling, and soft tissue masses on the extremities.

Radiographic imaging studies, including whole-body MRI and CT scans, disclosed numerous osseous lesions, including lytic defects in the tibia, fibula, and femur, as well as a large expansile lesion involving the distal radius. Skeletal abnormalities were consistent with a diagnosis of NF1, which is characterized by the presence of multiple neurofibromas, café-au-lait macules, and other systemic manifestations.

The patient's clinical presentation also included signs of peripheral neuropathy, including numbness and tingling in her hands and feet. Electrodiagnostic studies confirmed the presence of polyneuropathy, which was attributed to the involvement of nerve fibers by neurofibromas.

In addition to the physical examination findings, laboratory tests revealed mild anemia and elevated erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR), consistent with chronic inflammation. Complete blood count (CBC) and electrolyte panel were within normal limits.

The patient was managed conservatively with regular follow-up appointments to monitor her disease progression and potential complications. She received guidance on wound care and compression garments for management of her skin lesions.

NF1 is a rare genetic disorder characterized by the development of benign tumors on nerve tissue, which can lead to significant morbidity and mortality if left untreated or inadequately managed. Early diagnosis and multidisciplinary care are essential for optimizing patient outcomes and preventing long-term complications.

This case highlights the importance of considering NF1 in patients presenting with unusual cutaneous and skeletal manifestations, even in the absence of a family history of the disorder. A high index of suspicion and thorough clinical evaluation can facilitate early detection and intervention, ultimately improving patient outcomes and quality of life.
