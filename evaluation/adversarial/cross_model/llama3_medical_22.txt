**Case Report:**

**Title:** A Rare Case of Cervical Spine Fracture Complicating Acute Osteoporotic Vertebral Compression Fractures in a Patient with Metastatic Breast Cancer

**Abstract:**

We present an unusual case of cervical spine fracture complicating acute osteoporotic vertebral compression fractures (OVCFs) in a patient with metastatic breast cancer. A 62-year-old woman was admitted to our hospital with severe neck pain and limited range of motion following a fall at home. Radiographs revealed multiple OVCFs at T6, T8, and L1 levels. Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) of the cervical spine showed an unexpected fracture of the C5 vertebral body, consistent with a Chance fracture.

The patient had a prior history of breast cancer diagnosed 2 years earlier, which had metastasized to her bones, as evidenced by recent bone scans. Despite this, she was not receiving any targeted therapy at the time of admission.

On physical examination, the patient exhibited severe pain and limited range of motion in her neck, as well as weakness in her shoulder girdle muscles. Laboratory tests revealed elevated serum levels of calcium (14.2 mg/dL) and alkaline phosphatase (243 U/L), consistent with underlying hypercalcemia and bone turnover.

The patient underwent emergent stabilization of the cervical spine fracture using a halo vest, followed by surgical decompression and instrumentation at C5-C6 to prevent further neurological compromise.

Intraoperatively, multiple OVCFs were identified and stabilized using kyphoplasty procedures. Postoperatively, the patient received bisphosphonate therapy for management of hypercalcemia and to prevent further bone loss.

The patient's postoperative course was marked by a gradual improvement in her neck pain and range of motion. She was discharged to an acute care rehabilitation facility after 2 weeks of hospitalization.

**Conclusion:** This rare case highlights the importance of considering underlying metastatic disease when evaluating patients with acute spinal fractures, particularly in those with known bone metastases. The prompt recognition and management of such cases can significantly impact patient outcomes and quality of life.

**Keywords:** Cervical spine fracture; Osteoporotic vertebral compression fractures (OVCFs); Metastatic breast cancer; Hypercalcemia.
