Title: Rare Case of Primary Cutaneous Mucinous Carcinoma Presenting as an Intraoral Mass

Abstract:

Primary cutaneous mucinous carcinoma is a rare and aggressive skin cancer characterized by the production of excessive amounts of mucin, leading to tumor growth and metastasis. We report a unique case of primary cutaneous mucinous carcinoma presenting as an intraoral mass.

A 65-year-old male patient presented with a slowly growing, painless mass on the left side of his tongue, which had been present for approximately six months. The mass was asymptomatic until it began to bleed upon eating. On physical examination, a solitary, ulcerated lesion measuring approximately 2 cm in diameter was noted. No other oral or skin lesions were observed.

Laboratory tests, including complete blood count and serum chemistry profile, were unremarkable. A biopsy of the tumor was performed, which revealed a poorly differentiated squamous cell carcinoma with mucinous differentiation. Immunohistochemical staining showed positivity for MUC1, CA19-9, and CK5/6, consistent with primary cutaneous mucinous carcinoma.

The patient underwent surgical excision of the mass, followed by adjuvant radiation therapy to the tongue and surrounding tissue. Histopathological examination of the resected specimen confirmed a primary cutaneous mucinous carcinoma, measuring 3 cm in diameter, with a depth of invasion of 1.5 cm.

Postoperatively, the patient experienced mild xerostomia, which resolved within six weeks. No evidence of recurrent or metastatic disease was noted at one-year follow-up.

Primary cutaneous mucinous carcinoma is an extremely rare skin cancer that often presents as a solitary lesion on the head and neck. Its rarity and varied presentations can lead to delayed diagnosis and treatment. Our case highlights the importance of considering this condition in the differential diagnosis of intraoral masses, particularly those with mucinous differentiation.
