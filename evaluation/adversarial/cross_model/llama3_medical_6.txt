**Case Report:**

**Title:** "A Rare Case of Cerebral Amyloid Angiopathy Presenting as Repeated Episodes of Transient Ischemic Attacks"

**Abstract:**

We present the case of a 62-year-old male patient who presented to our emergency department with multiple episodes of transient ischemic attacks (TIAs) over a period of several weeks. The patient initially complained of left-sided weakness, followed by episodes of right facial drooping and difficulty speaking. A thorough neurological examination revealed no significant findings, while imaging studies including computed tomography (CT) scans and magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) showed subtle changes consistent with small vessel disease.

Further evaluation revealed a diagnosis of cerebral amyloid angiopathy (CAA), a rare condition characterized by the deposition of beta-amyloid peptide in the walls of cerebral blood vessels. The patient's symptoms were attributed to recurrent ischemic events caused by CAA-related small vessel disease.

The patient was treated with antiplatelet therapy and received regular follow-up care. Over the course of several months, his symptoms gradually resolved, and he experienced no further episodes of TIA.

**Discussion:**

CAA is a rare but increasingly recognized cause of cerebral small vessel disease. It typically presents as recurrent TIAs or strokes, often with characteristic features such as lobar hemorrhages or infarcts. The diagnosis is often challenging due to the lack of specific imaging findings, and CAA may be misdiagnosed as other conditions.

In this case, the patient's symptoms were initially attributed to other causes, highlighting the importance of considering CAA in the differential diagnosis of TIA. Our case demonstrates that a high index of suspicion and careful evaluation can lead to the accurate diagnosis of CAA, even in cases with subtle imaging findings.

**Conclusion:**

CAA is a rare but important consideration in patients presenting with recurrent TIAs or strokes. A thorough evaluation and consideration of this condition are essential for ensuring timely and effective treatment. Further research into the epidemiology, pathophysiology, and management of CAA is needed to improve our understanding of this complex condition.

**Keywords:** cerebral amyloid angiopathy, transient ischemic attacks, small vessel disease
