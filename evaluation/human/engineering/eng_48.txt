gravitating bodies significantly alter the flow pattern ( density and velocity ) of the gas that attempts to stream past . still , small protoplanets in the mars super - earth range can only bind limited amounts of nebular gas ; until the so - called critical core mass has been reached ( @xmath0110 earth masses ) this gas is in near hydrostatic equilibrium with the nebula . here we aim for a general description of the flow pattern surrounding these low - mass , embedded planets . using various simplifying assumptions ( subsonic , 2d , inviscid flow , etc ) , we reduce the problem to a partial differential equation that we solve numerically as well as approximate analytically . it is found that the boundary between the atmosphere and the nebula gas strongly depends on the value of the disc headwind ( deviation from keplerian rotation ) . with increasing headwind the atmosphere decreases in size and also becomes more asymmetrical . using the derived flow pattern for the gas , trajectories of small solid particles , which experience both gas drag and gravitational forces , are integrated numerically . accretion rates for small particles ( dust ) are found to be low , as they closely follow the streamlines , which curl away from the planet . however , pebble - size particles achieve large accretion rates , in agreement with previous numerical and analytical works .
